## The case report (30 minutes)

Write a one-page forensic report on the missing cake:

- what samples you had,
- the method, in five steps,
- the gel photo, labelled,
- your conclusion: which suspect matches, and how sure you are,
- one weakness: why a real court would need more than this.

## Debate: DNA testing in real life (35 minutes)

DNA testing is used in Ethiopia and around the world for:

- solving crimes and freeing people wrongly convicted,
- identifying victims of disasters,
- testing whether people are related (for example, in inheritance or citizenship cases),
- testing for genetic diseases before marriage or birth,
- ancestry tests sold to the public.

In teams, choose one use. Prepare **one benefit** and **one risk** (privacy, mistakes, misuse, discrimination, how results make people feel). Then hold a short class debate: should DNA databases include everyone?

## Back to the mystery (15 minutes)

Open *The mystery* from step 1. Next to each guess, write ✓ or ✗ and what you now know. Then answer properly: **how can something invisible become something you can pick up?**

## Explain it to the guide (25 minutes)

Your guide will ask each of you to:

- explain what each step of the extraction does, and what your fair test showed,
- write the matching strand for a short sequence,
- predict a cross with a Punnett square, and explain why real counts differ from predictions,
- explain your gel result and why the bands separated,
- give a benefit and a risk of DNA testing, with reasons.
