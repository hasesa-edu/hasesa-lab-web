## The case

A cake has disappeared from the staff room. At the scene, the guide found a coloured stain: the **crime-scene sample**. Four suspects each had a drink of a different colour. The guide has made a sample from each: **suspects 1–4**. Which suspect's drink matches the stain?

Real forensic scientists separate pieces of DNA by size and charge in a gel. We'll use the same method on food colourings, which are made of charged molecules too, so you can see the bands without special stains.

## Theory

Re-read **Reading DNA: fingerprinting** in *DNA and genes*. Then read **Chromatography**: electrophoresis is a cousin of chromatography. Both separate a mixture because different substances travel through a material at different speeds.

## Build the rig (with the guide, 50 minutes)

1. **Buffer:** the guide dissolves baking soda in distilled water (the guide's recipe). This salty liquid carries the current.
2. **Gel:** the guide dissolves agar in hot buffer and pours it into the plastic box, about 1 cm deep. Push the comb (or card strip) in near one end, to make **wells**. Let it set for 20–30 minutes, then pull the comb out gently.
3. **Electrodes:** a piece of stainless-steel wire (or pencil lead) at each end of the box, across its width, standing in the buffer.
4. Pour buffer over the gel until it's just covered.

## Load and run (60 minutes)

1. Mix each sample with a little sugar or glycerine so it sinks.
2. With a dropper, carefully fill one well per sample: crime scene, then suspects 1–4. Don't pierce the bottom of the well.
3. **Guide only:** connects four 9 V batteries in series, **negative** to the electrode nearest the wells and **positive** to the far end. Nobody touches the box while it runs.
4. Watch bubbles form at the electrodes (that's how you know current is flowing). Check every 10 minutes. After 30–60 minutes, the guide disconnects the batteries.

Photograph the gel on white paper.

## Notice something puzzling

Some colours split into two or more bands. Some move faster than others. Some may not move at all. What does that tell you about what's in each colouring? Which way did the coloured bands move, and what does that tell you about their electric charge?

## Record (40 minutes)

A labelled drawing or photo of the gel. A table: sample, number of bands, distance moved by each band (in mm). Which suspect matches the crime scene? How confident are you, and why?
