## Do: dissect a seed (20 minutes)

Your guide soaked chickpeas and beans in water overnight. Take one of each, and a dry one to compare.

1. Compare a dry seed and a soaked one. Measure both. How much did it swell?
2. Peel the seed coat off the soaked seed with your fingernail.
3. Gently split it into its two halves. Look with a magnifying glass for the tiny **embryo** tucked at one end: a little root and the folded first leaves.
4. Draw it, labelled: seed coat, cotyledon, root, shoot.

Then cut a soaked maize or wheat grain lengthways. How is it different?

## Do: test the food store (15 minutes)

Gloves and glasses on. Put one cotyledon on a white tile. Add a drop of dilute iodine. What colour? What does that tell you is inside?

## Notice something puzzling

The seed is packed with starch, and the shoot is folded up with no green at all. The seed is in the dark under the soil, with no light to make food. So how does it grow its first few centimetres?

## Theory: cells

Read **Cells**. You'll learn what's inside a plant cell, how plant and animal cells are different, and how to work out the real size of something you see under a microscope.

## Do: see the cells (25 minutes)

1. Peel the thin, see-through skin from the inside of an onion layer.
2. Lay it flat on a slide or clear plastic, add a drop of iodine, and look with the microscope or phone lens.
3. Then try a very thin slice of potato with iodine. The dark specks inside the cells are **starch grains**.
4. Count how many onion cells fit across your field of view, and estimate the size of one cell.

## Record

Your labelled seed drawing, the iodine result, a labelled drawing of a few onion cells, and your cell-size estimate with the working. Answer the puzzle: where does the young shoot get its energy from?
