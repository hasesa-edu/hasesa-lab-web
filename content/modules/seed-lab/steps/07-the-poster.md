## Make your poster (40 minutes)

Scientists share their results on posters at conferences. Make one, on a large sheet, with these sections:

1. **Question**: one sentence.
2. **Prediction**: what you expected, and why.
3. **Method**: a labelled drawing of your setup, what you changed, what you measured, and what you kept the same.
4. **Results**: your graph (big and clear) and a small table of means.
5. **Conclusion**: the answer to your question, using numbers from your data. How sure are you?
6. **Weakness and next step**: one thing that might have affected your results, and what you'd test next.

Add photos or drawings of the plants at the end.

## The poster session (20 minutes)

Put the posters up around the room. Half the class stands by their poster to explain it; the other half visits and asks questions. Then swap.

When you visit, ask each team at least one question, like: "How do you know it wasn't something else?" or "What was your control?"

## Back to the mystery (15 minutes)

Open *The mystery* from step 1. Next to each prediction write ✓ or ✗ and what you now know. Then answer: **what does a seed really need to grow, and how did you prove it?**

Your guide will talk with each of you about your plan, your graph and your conclusion.
