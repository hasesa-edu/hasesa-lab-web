## Choose a question (15 minutes)

As a team, choose **one** question to test. It must be about **one** factor that might affect germination or growth. Some ideas:

| Question | Independent variable |
|---|---|
| Does light affect how seedlings grow? | light / dark (box) / partial shade |
| How much water is best? | 0, 10, 20, 40 mL every two days |
| Does salt in the water stop seeds growing? | 0, 1, 2, 5 g of salt per litre |
| Does crowding matter? | 1, 3, 6, 10 seeds per pot |
| Does fertiliser help? | 0, ½ dose, full dose, double dose |
| Do seeds need soil to germinate? | soil, sand, wet paper towel |
| Does temperature matter? | warm windowsill / room / cool corner |

Your question must be one you can answer in **two weeks**.

## Theory: fair tests

Read **Fair tests and averages**. You'll learn about independent, dependent and controlled variables, controls, why we repeat, and how to work out a mean and a range.

## Plan (45 minutes)

Write your plan using the five lines from the unit. Then add:

- **What exactly will you measure?** Germination (yes/no each day), height in cm, number of leaves, colour? Choose one main measurement and how you'll take it (for example: "height from soil to the tip of the longest leaf, with a ruler held straight, to the nearest 0.5 cm").
- **How many seeds per group?** At least five. Why not just one?
- **Your control group.** Which pots are "normal"?
- **Labels.** How will you know which pot is which? Plan a code.
- **A results table**, ready to fill in: one row per seed or pot, one column per measuring day.

## Check (20 minutes)

Swap plans with another team. Check their plan for:

- only **one** thing changing between groups,
- at least four controlled variables (seed type, soil, pot size, depth of planting, watering, position…),
- a control group,
- enough seeds.

Give them one improvement. Then fix your own plan using their feedback.

## Record

Your final plan, neatly written, and your blank results table. Show it to the guide: you can't plant until it's approved.
