## First: check your experiment (10 minutes)

Water and measure your pots, as usual. Have any seeds germinated? Is there any difference between the groups yet?

## The puzzle

The seed lives on its stored starch at first. But a big plant has used up its seed long ago. So where does a growing plant get its food?

## Do: the covered leaf (50 minutes)

Two days ago, your guide put the potted plant in a dark cupboard, then brought it into bright light with a strip of foil folded over part of one leaf (top and bottom). Now:

1. Draw the leaf, showing where the foil was. Remove the foil.
2. **Guide:** dips the leaf in boiling water for 1 minute. This kills it and softens it.
3. **Guide:** puts the leaf in a small tube of ethanol, standing in a bowl of hot water from the kettle (**no flame anywhere**). After 5–10 minutes the green chlorophyll comes out into the ethanol and the leaf goes pale.
4. Rinse the leaf in warm water. It's now soft and brittle; handle it gently.
5. Spread it on a white tile and cover it with dilute iodine. Wait 2 minutes.

Which parts turn **blue-black**? Which stay **brown**? Compare with your drawing.

## Why the steps?

Answer in your notebook:

- Why was the plant kept in the dark for two days first?
- Why do we remove the green colour before adding iodine?
- What was the **control** in this experiment?

## Theory

Re-read the **photosynthesis** part of *Photosynthesis and plant growth*, and the **starch** test in *Chemical tests*.

## Do it better: seedlings in the dark (15 minutes)

If any team is testing light, look at their seedlings in the dark box. If not, your guide has a pot of seedlings grown in the dark. Compare them with seedlings grown in light: colour, height, leaf size, stem thickness. A seedling in the dark has no light to photosynthesise, so why does it grow **taller**?

## Record

Your leaf drawing before and after, with the result. One sentence: what does the covered-leaf test prove? Your experiment's latest measurements.
