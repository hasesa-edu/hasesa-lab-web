# Seed Lab: guide's notes

For the guide only. About 10 hours of sessions, spread over **about three weeks**, because plants need time. Up to 16 students in teams of three or four, each team running its own experiment. The students do the science; your job is to make sure their experiments are fair and that they record honestly.

Suggested calendar:

| Week | Session | Steps |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Session 1 | 1 and 2 |
| 1 | Session 2 | 3 and 4 (plant) |
| 2 | Session 3 | 5 (and check experiments) |
| 3 | Session 4 | 6 |
| 3 | Session 5 | 7 |

Between sessions, teams need about 5 minutes every two days to water and measure. Arrange access to the room (a sunny windowsill or bench where pots won't be disturbed).

---

## Before the module

### 1. The demo pots for step 1 (plant two weeks ahead)

Plant 5 chickpeas in each of five pots, same soil, same depth, and treat them like this (keep the key secret):

| Pot | Treatment | What students will see |
|---|---|---|
| A | Normal: window light, 20 mL water every two days | Healthy green plants |
| B | In a dark box | Tall, thin, pale yellow plants: they grew towards light that wasn't there |
| C | No water after planting | Nothing came up |
| D | Watered with salt water (10 g/L) | Few or no seedlings, or stunted ones |
| E | 15 seeds crowded in the pot | Many thin, competing plants |

### 2. The leaf test plant (start four days before step 5)

Use a healthy potted plant with broad, thin leaves (a bean plant grown from seed works well; so do geraniums). **Two days before**, put it in a dark cupboard (this uses up the starch already in its leaves). **The day before**, fold a strip of foil over the middle of two or three leaves, top and bottom, held with paper clips, and put the plant in bright light all day.

Practise the test once yourself. Leaves with thick, waxy surfaces don't work well.

### 3. Shopping

- Chickpeas and haricot beans work best: big, fast, easy to measure. Wheat and maize for comparison.
- Buy seeds sold for eating. Seeds sold for planting are sometimes coated in fungicide (often brightly coloured): don't use those.
- Iodine: dilute povidone-iodine 1:10 with water, or tincture 1:5.
- Ethanol or methylated spirit: 100 mL is plenty.
- Soak seeds for step 2 overnight before the session.

---

## Safety briefing (step 1; again at step 5)

- Iodine: gloves and glasses.
- **Ethanol is highly flammable.** You alone use it; heat it only in a bowl of kettle-hot water. No flames, no gas burners in the room, kettle switched off and unplugged before the ethanol comes out.
- Boiling water: you only.
- Hands washed after soil. No seeds eaten.

## Session plans

### Step 1: Two seeds (45 min)

Let students examine the pots before revealing the key. Pot B is the great surprise: most students expect plants in the dark to be smaller, not taller. Don't explain it yet; it comes back in step 5. On the last question (where the mass comes from), most will say "the soil". Leave it open.

### Step 2: Inside a seed (60 min)

Soaked chickpeas roughly double in mass. The embryo is easiest to see in haricot beans. Iodine on the cotyledon: blue-black (starch). Answer to the puzzle: the shoot respires the stored starch for energy until its leaves are in the light.

Onion skin under ×100 shows brick-shaped cells about 100–300 µm long; iodine darkens the nucleus. Potato starch grains are clear under ×100–×400.

### Step 3: Plan a fair test (90 min)

This is the most important session. Steer teams away from questions that take too long (fruiting) or that need equipment you don't have. Good choices for two weeks: light, water amount, salt, crowding, soil vs sand vs paper.

Check every plan before planting: one independent variable; same seed, soil, pot, depth, watering (except when water is the variable), position; at least 5 seeds per group; a control group.

**Expected results** (useful for you, not for students):

- **Light:** dark seedlings germinate as well but grow taller, thinner and yellow (etiolated), then collapse.
- **Water:** no water, no germination. Waterlogged pots (lots of water, poor drainage) may rot.
- **Salt:** germination falls sharply above about 2–5 g/L for chickpeas; at 10 g/L almost nothing.
- **Crowding:** more seedlings, each thinner and shorter after two weeks.
- **Soil vs paper:** germination is similar (seeds don't need soil to start); growth slows on paper after the food store runs out.

### Step 4: Plant your experiment (90 min)

Insist on labels and Day 0 records. Agree a rota, and check it's followed during the two weeks. Gaps in the record are normal; teach students to write "not measured" rather than inventing a number.

### Step 5: Light and leaves (90 min)

**The leaf test:** the uncovered parts turn blue-black; the part under the foil stays yellow-brown. The dark period beforehand ensures any starch was made during the test day. The control is the uncovered part of the same leaf.

The ethanol step: tube of ethanol in a bowl of very hot (just boiled) water, kettle unplugged. The leaf goes pale in about 5–10 minutes. Leaves become brittle; rinsing in warm water softens them.

**Etiolated seedlings:** in the dark, the shoot puts all its stored food into growing upwards fast, to reach light before the store runs out. It doesn't make chlorophyll, which it couldn't use anyway.

### Step 6: Measure and graph (120 min)

Help teams decide how to count non-germinated seeds and to be consistent. Many will want to join the points dot-to-dot; encourage a smooth best-fit curve. Growth curves often look S-shaped.

### Step 7: The poster (75 min)

Run the poster session in two halves so everyone presents and everyone visits. Sign-off conversations can happen at the posters.

## Judging the sign-off items

- **plan:** check the written plan dated before Day 0.
- **seed-parts:** drawing and iodine result; "the cotyledons are a food store for the seedling".
- **log:** six or more measuring days, with units. Honest gaps are fine.
- **graph:** labelled axes and units, key, best-fit lines, a growth rate with units.
- **leaf-test:** light is needed for photosynthesis, which makes the sugar that becomes starch.
- **conclusion:** uses their own numbers, comments on spread, names a real weakness.
