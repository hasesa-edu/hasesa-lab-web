## Build: an orange Earth (15 minutes)

1. Push a bamboo skewer straight through an orange, from top to bottom. This is Earth's **axis**. The top is the North Pole.
2. With a marker, draw the **equator** around the middle.
3. Mark Addis Ababa: a dot about one-tenth of the way from the equator to the North Pole (9° out of 90°). Mark London much higher up: just over halfway (51.5° out of 90°).
4. Mark the Tropic of Cancer (23.4°N, about a quarter of the way up) and the Tropic of Capricorn (23.4°S).

## Do: day and night (20 minutes)

Darken the room as much as you can. One person holds a battery torch steady, about 1 m away: this is the sun. Another holds the orange.

**No tilt.** Hold the skewer straight up and down. Turn the orange slowly towards the east (anticlockwise, seen from above the North Pole).

- Watch the Addis dot and the London dot. Does each spend half a turn in the light and half in the dark?

**Tilted.** Now lean the top of the skewer **towards** the torch by about 23° (a quarter of a right angle). Keep it leaning the same way, and turn the orange again. This is the northern summer, around June.

- Count how much of a turn London spends in the light. And Addis?
- Where on the orange is the torch shining straight down? That is where the noon sun is overhead.

**Walk round the sun.** Keep the skewer leaning towards the **same wall** of the room (not towards the torch), and walk the orange in a big circle around the torch. This is one year. At which points is the North Pole leaning towards the torch? Away from it? Neither?

## Theory: Earth, Sun and time

Read the rest of **Earth, Sun and time**. You'll learn:

- that Earth turns 15° every hour, and 1° every 4 minutes,
- what solar noon, latitude and declination are,
- why the line between day and night always cuts the equator in half, so places near it have days of almost exactly 12 hours,
- how to work out the noon sun's height from your latitude and the date,
- why the sun is straight overhead in Addis twice a year, around **14 April** and **30 August**,
- how Ethiopian time works, and how to convert it.

## Graph: two cities' days (15 minutes)

Here are day lengths (sunrise to sunset) on the 15th of each month:

| Month | Jan | Feb | Mar | Apr | May | Jun | Jul | Aug | Sep | Oct | Nov | Dec |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Addis Ababa (h) | 11.6 | 11.8 | 12.1 | 12.3 | 12.5 | 12.6 | 12.6 | 12.4 | 12.2 | 11.9 | 11.7 | 11.6 |
| London (h) | 8.3 | 9.9 | 11.8 | 13.8 | 15.6 | 16.6 | 16.2 | 14.7 | 12.7 | 10.8 | 8.9 | 7.9 |

1. Plot both cities on **one graph**: day length (y-axis, from 0 to 18 hours) against month (x-axis). Use a key.
2. Draw a smooth curve through each set of points.
3. Work out the **range** (longest minus shortest) for each city, in hours and minutes.

Then answer:

1. If Ethiopia used a dawn clock in London, at what "Ethiopian time" would the sun rise in June? (Hint: it rises at 4:43 am UK time, and the clock would still start at 6:00 am.)
