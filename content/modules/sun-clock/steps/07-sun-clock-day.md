## The sundial challenge (20 minutes)

Every team sets up its sundial on the north–south line from step 2, aimed and tilted correctly. Your guide calls out "**Read!**" three times during the session. Each time, every team writes down, without looking at a phone:

- the sundial time in international hours and in Ethiopian hours,
- the corrected clock time (using the table from step 4).

Then your guide reads the real EAT time. The team with the smallest total error over the three readings wins.

## Back to the mystery (15 minutes)

Open *The shadow mystery* from step 1. Next to each prediction, write ✓ or ✗ and what you now know.

Then answer the first puzzle properly, in your own words: **why can Ethiopian time start the day at dawn, when a dawn clock would be useless in London?** Use these words: *axis, tilt, equator, latitude, sunrise, day length*.

## Explain it to the guide (25 minutes)

Your guide talks to each team in turn. Bring your shadow plot (or its photo), your graphs, your sundial and your report from step 6. Be ready to:

- show how you found north with the equal-shadow method, and why the morning mark is the west end,
- point to solar noon on your shadow-length graph, and explain why it isn't at 12:00 on the clock,
- read your sundial in both Ethiopian and international time, and explain the correction,
- show how you calculated the latitude of Addis from the noon shadow, step by step,
- explain how two noon shadows can measure the whole Earth, and what your answer was,
- explain why the noon sun is straight overhead in Addis twice a year, around 14 April and 30 August, and which way noon shadows point in between.

Take your sundial home. Set it up in a spot that gets sun all day, aimed north and tilted to 9°, and check it against the clock through the year. Watch for the day the sun swaps to the other face.
