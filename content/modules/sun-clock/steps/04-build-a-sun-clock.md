## The idea (10 minutes)

The simplest sundial is a dial that is **parallel to the equator**, with a pencil through it that is **parallel to Earth's axis**. As Earth turns 15° an hour, the pencil's shadow turns 15° an hour round the dial. So the hour lines are simply 15° apart, like a protractor.

To point along Earth's axis from Addis, the pencil must point **due north**, tilted **up** from the ground by an angle equal to your **latitude**: about **9°**. The card dial is then at right angles to the pencil, almost upright.

## Build: the dial (35 minutes)

1. On a 25 × 25 cm card, draw a circle of radius 10 cm in the middle. Mark the centre clearly.
2. Draw a vertical line through the centre. The bottom half of this line is the **noon line**.
3. With a protractor at the centre, draw lines every **15°** from the noon line, out to the circle: 6 lines on each side. The horizontal line through the centre is 6 am on one side and 6 pm on the other.
4. Turn the card over and draw **exactly the same lines** on the back, lined up with the front. Hold it up to the light to check.
5. Make a hole at the centre with the compass point (into a rubber or folded cardboard, pointing away from your hand). Push the pencil through so the same length sticks out on each side, at **right angles** to the card. Check with a protractor in two directions. Fix it with tape or clay.

## Mark the hours (15 minutes)

The two faces are read from opposite sides, so the hours go **opposite ways round**.

**The north face** (the side the pencil's tip points out of): looking at it from the north side, write **12 noon** at the bottom, then **1 pm, 2 pm … 6 pm** going round to the **left**, and **11 am, 10 am … 6 am** going round to the **right**.

**The south face**: looking at it from the south side, write **12 noon** at the bottom, then **1 pm … 6 pm** going round to the **right**, and **11 am … 6 am** to the **left**.

On both faces, write the **Ethiopian time** next to each hour in a second colour: 12 noon is **6 ሰዓት**, 1 pm is **7**, 7 am is **1**, 6 am and 6 pm are **12**.

The sun lights the **north face** from about 21 March to 22 September, and the **south face** from about 23 September to 20 March. Near the equinoxes it shines along the edge, and the dial doesn't work for a few days.

## Build: the stand (15 minutes)

Make a stand from a second card or a small box so that:

- the pencil points **north**, rising at **9°** above horizontal (check with a protractor and the plumb line),
- the card dial leans so its top edge tilts slightly **south**, 9° from upright,
- the noon line points straight down.

Fix everything with tape and clay so a breeze can't move it.

## Do: set it up and read it (20 minutes)

1. Stand the dial on the **north–south line** you found in step 2, with the pencil along the line, pointing north.
2. Read the shadow of the pencil on the lit face. Estimate to the nearest 5 minutes: each 15° is one hour, so 5° is 20 minutes.
3. At the same moment, write down the phone's EAT time.

Your sundial shows **sun time** in Addis, not clock time. To get the EAT clock time, **add** the correction for the date:

| Date | Add to sundial time | Date | Add to sundial time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Jan | 28 min | 1 Jul | 28 min |
| 15 Jan | 34 min | 15 Jul | 31 min |
| 1 Feb | 38 min | 1 Aug | 32 min |
| 15 Feb | 39 min | 15 Aug | 30 min |
| 1 Mar | 38 min | 1 Sep | 25 min |
| 15 Mar | 35 min | 15 Sep | 20 min |
| 1 Apr | 29 min | 1 Oct | 15 min |
| 15 Apr | 25 min | 15 Oct | 11 min |
| 1 May | 22 min | 1 Nov | 9 min |
| 15 May | 21 min | 15 Nov | 10 min |
| 1 Jun | 22 min | 1 Dec | 14 min |
| 15 Jun | 25 min | 15 Dec | 20 min |

Where does that come from? About **25 minutes** is because Addis is 6.3° west of 45°E, the longitude EAT is set for (6.3 × 4 min). The rest, up to 16 minutes either way, is the **equation of time**, caused by Earth's tilted axis and its oval orbit.

Take three readings at least half an hour apart. For each, write the sundial time, the corrected time and the phone time. How far out are you?

## Maths: why not a flat sundial? (extra)

A **horizontal** sundial (a flat dial on the ground) is the kind you see in gardens in Europe. Its hour lines are **not** 15° apart. For latitude $\varphi$, the line for $t$ hours after noon makes an angle $\theta$ with the noon line where

$$\tan\theta = \sin\varphi \times \tan(15° \times t).$$

Work out $\theta$ for $t$ = 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 hours in Addis ($\sin 9° \approx 0.156$). Then try London ($\sin 51.5° \approx 0.783$). Why would a flat sundial be very hard to read in Addis in the middle of the day?
