Injera is one of the world's great fermented foods, and every household that makes it is running a biology experiment. In ten hours you'll meet the microbes that make it, measure what speeds them up and slows them down, and bake your own batches to test an idea.

1. **The bubbling bucket** (45 min). Look at batter at different ages, and predict what's happening.
2. **Alive or not?** (75 min). Balloons, yeast and sugar: prove that the bubbles come from living things.
3. **Measure the gas** (90 min). Collect the gas and find how temperature changes the rate.
4. **The sour side** (75 min). Make an indicator and track the acid in fermenting batter.
5. **Enzymes at work** (90 min). Watch malt amylase turn starch into sugar, and time it.
6. **Bake the test** (120 min). Bake two batches made under different conditions and compare them fairly.
7. **Report** (75 min). Explain injera from flour to eyes.

The batters for steps 4 and 6 are started a few days ahead and need checking between sessions.

By the end you'll understand microbes, respiration, fermentation, enzymes and pH, and why injera tastes the way it does.
