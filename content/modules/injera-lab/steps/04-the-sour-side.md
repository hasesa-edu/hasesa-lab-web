## Do: make an indicator (20 minutes)

1. Chop a quarter of a red cabbage finely. The guide covers it with hot water from the kettle and leaves it for 10 minutes.
2. Strain off the purple liquid. This is your **indicator**.
3. Make a colour scale: put a little indicator in five cups and add:
   - lemon juice or vinegar (strong acid),
   - a few drops of vinegar in water (weak acid),
   - plain water (neutral),
   - a pinch of baking soda (weak base),
   - more baking soda (stronger base).
4. Label the colours with pH values from the chart in **Acids, bases and pH**.

## Do: test the batter (30 minutes)

Your guide started a batter four days ago and has kept a little from each day in the fridge to stop it changing. Test samples from:

- dry teff flour mixed with water (day 0),
- day 1, day 2, day 3 and day 4,
- the ersho (the liquid layer on top),
- a piece of finished injera, crumbled in water.

For each, put 5 mL of the sample (or the liquid that settles out of it) in a cup with 10 mL of indicator. Compare with your colour scale. If you have pH paper, check with that too.

## Notice something puzzling

In step 2, yeast made gas. But yeast fermentation makes carbon dioxide and ethanol, not much acid. So what's making the batter more and more acidic?

## Theory

Read **Acids, bases and pH**, and re-read the **lactic acid** part of *Fermentation and respiration* and the **helpful microbes** in *Microbes*. Why might sour batter be safer from harmful bacteria than fresh batter?

## Plan your bake test (10 minutes)

In step 6 you'll bake injera from two batters that differ in **one** thing. Choose your factor now (see the table in step 6: fermentation time, ersho, temperature or absit), and write down what will stay the same. Give your plan to the guide, who will mix your batters at the right time so they're ready on baking day.

## Record

A table and a graph: pH against day. How many times more acidic is the day-4 batter than day 0? (Remember: each step of 1 on the pH scale is a factor of 10.) Which microbes are responsible for the gas, and which for the acid?
