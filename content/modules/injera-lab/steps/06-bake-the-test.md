## Your experiment (planned in step 4)

In step 4, your team chose **one** factor to test. Your guide mixed your two batters, which differ only in that factor, and has been keeping them. Possible factors:

| Factor | Batter 1 | Batter 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Fermentation time | 1 day | 3 days |
| Ersho | with a cup of ersho | without ersho |
| Temperature | warm place (about 25 °C) | cool place (about 15 °C) |
| Absit | with absit added on the last day | without absit |

Everything else must be the same: flour, water, jar, amount.

Read **Fair tests and averages** again if you need to.

## Before baking (20 minutes)

For each batter, record:

- how high it has risen (mark on the jar),
- its pH (red cabbage indicator or pH paper),
- its smell and thickness.

## Bake (60 minutes, with the guide)

The guide heats the mitad and does a test injera first. Then, for each batter:

1. The guide stands beside you. Pour the same amount (use a cup measure) in a spiral from the outside in.
2. Watch the surface. When do the eyes appear? Time it.
3. The guide covers it, then lifts the lid and takes the injera off.

Bake at least two injeras from each batter.

## Measure (25 minutes)

When the injeras have cooled:

- **Eyes:** count the eyes in three 2 cm × 2 cm squares on each injera. Work out the mean per cm².
- **pH:** crumble a piece in water and test it.
- **Blind taste test:** someone outside your team cuts small pieces, labelled only X and Y. Five tasters each score sourness (1–5) and liking (1–5). Taste only if you want to, and only with clean hands.

## Record (15 minutes)

A results table with every measurement, and means. Which batter made better injera, and by what measure? Was the difference bigger than the spread between injeras from the same batter?
