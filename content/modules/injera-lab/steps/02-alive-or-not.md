## Do: the balloon test (40 minutes)

Each team sets up five small bottles. In each, put 100 mL of warm water (about 35 °C, comfortable on your wrist) and:

| Bottle | Add |
|---|---|
| A | 1 teaspoon of yeast + 2 teaspoons of sugar |
| B | 1 teaspoon of yeast, no sugar |
| C | 2 teaspoons of sugar, no yeast |
| D | 1 teaspoon of yeast + 2 teaspoons of sugar, but in cold water with ice (about 5 °C) |
| E | 1 teaspoon of yeast that the guide has boiled for 2 minutes and cooled, + 2 teaspoons of sugar |

Swirl each one. Stretch a balloon over each neck. Put A, B, C and E in a warm water bath; D in an ice bath.

Every 10 minutes, measure each balloon's **circumference** with a string and ruler.

Add a sixth bottle: 2 tablespoons of **3-day-old teff batter** in warm water, with a balloon. Does it behave like bottle A?

## Notice something puzzling

Bottles C and E have sugar but no balloon growth. Bottle E has sugar **and** yeast. What's the difference between the yeast in A and the yeast in E? What does that tell you about the bubbles?

## Theory: fermentation and respiration

Read **Fermentation and respiration**. You'll learn:

- what respiration is, with and without oxygen,
- the word equation for yeast fermentation,
- the word equation for lactic acid fermentation, which makes no gas,
- what happens in injera batter, step by step.

## Do: test the gas (10 minutes, with the guide)

Carefully take the balloon off bottle A, keeping the gas in by pinching the neck. Bubble the gas through a little **limewater** (the guide has some) using a straw or tube. Limewater turns milky with carbon dioxide.

## Record

A table of balloon circumference for every bottle, every 10 minutes. For each bottle, one sentence explaining the result. Then complete the word equation for what happened in bottle A.
