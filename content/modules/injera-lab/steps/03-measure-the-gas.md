## The question

Balloons show **that** gas is made, but not exactly **how much**. To compare fairly, you need to measure the volume of gas, and the rate.

Question: **How does temperature affect how fast yeast ferments sugar?**

## Build: a gas collector (20 minutes)

1. Fill a measuring cylinder (or a clear bottle marked every 10 mL) completely with water. Cover the top with your hand, turn it upside down, and stand it in a bowl of water so no air gets in.
2. Push one end of a plastic tube into the cylinder from underneath.
3. Push the other end through a hole in the cap of a small bottle. Seal around the tube with modelling clay.
4. When you put yeast, sugar and warm water in the small bottle and close the cap, gas will travel down the tube and push water out of the cylinder. Read the volume of gas from the scale.

## Plan (10 minutes)

Each team takes **one** temperature: 10 °C (ice bath), 20 °C (room), 30 °C, 40 °C or 50 °C (guide makes the warm baths). Agree as a class what stays the same: amount of yeast, sugar and water, size of bottle, how long you wait before you start timing.

## Do (40 minutes)

1. Put your bottle in its water bath for 5 minutes so the liquid reaches the temperature. Check with a thermometer.
2. Add yeast, close the cap and connect the tube.
3. Wait 5 minutes for the yeast to get started (bubbles in the first minutes are mostly warm air expanding).
4. Then record the gas volume every 2 minutes for 20 minutes. Keep checking the bath temperature.

## Graph (20 minutes)

1. Plot your own results: gas volume (y) against time (x). Find the gradient: that's the rate in mL/min.
2. Collect every team's rate. Draw a class graph of **rate** (y) against **temperature** (x).
3. Describe the shape. Where is the rate highest? What happens at 50 °C?

## Record

Your table, your two graphs, and three sentences: what temperature is best, why it's slower when cold, and why it's slower (or zero) when too hot. Which temperature in your home is closest to the best?
