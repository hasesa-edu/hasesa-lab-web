# Soap & Bioplastic: guide's notes

For the guide only. About 12 hours, run over **about six weeks** because soap must cure for four weeks before step 6. Up to 12 students in teams of three. This is the module with the most serious hazard in the Academy: **sodium hydroxide**. Read the lye section of *Fats, oils and soap* yourself, practise the soap recipe at home at least twice, and never run step 5 alone: have a second adult present.

## Before the module

### 1. Lye: buying, storing, handling

- Buy 99% sodium hydroxide flakes or pellets from a chemical supplier in Merkato. Drain-cleaner products may contain other chemicals: don't use them.
- Store in its original, clearly labelled, airtight container (it absorbs water from the air), locked away from students, food and acids.
- Have ready on the day: splash goggles for everyone, nitrile gloves, aprons, a bowl of clean water, access to a running tap, and paper towels and a waste bag.
- **First aid:** skin, rinse with running water for at least 15 minutes and remove contaminated clothing. Eyes, rinse for at least 15 minutes, holding the eyelid open, and get medical help immediately. Don't use vinegar on skin or in eyes.
- Know the location of the nearest clinic before the session.

### 2. Practise the soap

A reliable beginner recipe (5% superfat):

| Ingredient | Mass |
|---|---|
| Palm oil | 250 g |
| Sunflower oil | 150 g |
| Coconut oil | 100 g |
| Sodium hydroxide | 68 g |
| Water | 170 g |

Check: 250 × 0.142 = 35.5; 150 × 0.134 = 20.1; 100 × 0.183 = 18.3; total 73.9 g; × 0.95 = 70.2 g. With different SAP values from different suppliers, 68–70 g is fine. **Recalculate for the oils you actually buy**, and check each team's recipe before step 5. If you only have sunflower and palm, that's fine too.

Mix at 40–45 °C. Trace takes 5–20 minutes with a stick blender, 30–60 minutes by hand. Sunflower-heavy recipes trace slowly.

### 3. Practise the bioplastic

The recipe in step 3 works with cornstarch and with potato starch. Heat on low, stirring; it turns clear in 3–6 minutes. Films 2–3 mm thick dry in 2–3 days at room temperature; turn them over after a day. Make a batch **a week before step 3** so students can bury strips from it in the biodegradation pots during step 3.

### 4. Shopping

See the module materials list. Buy extra starch and oil for mistakes. Moulds: silicone loaf moulds, plastic food boxes, or cartons cut lengthways (line them with baking paper).

## Safety briefing (step 1; in full at step 2; again at the start of step 5)

- Goggles, gloves, apron, long sleeves whenever lye or raw soap is present.
- You alone handle solid lye and lye solution. Lye into water, never water into lye. Outdoors or at an open window. No aluminium.
- Students stir raw batter only with you beside them.
- First aid as above. Everyone knows where the water and sink are.
- Hot starch mixture: you heat; students stir with long spoons.
- Nothing is eaten. Soap is used on skin only after curing and testing at pH 10 or below.

## Session plans

### Step 1: From the market (45 min)

Use a bar of soap you made at least four weeks earlier and tested. Students are usually surprised that the soap is "made from oil".

### Step 2: Oil and water (75 min)

Without soap, oil and water separate in seconds; with a drop of soap, the emulsion lasts minutes; washing powder often lasts longest. Cold water alone barely cleans; hot water helps a little; soap clearly wins. Epsom salt (magnesium sulfate) in water makes it hard and reduces lather, with some scum.

### Step 3: Starch to plastic (120 min)

**Expected:** 0 mL glycerol: hard, brittle, cracks when bent, often curls. 2.5 mL: stiff, slightly flexible. 5 mL: flexible and fairly strong. 10 mL: very soft, stretchy, slightly sticky, weaker. The heating change (gelatinisation) happens around 60–70 °C: the starch grains swell and burst, releasing their chains.

Keep the films flat and in the same place to dry.

### Step 4: Calculate the lye (75 min)

The calculation is the most important safety step in the module. Check every recipe yourself and sign it. Typical errors: forgetting one oil, applying the superfat to each oil and then again to the total, using the KOH SAP value instead of the NaOH one.

**Film tests:** flexibility rises with glycerol; breaking mass usually falls above about 5 mL.

### Step 5: Make soap (150 min)

Run one team at a time at the lye stage if you're the only person allowed to handle it, with the second adult supervising the other teams' oil preparation. Expected pH of raw batter: about 12–13. After 4 weeks: about 9–10.

Unmould after 24–48 hours (wear gloves; fresh soap is still caustic), cut into bars, and cure on a rack in a dry, airy place, labelled with the team and date, out of students' reach.

**If something goes wrong:** soap that stays oily and separated after 48 hours, or has white crusty pockets, may have had a measuring error. Don't let anyone use it. Test it at step 6 as a learning example, then dispose of it safely.

### Step 6: Test and compare (120 min)

pH: test before anything else. Commercial soaps range from about pH 9–10 (true soaps) to 5–7 (synthetic "beauty bars"). Lather: home-made soaps with palm and sunflower lather less than commercial soaps; coconut oil helps.

**Biodegradation:** after 4 weeks in damp soil, starch bioplastic typically loses a large share of its mass, often becoming fragmented, with mould; the plastic bag strip loses almost nothing. Keep the soil damp throughout.

### Step 7: The product pitch (90 min)

Market prices for the cost estimate: have students research them in advance, or give them a price list. Sign-off conversations during preparation.

## Judging the sign-off items

- **bioplastic:** heating bursts starch grains (gelatinisation); vinegar helps break branched chains; glycerol is a plasticiser.
- **plasticiser-test:** at least three glycerol levels; same thickness and drying; a graph.
- **lye-calc:** correct working, superfat applied once to the total, checked and signed **before** step 5.
- **soap-safety:** no reminders needed. Any serious breach means no sign-off for this item until discussed.
- **soap-chemistry:** fat + sodium hydroxide → soap + glycerol; head and tail explanation.
- **product-tests:** numbers for both comparisons.
