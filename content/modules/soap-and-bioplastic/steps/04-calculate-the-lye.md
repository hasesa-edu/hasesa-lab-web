## First: test your films (25 minutes)

Your films from step 3 have dried. Peel them off carefully. For each one, record:

- **look and feel:** clear or cloudy, smooth, sticky, brittle?
- **flexibility:** wrap a strip around rods of decreasing diameter (a pipe, a marker, a pencil, a skewer). What's the smallest it bends around without cracking?
- **strength:** cut a strip 2 cm wide. Clamp one end, hang a paper clip from the other and add weights (coins or sand bags) until it tears. Record the mass that broke it and the strip's thickness.

Graph flexibility (or breaking mass) against the amount of glycerol. Were your predictions right?

## The soap question

Next session you'll make soap. Too little lye and the soap is soft and greasy. Too much and it's caustic: dangerous to use. You need **exactly** the right amount, and then a little less.

## Theory: reacting quantities

Read **Reacting quantities**. You'll learn:

- why reactions need fixed ratios,
- what SAP values are and how to use them,
- limiting and excess reactants,
- why soap-makers use a superfat.

Re-read **Rearranging formulas** if you need help working backwards.

## Calculate your recipe (35 minutes)

Your guide tells you which oils are available. Design a recipe for **500 g of oil in total**, using one or two oils. Then work out:

1. the NaOH needed for each oil (oil mass × SAP value),
2. the total NaOH,
3. the NaOH with a **5% superfat** (× 0.95),
4. the water: 2.5 × the mass of NaOH.

Show every line of working, with units. Then **swap** with another team and check each other's calculations independently. If you disagree, find the mistake.

## Record

Your final recipe card: oils (g), NaOH (g), water (g), superfat (%), and the names of the two people who checked it. Give it to your guide, who checks it again. **No recipe is made without the guide's signature.**
