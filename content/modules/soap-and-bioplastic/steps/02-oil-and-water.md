## Do: oil and water (25 minutes)

1. Put 20 mL of water and 5 mL of cooking oil in a clear bottle with a lid. Shake hard for 10 seconds. Time how long it takes to separate into two clear layers.
2. Add one drop of liquid soap. Shake again. Time it again.
3. Repeat with two drops, then with a pinch of washing powder.

Draw what you see each time.

## Do: clean a greasy plate (15 minutes)

Smear the same amount of oil on three saucers. Try to clean each with:

- cold water alone,
- hot water alone (guide pours),
- cold water with soap.

Rub each the same number of times with a cloth. Which leaves the saucer least greasy? How can you tell?

## Do: hard and soft water (10 minutes)

Shake soap with rainwater (or distilled water) in one bottle, and with tap water from a borehole area (if available) or water with a pinch of Epsom salt in another. Compare the lather. What forms in the "hard" water?

## Theory: fats, oils and soap

Read **Fats, oils and soap**. You'll learn:

- what a fat or oil molecule looks like,
- how sodium hydroxide turns it into soap and glycerol (saponification),
- how a soap molecule's head and tail let it carry grease into water,
- why hard water makes scum,
- why lye is dangerous and how to handle it.

## Record (25 minutes)

Your timing table. A labelled drawing of a soap molecule, and a drawing of soap molecules surrounding an oil droplet (a micelle) in water. Write the word equation for saponification. Finally, write the lye safety rules in your own words: you'll need them in step 5.
