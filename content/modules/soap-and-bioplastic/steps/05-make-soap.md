## Safety first (20 minutes)

Before anything else:

- Goggles, gloves, apron and long sleeves on, for everyone, for the whole session.
- Find the bowl of clean water and the sink. Your guide shows you what to do if lye touches skin (rinse with running water for 15 minutes) or eyes (rinse for 15 minutes and get medical help).
- Clear the bench of everything not needed. No food or drinks.
- Check all containers: plastic (PP) or stainless steel only. **No aluminium.**

Read your safety rules from step 2 aloud as a team.

## The lye solution (guide only, 15 minutes)

Outdoors or at an open window, the guide weighs the water into a heat-resistant plastic jug, then weighs the sodium hydroxide separately and slowly adds the **lye to the water**, stirring, face turned away from the fumes. The solution gets very hot. It's set aside, labelled, to cool to about 40–45 °C.

Watch from a distance and record the temperature rise.

## Prepare the oils (20 minutes)

Weigh your oils exactly to your recipe card. If you're using a solid oil (coconut, palm), the guide warms it gently until melted, then mixes it with the liquid oil. Let the oils cool to about 40–45 °C as well.

## Make soap (45 minutes)

1. When both are at about 40–45 °C, the guide slowly pours the lye solution into your oils.
2. You stir steadily with a long spoon (or the guide uses a stick blender in short bursts). Keep your face back.
3. Watch it change: thin and oily, then cloudy, then thicker like custard. When a drizzle from the spoon leaves a trail on the surface for a moment, it has reached **trace**.
4. Pour it into your mould. Tap the mould to remove bubbles. Cover with a cloth and leave it somewhere warm where it won't be disturbed.

## Test the pH (20 minutes)

Using a fresh piece of pH paper held with tweezers, the guide touches it to a smear of raw batter left in the jug. Record the colour and pH. Compare with oil alone and with the cooled lye solution (guide only).

Read **Acids, bases and pH**. Why is the raw batter so alkaline? What do you expect to happen to the pH over the next few weeks?

## Clean up (15 minutes)

Everything that touched lye or batter is wiped with paper towel (into a bag), then washed with plenty of water while still wearing gloves.

## Record (15 minutes)

Your recipe, the temperatures, the time to trace, and the pH. In 1–2 days, the guide unmoulds the soap and cuts it into bars, which cure on a rack for 4 weeks.
