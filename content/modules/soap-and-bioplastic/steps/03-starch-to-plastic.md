## The basic recipe

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Cornstarch (or potato starch) | 10 g |
| Water | 60 mL |
| White vinegar | 5 mL |
| Glycerol | 5 mL (this is what you'll change) |

## Plan a fair test (20 minutes)

Your question: **How does the amount of glycerol change the plastic?** Read **Fair tests and averages** if you need a reminder.

Each team makes four films with **0, 2.5, 5 and 10 mL** of glycerol. Everything else stays the same. Write down your controlled variables: starch, water, vinegar, heating time and temperature, how thick you spread it, drying time and place.

## Make the films (60 minutes)

For each film:

1. Mix the starch and cold water in a small saucepan until smooth. Add the vinegar and the glycerol. Stir.
2. The guide heats it on a low setting while you stir **constantly** with a long spoon.
3. Watch it change: milky and runny, then suddenly thick, then clear and gel-like. Keep stirring for 2 more minutes after it turns clear.
4. The guide pours it onto a labelled sheet of baking paper on a tray. Spread it evenly with a spatula to about 2–3 mm thick. Use a ruler or two sticks as guides so all films are the same thickness.

Leave the films to dry in the same place for 2–3 days (the guide keeps them).

## Notice something puzzling

The mixture changed from runny and white to thick and clear in a few seconds, at a certain temperature. What happened to the starch grains? (Think of what happens when you make a sauce, or the absit for injera.)

## Theory

Read **Polymers**, especially **plasticisers** and **making a starch bioplastic**. Predict which film will be the most bendy and which the strongest.

## Record (40 minutes)

A table of your four recipes, your controlled variables and your predictions. A drawing of the heating stages. While the films dry, start the biodegradation test: the guide will give you a strip of last week's bioplastic and a strip of plastic bag, the same size. Weigh each, bury them in separate labelled pots of damp soil, and note the date.
