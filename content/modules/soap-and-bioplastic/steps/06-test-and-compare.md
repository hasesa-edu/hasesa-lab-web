About four weeks have passed. Your soap has cured, and your plastic strips have been in the soil.

## Soap tests (60 minutes)

Test your soap and a commercial soap side by side. Gloves on until the pH result is known.

1. **pH:** dissolve a small shaving (about 1 g) in 10 mL of distilled water. Test with pH paper and with red cabbage indicator. Your soap must be **pH 10 or below** before anyone uses it on skin. If it's higher, it's not ready, or the recipe had too much lye: tell the guide.
2. **Lather:** put 1 g of soap shavings and 20 mL of water in a bottle. Shake 10 times. Measure the height of the foam immediately and after 5 minutes.
3. **Hardness:** press a nail into the bar with a fixed weight (for example, a 500 g bag balanced on it) for 10 seconds. Measure how deep it goes.
4. **Cleaning:** clean an oily saucer as in step 2. Which soap cleans better?

Re-read the part of *Chemical tests* about controls and fair tests: what is the control here, and why must both soaps be tested in exactly the same way?

## Plastic tests (40 minutes)

1. Dig up the bioplastic strip and the plastic-bag strip from step 3. Rinse gently, dry them, and weigh them. Calculate the **percentage mass lost** for each:
   $$\text{mass lost \%} = \frac{\text{mass before} - \text{mass after}}{\text{mass before}} \times 100$$
2. Look closely at both with a magnifying glass. Any holes, mould, colour change?
3. Put a fresh strip of your best bioplastic and a strip of plastic bag in water for 10 minutes. Weigh before and after. Which absorbs water?

Re-read **The plastic problem** in *Polymers*. Why did one strip break down and the other not?

## Record (20 minutes)

A results table for each product compared with its shop-bought rival, with numbers. For each product, one strength and one weakness. Would you use your bioplastic for a shopping bag? For a seedling pot? Why?
