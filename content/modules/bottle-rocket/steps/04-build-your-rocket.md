## Check your bottle (5 minutes)

Use a fizzy-drink bottle with no dents, cracks or deep scratches. Peel off the label. Your guide checks every bottle before it is launched.

## Build (50 minutes)

**Fins.** Cut three or four identical fins from stiff cardboard or plastic folder. A good shape is a right-angled triangle about 12 cm tall and 8 cm wide. Make a template first so they're all the same.

Tape them near the **mouth** of the bottle (the bottle flies upside down, so the mouth is the back). Space them evenly: for three fins, mark the bottle at every 120°; for four, every 90°. Keep them straight along the bottle, not twisted, and keep the last 3 cm of the neck clear so it fits the launcher.

**Nose cone.** Roll a cone of cardboard or cut the top off a second bottle. Put a small lump of modelling clay inside the tip (start with about 30 g), then tape the cone onto the bottom of the bottle (which becomes the top).

**Weigh it.** Record your rocket's empty mass.

## The balance test (15 minutes)

A rocket flies straight when its **centre of mass** (balance point) is closer to the nose than its **centre of pressure** (roughly, the middle of its side area, which the fins pull backwards).

1. Balance your empty rocket sideways on one finger. Mark the balance point.
2. Tie a 1.5 m string at that point and swing the rocket around your head in a circle (outside, nobody nearby).
3. If it flies nose-first, it's stable. If it wobbles or goes tail-first, add more clay to the nose or make the fins bigger, and try again.

## First flights (20 minutes)

Each team launches once, with about a third of the bottle full of water, at 3 bar. Follow every launch rule. Watch: does your rocket fly straight, wobble, or tumble?

## Record

Sketch your rocket with measurements: fin size and number, nose mass, total mass. Note how the first flight went and **one change** you'd make. Make it before the next session.
