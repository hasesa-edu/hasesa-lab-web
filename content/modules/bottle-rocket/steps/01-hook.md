## The launch rules first

Before anything flies, your guide reads out the launch rules. Copy them into the front of your notebook:

1. Safety glasses on at every launch.
2. Everyone behind the line, 10 m back, while the rocket is pumped.
3. Only the guide pulls the release, after a countdown from five.
4. Nobody goes near a rocket that didn't launch until the guide has let the air out.
5. Never catch a falling rocket.

## Watch

Your guide pours some water into a fizzy-drink bottle, fixes it upside down on the launcher and connects a bicycle pump. Watch the pump's gauge as it climbs. Then: **5, 4, 3, 2, 1**.

Look carefully at:

- what comes out of the bottle, and in which direction,
- how long the push lasts,
- how the rocket moves after the water runs out,
- how it comes down.

Your guide will launch it again, this time **without any water**, at the same pressure. Then once **almost full** of water.

## Predict

On a page titled *The mystery*, write:

1. **What pushed the rocket up?** Write your best guess, then a second one.
2. **Why did the empty bottle and the full bottle do so badly?**
3. **Guess:** how high did the best launch go, in metres? Each person writes a number. You'll find out how close you were in step 5.

## Questions

As a team, list every question you have. For example:

- Why does the water have to come out of the bottom?
- What would happen with more pressure?
- Why do some rockets wobble and tumble?
- How could we ever measure how high it went?

## The first clue

Here is a clue to take home: **every push has a push back**. Read *Forces and motion* tonight. Next time, you'll race rockets along a string.
