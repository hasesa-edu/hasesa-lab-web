## The question

In step 1, the empty bottle hardly flew, and the almost-full one wasn't much better. Somewhere in between is the best amount. Your job is to find it, and to **prove** it.

## Theory: fair tests and averages

Read **Fair tests and averages**. You'll learn:

- the independent, dependent and controlled variables,
- why you need to repeat each setting at least three times,
- how to work out a mean and a range,
- what to do with an outlier.

## Plan (15 minutes)

Write a fair-test plan in five lines:

1. **Question:** Does the amount of water affect how high the rocket flies?
2. **I will change:** the amount of water. Choose at least four amounts, for example 0, 300, 600 and 900 mL in a 2 L bottle. Measure them with a jug.
3. **I will measure:** the height, with two clinometers.
4. **I will keep the same:** ___ (list at least four things).
5. **Repeats:** three launches per amount.

Show your plan to the guide before you launch.

## Do: launch (50 minutes)

Work through your amounts. Before every launch check: glasses on, everyone behind the line, the same pressure every time, the same rocket. Record every launch, even the bad ones, and note anything unusual (a gust, a fin bending).

## Theory: graphs

Read **Graphs and relationships** to learn which variable goes on which axis, how to draw a line or curve of best fit, and how to read the shape.

## Record (25 minutes)

1. Make a results table: amount of water, three heights, mean, range.
2. Draw a graph of **mean height** (y-axis) against **amount of water** (x-axis). Draw a smooth curve of best fit.
3. Answer: which amount is best? Is the difference between the best two amounts bigger than their ranges? What would you test next to be more certain?
