## Do: balloon rockets (30 minutes)

Tie a 5 m string between two chairs, tight and level. Thread a drinking straw onto it first.

1. Blow up a balloon. Pinch the neck (don't tie it).
2. Tape the balloon to the straw, with the neck pointing towards one chair.
3. Let go. Time how long it takes to reach the end, or mark how far it gets.

Now change **one thing** at a time and see what happens:

- a big balloon vs a small (less blown-up) balloon,
- a balloon carrying a paper clip "payload" vs one with nothing,
- two balloons taped side by side.

Record each try in a table: what you changed, how far or how fast it went.

## Notice something puzzling

The air comes out of the balloon **backwards**, but the balloon goes **forwards**. The air out there isn't pushing on anything solid. So what pushes the balloon?

And why did the paper clip slow it down, even though the balloon pushed just as hard?

## Theory: forces and motion

Read **Forces and motion**. You'll learn:

- what a force is, and that it is measured in newtons,
- Newton's first law: nothing speeds up or slows down without an unbalanced force,
- Newton's second law, $F = ma$: more mass means less acceleration for the same push,
- Newton's third law: forces come in pairs, so pushing air or water backwards pushes you forwards,
- the difference between mass and weight.

## Do it better: feel the third law (20 minutes)

- Stand on a skateboard or a smooth floor in socks, and push a partner's hands. Who moves?
- Throw a heavy ball while standing on the skateboard (outside, with space). Which way do you roll?

Then answer in your notebook: in the water rocket, what is being thrown out, and which way? So which way must the rocket go?

## Record

Draw a **force diagram** of the water rocket at the moment of launch: one arrow for the thrust and one for the weight, with labels. Make the arrows the right lengths compared with each other. Then draw one for the moment the water runs out.
