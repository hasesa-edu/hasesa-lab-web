## The case

A box has arrived at the Academy. Inside is a note:

> *"We bought these from different market stalls. Some traders are honest. Some are not. We need to know which samples are faked, how, and we need proof. Please help."*

There are four foods, each with four samples labelled **A, B, C, D**:

- **Honey** (mar)
- **Milk** (wetet)
- **Berbere**
- **Kibe** (spiced butter)

Your job for the next ten hours: find the fakes and prove it.

## Rule zero

**Never taste anything in the case file.** Not even a lick from your finger. Some fakes contain things that are not food at all, and we share spoons and cups. Your tools are your eyes, your nose (by wafting), a scale, and chemistry.

Put on gloves and safety glasses before you open any sample.

## Investigate with your senses (25 minutes)

Work in teams of three or four. For each food, line up A–D next to each other on a white sheet of paper. Compare them **side by side**: differences jump out when samples are next to each other.

Make an observation table in your notebook with one row per sample. Look for:

- **Colour and clearness.** Clear or cloudy? Bright or dull? Same all through, or with specks or layers?
- **How it flows.** Tilt the honey and milk cups slowly. Which honey pours like a slow rope, and which runs like juice? Does any milk look thinner or bluish?
- **Texture.** Rub a tiny pinch of each berbere between gloved fingertips over the paper. Soft and fine, or gritty? Look at kibe with a spoon: smooth, or lumpy with pale bits?
- **Smell.** Hold the cup away from your face and waft the air towards you with your hand. Flowers? Spices? Nothing? Something sour?

Write what you **observe**, not what you think. "Runs fast, smells of almost nothing" is an observation. "Has syrup in it" is a suspicion, and goes in a different column.

## Notice something puzzling

Some samples look almost identical. Two berberes may be the same bright red. Two milks may look equally white. Your senses can raise suspicion, but they cannot prove anything. A faker's whole job is to fool your eyes.

So what can we measure that a faker *cannot* easily copy?

## Plan the investigation (15 minutes)

As a team, brainstorm: for each food, what might a faker add, and what property would that change? For example:

- Water in milk would make it... thinner? lighter? How could we measure "lighter"?
- A cheap red powder in berbere: would it dissolve? sink? float?

Write a first **suspect list** (which samples you think are faked) and a **test plan** (which tests you want to try). Keep this page: at the end of the case you will compare your first suspicions with the proof.

## Before you go

Read the concept unit **Properties of matter** at home. It explains the difference between properties a faker can fake easily (colour) and properties that are hard to fake (density, solubility, chemical reactions).
