## Do: build the evidence board (40 minutes)

Make one big **evidence table** for the whole team: 16 rows (Honey A–D, Milk A–D, Berbere A–D, Kibe A–D) and a column for every test you ran:

| Sample | Senses | Water glass / density | Starch (iodine) | Dye (Rf) | pH / fizz | Sediment | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Fill in every cell with a measurement or an observation, not just a tick. "1.33 g/mL" is evidence. "Looks fake" is not. Put "not tested" where a test did not apply.

Stick your photos and chromatogram next to the table.

## Notice: what counts as proof?

Look at each sample. How many **independent** tests point the same way? A strong case has:

- a **measurement** that is outside the genuine range, or a clear test result,
- a **control** that shows what genuine looks like,
- ideally a **second test** that agrees.

Where your evidence is weak or two tests disagree, say so honestly. If you can, **re-test** now. A good detective would rather say "probably" than be confidently wrong.

## Write the case report (40 minutes)

For each of the four foods, write a short section:

1. **Verdict.** Which samples are genuine and which are faked?
2. **What the faker did.** For example: "Milk C was watered and then thickened with flour."
3. **How we proved it.** Name the tests, give the numbers (density with the calculation, Rf values, sediment percentage) and the controls you used.
4. **Confidence.** Strong, moderate or weak, and why.

Finish with:

- a comparison of your **first suspicions** from the hook with your final verdicts. Which ones did your senses get right? Which ones fooled you?
- one paragraph of **advice for shoppers**: one simple check anyone could do at home, and why it works.

## Present the case (35 minutes)

Each team has about 5 minutes to present to the class and the guide, like detectives closing a case. Pick one food to explain in depth and show your strongest piece of evidence. Then take questions. Other teams should ask: "How do you know it wasn't contamination?" or "What was your control?"

When every team has presented, the guide opens the sealed answer key. How did you do?

## Clean up and sign off (5 minutes)

Dispose of every sample as the guide directs. Wash all cups and spoons. Wash your hands.

Then go through the sign-off checklist with your guide.
