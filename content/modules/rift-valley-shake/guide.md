# Rift Valley Shake: guide's notes

For the guide only. About 10 hours, best as five sessions of about two hours, for up to 16 students in teams of four. Everything is cheap and safe; the main skills you need are keeping the shake tests consistent and helping students look carefully at **where** things fail.

---

## Before the module

### 1. Build a class shake table and the two demo towers (a week ahead)

Build one shake table exactly as in step 3; it becomes the "official" table for the final test. Use a 50 × 40 cm base with two end blocks, eight marbles (or 2 cm PVC pipe rollers, which don't roll away), and inner-tube strips for springs. The top board should move freely about ±5 cm.

**Demo towers for step 1** (both about 50 cm, 200 g on top):

- **Tower A (fails):** four tall spaghetti bundles as legs, horizontal ties every 10 cm, **no diagonals**, narrow base (10 cm), weight at the top.
- **Tower B (survives):** a wider base (20 cm), tapering upwards, diagonal cross-bracing on all four sides, strong clay joints.

Test them first. At about 2 Hz, tower A should fold sideways within a few seconds; tower B should survive. Build spares: you'll want to repeat the demo.

### 2. Shopping

| Item | Notes |
|---|---|
| Spaghetti | 4–5 packets: towers eat a lot. |
| Clay | Plasticine or a homemade flour-salt dough (dries out; keep it wrapped). Small marshmallows also work as joints. |
| Slinkies | Plastic or metal; metal ones show P waves better. |
| Boards | Ask a carpenter for four pairs, so every team has a table. |
| Sand weights | Small plastic bags of 50 g, 100 g, 200 g, taped shut. |

### 3. Maps

Print a map of Ethiopia with the Rift Valley, and a map of central Ethiopia with a scale bar showing Addis Ababa, Adama, Debre Berhan and Mount Fentale.

---

## Safety briefing (step 1; again at steps 4 and 6)

- Glasses on whenever a tower is shaken.
- Skewer points away from people.
- Fingers off the table edges and rollers while shaking.
- Marbles counted in and out.
- No eating materials.

---

## Session plans

### Step 1: The shaking table (45 min)

Let students draw both towers **before** the shake. Afterwards, ask them to point to the place where tower A failed first. Typical answer: the joints near the base, as the frame leaned over.

### Step 2: A cracking continent (75 min)

Foam or a 1–2 cm layer of play dough on card cracks nicely when pulled; a central block often drops. P waves on a metal slinky travel faster than S waves, but on a short slinky the difference is hard to time; several repeats and the class mean help. Ropes can carry S waves but not P waves (you can't push a rope).

**Epicentre answers:** Addis gap 16 s → about 128 km; Adama and Debre Berhan gap 11 s → about 88 km each. The circles cross near **Mount Fentale**, close to the 2025 earthquake swarm. Accept anything nearby: hand-drawn circles are rough.

### Step 3: Build a shake table (90 min)

Common problems: rollers jamming (use smooth boards and fewer, bigger rollers), bands too loose (the board doesn't return) or too tight (hard to push). Keeping a steady frequency by hand is surprisingly hard; let everyone practise with the metronome. **120 beats per minute with one full cycle per two beats = 1 Hz.**

### Step 4: Strong shapes (75 min)

**Expected:** square folds; triangle doesn't; one rigid diagonal fixes both directions; one string diagonal only works when it's being stretched (in tension), so it stops leaning in one direction only; an X of string works both ways. Long spaghetti buckles easily; short pieces are much stronger. Frame C beats B beats A at level 2.

### Step 5: Sway and resonance (90 min)

Use wooden rulers or strips of stiff card for the towers; they give clear natural frequencies of roughly 1–5 Hz depending on height and mass. **Expected:** taller or heavier (mass higher up) → lower natural frequency. The resonance graph peaks near the plucked natural frequency. Damping with a hanging bag of rice reduces the peak noticeably; base isolation on marbles also helps a lot.

The resonance concept unit was written for the Radio module. Point students to its first section only.

### Step 6: Design and test (120 min)

Enforce the budget: hand out materials in a bag per team. Encourage filming in slow motion to see the **first** failure. The best towers usually have a wide base, full diagonal bracing, careful joints, and the load mounted low on the top platform. Some teams discover that a stiffer tower can survive level 3 easily but fail at level 2 because of resonance; that's an excellent discussion.

### Step 7: The big shake (60 min)

You run the shaking, the same way for every team. Have a student operate the metronome and another time each level. Sign-off conversations happen at the end.

## Judging the sign-off items

- **rift:** plates pulling apart, crust cracking along faults and dropping.
- **waves:** demonstrates both on the slinky; uses the 8 × gap rule correctly.
- **frames:** a triangle's shape is fixed by its side lengths.
- **resonance:** a graph with a peak; connects it to natural frequency.
- **tower:** survives, or a precise failure analysis with a credible fix.
- **design-reasons:** every choice backed by a test they did.
