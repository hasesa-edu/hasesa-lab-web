## Do: towers have a rhythm (25 minutes)

Make three simple towers from a single strip of thin card or a wooden ruler, clamped or taped upright to the shake table, with a lump of clay on top:

- short (15 cm), medium (25 cm), tall (40 cm).

Pull the top of each to one side and let go. Count how many swings it makes in 10 seconds. Calculate its **natural frequency** (swings per second).

What happens to the natural frequency when you:

- make the tower taller?
- move the clay lower?
- add more clay?

## Notice something puzzling

Now shake the table slowly (0.5 Hz), then gradually faster, up to 4 Hz. Watch all three towers at once. Each tower sways wildly at one shaking speed, but hardly at all at others. And it's a **different** speed for each tower.

## Theory: resonance

Read the section **Everything has a natural frequency** in *Resonance and tuning*. (The rest of that unit is about radios, which use the same idea with electricity.) Then re-read **earthquake forces** in *Structures and forces*.

Why would a medium-height building collapse while shorter and taller ones beside it survive?

## Do: measure it (40 minutes)

For each tower:

1. Shake at 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 2.5, 3 and 4 Hz (use the metronome), 10 seconds each, same amplitude.
2. Measure how far the top swings each time (the amplitude, in cm), against a ruler held behind it. A phone video in slow motion helps.

Draw a graph of the top's amplitude (y) against shaking frequency (x) for each tower. Where is the peak? Compare it with the natural frequency you measured by plucking.

## Do: tame the sway (15 minutes)

Try one of these on the tower that sways worst, at its resonant frequency:

- a **damper**: a small bag of sand or rice hanging from the top on a short string,
- **stiffening** it with a brace,
- **base isolation**: stand it on a smaller board resting on four marbles on the shake table.

## Record

Your natural frequency table, the resonance graph, and the result of your taming test. Two more rules for your tower design.
