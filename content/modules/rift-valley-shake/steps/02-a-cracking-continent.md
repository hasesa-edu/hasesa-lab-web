## Do: pull a continent apart (20 minutes)

1. Lay a sheet of foam or a thick layer of play dough on two flat pieces of card placed edge to edge.
2. Slowly slide the two cards apart.
3. Watch the surface. Where does it crack? Does a block drop down between the cracks?

Draw what you see. Label the **faults** (cracks) and the **valley** (the dropped block). Compare with a cross-section of the Rift Valley.

Then try two biscuits pushed together and two biscuits slid past each other. Which matches the Rift?

## Theory: earthquakes and the Rift Valley

Read **Earthquakes and the Rift Valley**. You'll learn:

- what plates are and how fast they move,
- why the African plate is splitting through Ethiopia,
- what happens at a fault during an earthquake,
- the difference between P waves, S waves and surface waves.

## Do: P and S waves (30 minutes)

Stretch a slinky along the floor between two people.

- **P wave:** push one end sharply forward and back, along the slinky. Watch a squashed section travel along it. The coils move **along** the direction of travel.
- **S wave:** flick one end quickly sideways. The coils move **across** the direction of travel.

Time each wave over the length of the slinky (repeat three times). Which is faster? Try the same with a long rope: can a rope carry a P wave?

Re-read **Two kinds of wave** in *Waves and vibrations*: which kind of wave is each one?

## Do: how far away? (15 minutes)

Use the rule from the unit: distance in km ≈ 8 × (gap between the P and S waves, in seconds).

1. A seismometer in Addis Ababa records the P wave, then the S wave 16 s later. How far away was the earthquake?
2. Stations in Adama and Debre Berhan both record a gap of 11 s. How far is the earthquake from each?
3. On a map of central Ethiopia, use the scale to draw a circle around each of the three towns at the right distance. Where do the circles cross? What's there?

## Record

Your plate model drawing, your wave timing table, and the map with circles. Explain why the S wave arrives later, and why three stations are needed to find the earthquake.
