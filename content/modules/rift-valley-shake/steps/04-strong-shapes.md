## Do: squares and triangles (25 minutes)

Using straws (or skewers) joined at the corners with a pin, a piece of clay or a twist of tape so that the joints can turn:

1. Make a **square**. Hold one side on the table and push the top sideways. What happens?
2. Make a **triangle** the same way. Push it. What happens?
3. Add **one diagonal** to the square. Push it both ways (left and right).
4. Try a diagonal made of **string** instead of a straw. Does it work for both directions? Why not?
5. Add **two string diagonals** (an X). Now?

## Do: tension and compression (20 minutes)

- Pull a strand of spaghetti from both ends. Hard to break?
- Push a strand from both ends. What happens? Now try a short piece (3 cm).
- Hang 100 g from a string and from a strand of spaghetti.

Which materials are good in tension? Which are good in compression? Which fail by **buckling**?

## Theory: structures and forces

Read **Structures and forces**. You'll learn:

- tension, compression and buckling,
- why triangles keep their shape,
- how a wide base and a low centre of mass stop things tipping,
- how earthquakes push buildings sideways, and how engineers fight back.

## Do: test frames on the shake table (20 minutes)

Build three frames the same size (about 20 cm tall, a square box shape) from spaghetti and clay:

- A: no bracing,
- B: diagonal bracing on two sides,
- C: diagonal bracing on all four sides.

Put 50 g on top of each. Shake at test level 1 for 20 seconds, then level 2. Record what happens.

## Record

Drawings of each experiment with arrows showing tension (pulling out) and compression (pushing in). Your frame test results. One rule for your tower design, backed by evidence.
