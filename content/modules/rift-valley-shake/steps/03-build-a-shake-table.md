## The design

A shake table is a board that slides back and forth on rollers, pulled back to the middle by rubber bands. You push it at a steady rhythm to make a controlled "earthquake".

## Build (45 minutes)

1. Lay the **base board** flat. Put 6–8 marbles (or round pens, or short PVC pipes) on it, spread out.
2. Lay the **top board** on the rollers. It should slide easily in one direction.
3. Screw or nail a small block to each end of the base board. Connect the top board to each block with a thick rubber band or inner-tube strip, so it's pulled back to the centre from both sides.
4. Fix a short handle to one end of the top board, for pushing.
5. Mark a ruler on the base board so you can see how far the top board moves (the **amplitude**).

Test it: push the handle and let go. Does the board slide back and forth on its own a few times?

## Theory: frequency and period

Read **Frequency and period**. You'll learn what frequency (hertz) and period (seconds) mean, how they are related, $f = 1/T$, and how to measure them with a stopwatch.

## Calibrate (30 minutes)

Your earthquakes need numbers.

1. Set a metronome to 120 beats per minute. Push the table so it goes **one full back-and-forth** for every **two** beats. What's the frequency in Hz? (Hint: 120 beats per minute is 2 beats per second.)
2. Practise shaking at 0.5 Hz, 1 Hz, 2 Hz and 3 Hz with an amplitude of 2 cm. Use the metronome to keep time.
3. If a phone has an accelerometer app, tape the phone to the table and record the shaking. Does the graph show a steady frequency?

Practise until each person can keep a steady frequency for 20 seconds. Agree a class standard: **"Test level 1"** = 1 Hz at 2 cm, **level 2** = 2 Hz at 2 cm, **level 3** = 3 Hz at 3 cm.

## Record

A labelled drawing of your table. A table of metronome settings for each frequency. The phone graph, if you have one. Why is it important that every team shakes at the same frequency and amplitude?
