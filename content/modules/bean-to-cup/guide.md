# Bean to Cup: guide's notes

For the guide only. About 8 hours, best as four or five sessions, for up to 16 students in teams of four. You do all the roasting, boiling and pouring; students measure, grind, time and calculate. If you've never roasted coffee, practise twice at home first.

---

## Before the module

### 1. The three cups for step 1 (brew on the day)

All from the same green beans:

| Cup | Roast | Grind | Brew | Expected taste |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| X | light (stopped before first crack) | coarse | 2 min steep, water off the boil | thin, sour, grassy (under-extracted) |
| Y | very dark | very fine | 15 min steep, or boiled 10 min | harsh, bitter (over-extracted) |
| Z | medium (1–2 min after first crack) | fine | jebena-style: brought to the boil, settled 3–4 min | balanced |

Use about 6 g of coffee per 100 mL for all three. Roast the beans a day ahead and keep each roast in a labelled jar.

### 2. Roasting

On a heavy pan over a steady medium heat, with constant stirring:

- 0–5 min: green to yellow, grassy smell, steam.
- 5–9 min: tan to light brown, bread/toast smell.
- About 9–12 min: **first crack** (audible popping). Beans swell.
- 12–15 min: medium to dark brown, oily patches appear on dark roasts, possibly second crack.

Times vary a lot with the heat; the colours and the crack are what matter. Stop **before** heavy smoke. Cool beans on a tray.

**Expected mass loss:** about 12–15% for a medium roast, 16–20% for a dark roast. Beans swell by roughly 50–80% in volume.

### 3. Shopping

| Item | Notes |
|---|---|
| Green beans | 1 kg is enough for everything. |
| Instant coffee | Plain, no sugar or milk. |
| Scale | 0.1 g resolution matters for the 2.0 g doses. |
| pH paper | Range 1–14 is fine; narrow-range paper (4–7) is better if you can find it. |
| Infrared thermometer | Optional but very useful for the roast. |

## Safety briefing (step 1; again at step 2)

- Only you roast, boil and pour. Students stay behind a line at least 1 m from the pan.
- **Charcoal indoors is dangerous** (carbon monoxide). Roast outside, or use an electric hot plate.
- Caffeine: tasting is optional, a few sips per session at most. Ask beforehand about anyone who should avoid caffeine.
- Indicator cups are never tasted. Keep tasting cups and test cups on separate tables.

## Session plans

### Step 1: Three cups (45 min)

Reveal the recipes at the end. Students usually blame the beans or the water first; the point is that the same beans can give all three cups.

### Step 2: Roast it (90 min)

Sample every 2 minutes as the step describes; 15 beans per sample is plenty. Speed-up puzzle: the beans' temperature keeps rising, and browning reactions speed up roughly twice for every 10 °C, so the last minutes change much faster than the first. Stirring prevents the beans touching the pan from burning (conduction) while others stay pale.

### Step 3: What dissolves? (75 min)

**Expected races:** granulated beats cubes (surface area); hot beats cold (temperature); stirred beats unstirred. 50 mL of cold water dissolves roughly 15–20 level teaspoons of sugar before saturating, which surprises students.

**Colour scale:** stock 2.0 g in 100 mL = 20 g/L. Cups A–F: 20, 10, 5, 2.5, 1.25, 0.63 g/L. Instant coffee is a slightly different colour from brewed coffee, so the scale gives an estimate, not an exact value. That's a good discussion point about the limits of a method.

**Tona/baraka:** the same grounds are brewed again; most of the soluble material has already dissolved, so less is left to extract.

### Step 4: Grind and extract (90 min)

The guide pours for all cups at once (or pours quickly and students note the actual start times). **Expected:** the fine grind reaches roughly 3–5 g/L in 2–4 minutes and then levels off; the coarse grind rises more slowly and may still be rising at 15 minutes. Both level off because a bean has only a limited amount of soluble material (roughly 20–30% of its mass).

### Step 5: Sour or bitter? (60 min)

**Expected pH:** roughly 4.8–5.1 for light roasts and 5.2–5.6 for dark, depending on the beans. Differences are small, so narrow-range paper or careful colour comparison helps. If your results don't show a difference, that's an honest result; discuss why. Heating longer breaks down some of the acids in the bean (such as chlorogenic acids), while bitter compounds build up: **bitter is not the same as sour**.

### Step 6: Your recipe (60 min)

Brew each team's recipe yourself, exactly as written. That tests whether the recipe is complete. Sign-off conversations happen during the brewing.

## Judging the sign-off items

- **roast-data:** a complete table, a mass-loss calculation, and stages explained (drying, browning, cracking).
- **colour-scale:** correct concentrations for the dilution series; a sensible estimate for a brew.
- **extraction-graph:** two labelled lines; the explanation mentions surface area.
- **acidity:** the same recipe for each roast, an honest result.
- **rate-factors:** one example of each factor from their own data (temperature: roast or dissolving race; surface area: grind or sugar cube; time: extraction; concentration: tona being weaker).
- **recipe:** every choice backed by their own evidence.
