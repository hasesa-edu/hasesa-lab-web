## The question

**How do grind size and brewing time change how much coffee dissolves?**

## Grind (15 minutes)

Using beans from the medium roast:

- **Coarse:** crush 20 g in the mortar until the pieces are about the size of teff grains or a bit bigger.
- **Fine:** grind 20 g until it feels like flour (the guide may finish it in a grinder).

Look at both under a magnifying glass. Which has more surface?

## Brew (40 minutes)

Each team does one grind; pair up with a team doing the other.

1. Put 2.0 g of grounds in each of five cups.
2. The guide pours 100 mL of just-boiled water into all five at the same moment. Start the timer.
3. Strain one cup after **1 minute**, one after **2**, **4**, **8** and **15** minutes (pour through a strainer or filter into a clean cup).
4. Let them cool for a few minutes.

## Measure strength (20 minutes)

Some brews may be darker than your strongest standard. If so, dilute them: mix 10 mL of brew with 10 mL of water (and double your answer afterwards).

Compare each brew with your colour scale from step 3 against white paper, in the same light. Which standard does it match best? Record its concentration.

## Notice something puzzling

The fine grind probably got strong fast and then stopped getting much stronger. Why would it level off? Is there a limit to how much can come out of 2 g of coffee?

## Theory

Re-read **surface area** in *Rates of reaction* and **extraction** in *Solutions and concentration*.

## Record (15 minutes)

1. A table: time, colour-scale concentration, for both grinds.
2. A graph of strength (y) against time (x), with a line for each grind.
3. Calculate the starting rate for each (the gradient over the first 2 minutes).
4. Explain the difference between the lines using *surface area* and *collisions*.

Coffee in a jebena is ground very fine and boiled. Using your graph, why does it only need a few minutes?
