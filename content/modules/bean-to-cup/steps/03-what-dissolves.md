## Do: dissolving races (20 minutes)

Time how long each takes to disappear completely in 100 mL of water, stirring the same way:

| Race | Compare |
|---|---|
| 1 | a sugar cube vs a teaspoon of granulated sugar (cold water) |
| 2 | a teaspoon of granulated sugar in cold water vs in hot water (guide pours) |
| 3 | a teaspoon of sugar, stirred vs not stirred |

Then keep adding sugar, one level teaspoon at a time, to 50 mL of cold water, stirring for a minute each time. When does it stop dissolving? That solution is **saturated**.

## Theory: solutions and concentration

Read **Solutions and concentration**. You'll learn:

- solute, solvent, solution and saturated,
- how to calculate concentration in g/L and %,
- the dilution formula $C_1V_1 = C_2V_2$,
- how to make a dilution series and use it as a colour scale.

## Build: a coffee colour scale (35 minutes)

Coffee is dark, and darker means stronger. You can use that to measure strength.

1. **Stock solution:** dissolve exactly 2.0 g of instant coffee in hot water and make it up to exactly 100 mL. What's its concentration in g/L?
2. **Dilute:** put 20 mL of stock in cup A. Mix 20 mL of stock with 20 mL of water in cup B. Take 20 mL of cup B and mix with 20 mL of water in cup C. Keep going to cup F.
3. Calculate the concentration of each cup.
4. Line them up on white paper in good light, and photograph them. Label each with its concentration.

Keep the photo: in the next step you'll compare your brews with it.

## Record

Your race times, with one sentence for each race explaining the result. Your colour scale photo with concentrations. Answer: why is the second pour of the coffee ceremony (tona) weaker than the first (abol)?
