## Before the roast (15 minutes)

1. Count out exactly 50 green beans. Weigh them. Measure the length of five beans and take the mean.
2. Look at a green bean closely: colour, smell, hardness. Try to crush one in the mortar.
3. Weigh 200 g of green beans for the guide to roast.

## Watch the roast (40 minutes, from behind the line)

The guide roasts the 200 g of beans on the pan, stirring all the time. Every **2 minutes**, the guide scoops out about 15 beans onto a labelled white saucer. Your team's jobs:

- **Timer:** call out every 2 minutes.
- **Recorder:** colour, smell and sound at each sample.
- **Listener:** note the exact time of **first crack** (popping, like popcorn) and, if it happens, second crack (quieter crackling).
- **Thermometer:** if there's an infrared thermometer, the guide reads the bean temperature at each sample.

Keep going until the beans are very dark (the guide decides when to stop). Lay the samples out in a row: you now have a **roast series**.

## Notice something puzzling

For the first few minutes, not much happens. Then the colour changes faster and faster, and the beans can go from good to burnt in under a minute. Why would the change speed up?

## Theory: rates of reaction

Read **Rates of reaction**. You'll learn:

- how to measure a rate,
- collision theory and activation energy,
- the four factors: temperature, concentration, surface area and catalysts,
- what happens inside a coffee bean at each stage of roasting.

Then re-read the **conduction** part of *Heat transfer*: why must the beans be stirred all the time?

## After the roast (35 minutes)

1. When the beans are cool, count out 50 from a medium roast (just after first crack) and weigh them. Calculate the **percentage mass loss**:
   $$\text{mass loss \%} = \frac{\text{mass before} - \text{mass after}}{\text{mass before}} \times 100$$
2. Do the same for a dark roast.
3. Measure the length of five roasted beans. Did they swell?
4. Try crushing a roasted bean. Compare with a green one.

## Record

A roast table: time, colour, smell, sound, temperature. Mass loss % for medium and dark. One paragraph: what happens to the bean, and where did the lost mass go?
