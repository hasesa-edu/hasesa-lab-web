## The question

Does the roast change how acidic coffee is?

## Do (30 minutes)

Choose three samples from your roast series: **light** (just before first crack), **medium** (just after) and **dark**.

1. Grind 2.0 g of each to the **same** fineness.
2. The guide pours 100 mL of just-boiled water onto each. Brew for exactly 4 minutes, then strain.
3. Let them cool to room temperature (temperature changes pH readings a little).
4. Dip a strip of pH paper into each, or add 5 mL of each to 5 mL of red cabbage indicator. Compare with the colour chart.

Also test: plain water, a few drops of lemon juice in water, and black tea, for comparison.

## Optional taste panel (10 minutes)

Using cups brewed for tasting (no indicator), volunteers take one small sip of each roast, labelled only 1, 2, 3, and score **sourness** and **bitterness** from 1 to 5.

## Theory

Read **Acids, bases and pH**. Then answer: coffee has a pH of about 5. How many times more acidic than pure water (pH 7) is that?

## Notice something puzzling

A dark roast may taste stronger and harsher, but measure slightly **less** acidic. How can a cup taste more "biting" and be less acidic? (Hint: is bitter the same as sour?)

## Record

A table of pH for each roast and the comparisons. If your class did the taste panel, add the mean scores. One sentence: how does roasting change acidity, and why might that be? (Think about what happens to molecules when they're heated for longer.)
