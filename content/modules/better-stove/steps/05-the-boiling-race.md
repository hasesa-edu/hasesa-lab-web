## Why side by side?

Wind, the dryness of the wood and even the starting temperature of the water change how a fire behaves. If you test your stove today and the three-stone fire last week, you can't tell whether a difference came from the stove or the weather. So today both fires run **at the same time, in the same place, with wood from the same bundle**.

## Plan (10 minutes)

Write down, before you start:

- What stays the same for both fires? (List at least five things: think water, pot, wood, starting temperature, where they stand, how they are fed.)
- What will you measure, and how often?
- Who does each job? You need a fuel person, a temperature person, a timer and a recorder for each fire. Your guide runs the three-stone fire with one team helping.

## Do: the race (45 minutes)

Follow the same Water Boiling Test as in step 3, for both fires at once:

1. Weigh a bundle of about 400 g of sticks (plus kindling) for each fire. Same thickness, same bundle of wood.
2. Weigh 1.000 kg of water into each identical pot. Record both starting temperatures.
3. Pot skirt **on** your stove. Your guide lights both fires together. Start the stopwatch.
4. Record each pot's temperature every 2 minutes.
5. Keep each fire burning steadily. On your stove, push sticks in as their tips burn away; don't stuff the feed tube full.
6. **Soot cards.** At 6 minutes, hold a clean white cloth square on a long stick 50 cm above each pot for exactly 60 seconds. Both at the same time, both at the same height. Stand upwind.
7. When each pot reaches its boiling point, record the time. Your guide puts out that fire. Weigh the unburnt wood and the charcoal left, as in step 3.

## Calculate (25 minutes)

For **each** fire:

1. Wood burnt, $m_{\text{wood}}$, in kg.
2. Energy into the water, $Q = mc\Delta T$.
3. Energy in the wood burnt, $E_{\text{fuel}} = m_{\text{wood}} \times 16{,}000{,}000$ J.
4. Efficiency $= Q \div E_{\text{fuel}} \times 100\%$.
5. **Firepower**: how fast the fire released energy, $P = E_{\text{fuel}} \div t$, in watts ($t$ in seconds).

Then compare:

6. **Wood saved** by your stove: $\dfrac{m_{\text{three-stone}} - m_{\text{stove}}}{m_{\text{three-stone}}} \times 100\%$.
7. How many times more efficient is your stove?

## Graph (15 minutes)

Plot both fires on **one** temperature–time graph, with a key. Draw a smooth curve through each set of points.

- Which curve is steeper at the start? What does the gradient tell you?
- Why do both curves flatten out at the same temperature, however long the fire keeps burning?

## Compare the soot cards (5 minutes)

Lay the two cards side by side on white paper. Give each a soot score from 0 (clean) to 5 (black). Use the words *complete combustion*, *incomplete combustion* and *insulation* to explain the difference.

## Record

Your table, all calculations with units, your graph and both labelled soot cards. Compare with your guesses from step 1. Did your stove beat three stones? By how much?
