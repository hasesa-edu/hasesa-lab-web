## The question

A fire is a chemical reaction. What goes into it, and what comes out? And why does one fire make thick smoke while another burns almost clean?

## Do: starve a candle (25 minutes)

Your guide lights four candles of the same size on a metal tray. Your team has glass jars of different sizes.

1. Measure the volume of each jar: fill it with water from the measuring jug, then empty and dry it.
2. Put a jar over a burning candle and start the stopwatch. Stop it when the flame goes out.
3. Lift the jar off with a gloved hand (the glass is warm). Your guide relights the candle. Repeat each jar three times.
4. Look at the inside of the jar straight after the flame goes out. What do you see on the cold glass?

|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | |

Draw a graph of **mean time** (y-axis) against **jar volume** (x-axis). Is it close to a straight line through the origin? What does that tell you about what the candle was using up?

## Do: catch the soot (10 minutes)

Your guide holds a cold steel spoon with tongs in the yellow tip of a candle flame for five seconds, then in the blue base of the flame. Look at the back of the spoon each time.

Where did the black come from? Why only from the yellow part?

## Theory: combustion and fuels

Read **Combustion and fuels**. You'll learn:

- the **fire triangle**: fuel, oxygen and heat,
- **complete combustion**, which makes carbon dioxide and water, and the balanced equation for burning methane (the gas in biogas): $\text{CH}_4 + 2\text{O}_2 \rightarrow \text{CO}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$,
- **incomplete combustion**, which makes soot, smoke and **carbon monoxide**,
- why carbon monoxide is deadly, and why charcoal must never burn in a closed room,
- the energy content of wood, charcoal, dung and biogas.

## Think it through (20 minutes)

Answer in your notebook:

1. The water droplets on the jar and the soot on the spoon are both products of the candle burning. Which comes from the hydrogen in the wax, and which from the carbon?
2. For each side of the fire triangle, give one way a cook in Ethiopia puts out or controls a fire.
3. In the boiling race, which fire looked more like the yellow tip of the candle, and which more like the blue base? Use the words *complete*, *incomplete* and *oxygen* in your answer.
4. Many families in Addis bring a charcoal stove indoors for the coffee ceremony or to warm the house at night. Using what you have read, write three sentences you would say to them.

## Calculate (15 minutes)

Use the energy contents in the unit:

1. How much energy is in a 2 kg bundle of eucalyptus sticks?
2. How much is in 2 kg of charcoal?
3. A family's biogas stove burns 0.5 m³ of biogas holding 22 MJ/m³. Which holds more energy: that, or 1 kg of wood?
