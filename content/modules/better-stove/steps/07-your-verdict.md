## Final demonstration (25 minutes)

Each team runs its stove, with its best setting, one last time: 1 kg of water to the boil, wood weighed before and after. Other teams time it and hold the soot card. Your guide runs one three-stone fire alongside for the class. Record every team's results on a big board:

| Team | Time to boil (min) | Wood burnt (g) | Efficiency (%) | Soot score (0–5) |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Which stove was best? Was it best at everything, or did one boil fastest while another used least wood?

## Back to the race (10 minutes)

Open the page titled *The race* from step 1. Next to each guess, write ✓ or ✗ and what you now know. How close were your guesses for the efficiencies?

Answer the big question properly, in your own words: **why does a tin-can stove boil water on less wood and with less smoke than three stones?** Use these words: *insulation, conduction, convection, draught, oxygen, complete combustion, pot skirt*.

## Scale it up (15 minutes)

A family that cooks on three stones might burn about 6 kg of wood a day. Use **your own** "wood saved" percentage from step 5:

1. How many kilograms of wood a day would your stove save?
2. How many kilograms a year?
3. How much energy is that, in MJ?
4. If wood costs the family money, what would they save? (Ask at home or at the market what a bundle costs and how much it weighs.)

## Smoke and health (10 minutes)

Look at the soot cards from steps 3, 5 and today. Discuss as a class:

- The soot on the card is only the smoke that hit 100 cm² of cloth for one minute. Where does the rest go in a kitchen with no chimney?
- Who in a family breathes the most cooking smoke? Why?
- Your stove made less smoke, but it still made some, and it still makes carbon monoxide. What rule must the family follow even with a better stove?
- What would cut the smoke even more: a chimney, a cleaner fuel like biogas, or cooking outside? What are the problems with each?

## Write your verdict (25 minutes)

Write a one-page recommendation to a real family you know who cooks with wood, charcoal or dung. It must include:

1. **What you tested**, in two sentences.
2. **Your numbers:** time to boil, wood used, efficiency and soot score, for three stones and for your stove.
3. **Your advice:** should they switch to an improved stove? Which design feature matters most, from your fair test?
4. **Safety:** the carbon monoxide rule, in words they'll remember.
5. **One limit of your test:** for example, you boiled water but they bake injera; you tested a few runs on one day; your tin stove will rust and burn through in time.

Your guide will ask you to explain your verdict and your stove. Be ready to show your graph, your calculations and your fair test.

Take your stove home only if your family agrees to use it **outdoors**. It's yours.
