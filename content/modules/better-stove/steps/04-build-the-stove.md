## How a rocket stove works (15 minutes)

Look at your guide's stove from step 1, cold, and the drawing on the board. A rocket stove has four parts:

- **A feed tube.** Sticks go in sideways, so only their tips burn. Push them in as they burn down. Less wood burns at once, so the fire gets the air it needs.
- **A fuel shelf** across the feed tube. Sticks rest on it and air flows in **underneath** them, straight to the flame.
- **A chimney** (the upright can) above the fire. Hot gases rise up it fast (convection), pulling fresh air in through the feed tube. This draught is what makes the stove roar.
- **Insulation** (ash) all round the tube. It stops heat being conducted away through the walls, so the inside stays very hot and the smoky gases burn completely.

On top, **pot supports** leave a small gap under the pot, and a **pot skirt** (a ring of tin round the pot) forces the hot gases to scrape up its sides.

Using **Heat transfer**, label each part on your own sketch with the type of heat transfer it controls: conduction, convection or radiation.

## Safety check (5 minutes)

Gloves and glasses on for all cutting, punching and filing. Snips and nails only on the work table. Every cut edge is filed or folded before you pass the can to anyone. Your guide has already burnt out the inside of each can and tin, so there is no plastic lining or paint left to smoke.

## Build (80 minutes)

You need: one 4–5 L oil tin (the **body**), two food cans of the same diameter (the **chimney** and the **feed tube**), a third can for spare tin, ash, bolts or wire for the pot supports.

1. **Feed tube.** Cut both ends off one food can, so it's an open tube. File the edges.
2. **Chimney.** Cut the top off the second can (keep its bottom on). Near the bottom of its side, draw round the end of the feed tube. Cut inside the line, making a hole a little smaller than the tube. Snip small tabs round the hole and bend them **inwards**. Push the feed tube onto the tabs so the two cans make an **L**. Squeeze the tabs tight with pliers.
3. **Body.** Cut the top off the oil tin, leaving a smooth folded or filed rim. Near the bottom of its side, cut a hole just big enough for the feed tube to poke through.
4. **Assemble.** Lower the L into the body so the feed tube sticks out of the side hole by about 3 cm. The top of the chimney should be about 2 cm below the rim of the body. If the chimney is too short, add a third can (both ends cut off) on top, joined with tabs.
5. **Insulate.** Pour dry, sifted ash into the body all round the chimney and feed tube, tapping the tin so it settles, up to the top of the chimney. Don't let any ash fall inside the tube.
6. **Seal the top.** Cover the ash with a 2 cm layer of damp clay soil, smoothed down to the rim, leaving the chimney open. Let it dry.
7. **Fuel shelf.** Cut a strip of tin as wide as the feed tube and about 15 cm long. Slide it into the feed tube about one-third of the way up, so the sticks sit on top and air can flow underneath. Its back end should stop where the feed tube meets the chimney.
8. **Pot supports.** Push three bolts or bent pieces of thick wire through the clay, evenly spaced round the chimney, so the pot rests about 1.5–2 cm above the chimney's top. Check the pot sits level and steady.
9. **Pot skirt.** Wrap a strip of tin, about 10 cm tall, round your pot so it leaves a gap of about 1 cm all round. Fold the ends together or join them with wire. The skirt should stand on the pot supports, not touch the pot, and lift off easily with tongs.

## Test fire (15 minutes)

Your guide lights a small handful of kindling in the feed tube of each stove. Feed in three or four thin sticks, tips first. Watch:

- Does the fire draw air in strongly through the feed tube?
- Do flames come out of the top of the chimney?
- Is there more or less smoke than the three-stone fire?

If it smokes a lot or burns weakly, check the gap under the pot, the space under the fuel shelf, and that the feed tube isn't blocked. Your guide puts every fire out at the end.

## Record (5 minutes)

A labelled sketch of your stove, with measurements: chimney diameter and height, feed tube length, gap under the pot, skirt gap, and the stove's empty mass. Note how the test fire went and one thing you'd fix.
