## The fire rules first (10 minutes)

Before any fire is lit, your guide reads out the fire rules. Copy them into the front of your notebook:

1. Fires only outdoors. Never a stove, charcoal or embers inside a room.
2. Only the guide lights a fire.
3. Water bucket and sand bucket next to every fire, always.
4. Hair tied back, scarves and netela off or tucked in.
5. Gloves on to touch a stove, a pot or anything near the fire. Everything is hot until the guide says it isn't.
6. Stand upwind. Never breathe or sniff smoke.

## Watch the race (20 minutes)

Your guide has two identical pots, each with exactly 1 litre of water, and two fires on bare ground:

- a **three-stone fire** (*gulicha*, ጉልቻ), the way many Ethiopian families cook,
- a **tin-can stove** the guide made from an old oil tin, two food cans and a bucket of ash.

Before lighting, your guide weighs two identical bundles of sticks, one for each fire. Then both fires are lit at the same moment and fed from their own bundle.

While they burn, take turns (wearing gloves, from the side) with the thermometers and the stopwatch. Every 2 minutes, record the temperature of each pot in a table:

| Time (min) | Three-stone fire (°C) | Tin-can stove (°C) |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | | |
| 2 | | |
| … | | |

Your guide also holds a square of white cloth on a long stick in the smoke above each pot for one minute. Compare the two squares.

When each pot boils, write down the time. Afterwards your guide weighs what is left of each bundle.

## Predict (10 minutes)

On a page titled *The race*, write:

1. Which fire won? By how many minutes? How much wood did each use?
2. **Why?** Write your best guess, then a second one. Think about where the heat from each fire goes.
3. Why was one cloth darker than the other? What is the black stuff?
4. **Guess:** out of all the energy in the wood, what percentage do you think ended up in the water on the three-stone fire? On the tin stove? Write two numbers. You'll find out in steps 3 and 5.

## Questions (5 minutes)

As a team, list every question you have. For example:

- Why is the ash packed around the inside can?
- Where does smoke come from, and why does one fire make more?
- How can we measure the energy in a stick?
- Could the stove be even better?
