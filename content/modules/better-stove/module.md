Most Ethiopian families still cook over wood, charcoal or dried dung, many on three stones. That fire eats a lot of wood, and its smoke fills kitchens and lungs. Improved stoves such as the *Mirt* injera stove and the *Lakech* charcoal stove do the same job on less fuel. In ten hours you find out why, build your own improved stove from tin cans and ash, and prove with your own numbers how much better it is.

1. **Three stones and a tin** (45 min). Watch a boiling race between a three-stone fire and a tin-can stove, and learn the fire rules.
2. **What a fire needs** (80 min). Candles, jars and a cold spoon: the fire triangle, complete and incomplete combustion, and where smoke and carbon monoxide come from.
3. **Counting the energy** (80 min). $Q = mc\Delta T$ and fuel energy. Run a Water Boiling Test on a three-stone fire and work out its efficiency.
4. **Build the stove** (120 min). Cut, file and assemble an L-shaped fire tube inside an oil tin, insulate it with ash, and add pot supports and a pot skirt.
5. **The boiling race** (100 min). Your stove against three stones, side by side: time, fuel, temperature and soot.
6. **Make it better** (90 min). Change one thing (insulation, skirt or gap) and fair-test it.
7. **Smoke, health and your verdict** (85 min). Put your results together, explain them, and write a recommendation for a family.

By the end you'll understand combustion, heat transfer, specific heat capacity, fuel energy and efficiency, and you'll have a stove that boils water on far less wood than an open fire.
