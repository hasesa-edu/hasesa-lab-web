## Do: become a nerve

The whole class stands in a line, holding hands. Each person is one **neuron**.

1. Measure the length of the line with a metre stick or tape (roughly: count people and measure one arm-span gap).
2. Everyone closes their eyes. The guide squeezes the first person's hand and starts a stopwatch.
3. When you feel a squeeze, squeeze the next hand. The last person shouts "Now!" and the guide stops the clock.
4. Repeat 3 times. Record the times.

Then try two variations:

- **Shoulder taps** instead of hand squeezes.
- **Dominoes:** set up a line of dominoes (or standing books, or folded cards) about 2 cm apart along a table. Time how long it takes for the line to fall, and measure its length.

## Notice

Calculate, in your notebook:

- The **speed** of the signal: $v = \dfrac{\text{length of line}}{\text{time}}$
- The **delay per person**: total time ÷ number of people. Compare this with your reaction time from the ruler drop. Surprised?
- The speed of the dominoes.

Puzzle: the squeeze was not *pushed* down the line like water in a pipe. Every person made a **new** squeeze. Is a real nerve like that too? And why did each "neuron" need so long?

## Just enough theory

Read *Neurons and nerve signals*. Look for:

- The parts of a neuron: dendrites, cell body, axon, myelin.
- The **resting potential** of about −70 mV: each neuron is a tiny battery.
- The **action potential**: sodium rushes in, potassium flows out, and the spike is **re-made** at every point along the axon — like your squeeze being re-made by each person.
- **All-or-nothing:** a spike is always the same size. A stronger command means **more spikes, more often**.
- Real speeds: **50–120 m/s** in myelinated motor neurons.

## Do it better: model a real neuron

Improve your class model so it behaves more like an axon:

- **Myelin model:** only every third person squeezes; the others just pass it along instantly by holding a stick or rope between them. Does it go faster? This is like the signal jumping between gaps in the myelin.
- **Refractory period:** after you squeeze, you must count "one-thousand" before you can squeeze again. What happens if the guide sends two squeezes very close together?
- **Stronger command:** how could your chain show a "strong clench" if every squeeze must be the same strength?

## Think

1. How many times faster is a real motor neuron (say 80 m/s) than your class chain?
2. A motor neuron runs about 1 m from your spinal cord to your foot. At 60 m/s, how long does the signal take?
3. Draw a neuron and label where the signal comes in, where it goes out, and where the myelin is.
4. Back to the claw: if the brain's command is just a pattern of spikes in a nerve inside your arm, how could a sticker on the **skin** pick it up? Write a guess — we test it in step 5.
