## Watch

Your guide has a sticker on their forearm, a few wires, a small blue board — and a cardboard claw on the table. They hold their arm still. Then they **clench their fist**.

The claw snaps shut.

They relax. The claw opens. Clench. Shut. Relax. Open.

Nobody touched the claw. The wires go to the guide's **arm**, not their hand, and not their head.

## Predict

Before anyone explains anything, write in your notebook:

1. **How does the machine know?** Write your best guess. There are no wrong guesses yet.
2. **Is it reading the guide's thoughts?** Why or why not?
3. Why are the stickers on the **forearm**, when the fist is the thing that moves?
4. Draw a quick picture of what you think happens, from "I decide to clench" to "the claw closes". Use arrows. How many steps does your picture have?

## Try it on yourself

You do not need electronics to start investigating.

- Rest your left hand on your right forearm, palm side. Slowly open and close your right fist. **What do you feel under your fingers?**
- Wiggle only your fingers. Where does the movement come from?
- Clench gently, then hard. Does anything change in your forearm?

## Talk

In your group, share your pictures. Where do they agree? Where do they disagree?

Then the whole class builds one big list of **questions** on the board, for example:

- How does a thought travel to the muscle? How fast?
- Is there electricity in my body?
- Why do the fingers move when the muscle is in the arm?
- How did the machine turn my muscle into a motor turning?

Copy the list into your notebook. Over the next steps you will answer every one — and by the end you will build your own claw.
