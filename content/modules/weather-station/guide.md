# Weather Station: guide's notes

For the guide only. About 12 hours of sessions, spread over **two to three weeks** because the station needs at least seven days of readings, and ideally more. Up to 16 students in four teams. Any season works, but the change from bega to belg, or the start of kiremt, gives the most interesting data. In mid-bega you may get no rain at all; that's fine, and pressure, temperature and humidity still vary.

Suggested calendar:

| Week | Sessions | Steps |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2 sessions | 1–2, then 3 |
| 1–2 | 1 long session | 4 (instruments up by the end of week 1) |
| 2 | daily 10-minute readings, 1 session at the end of the week | 5 |
| 3 | daily readings, 2 sessions | 6, then 7 |

---

## Before the module

### 1. The site

Find a spot inside the school grounds that's open, safe and can't be disturbed by football or animals: away from walls and trees, no power lines. Ask a carpenter for a simple post and a **ventilated box** for the thermometers (slatted sides, painted white, open at the bottom, about 1.2–1.5 m above the ground). A white box with many large holes, shaded from the sun, is an acceptable substitute.

### 2. Find a phone barometer

Many Android phones contain a pressure sensor. Install a free barometer app on your phone or a student's (with permission) and check it reads about 770 hPa in Addis. It is your calibration reference for the jar barometers. If no phone has one, use the National Meteorology Agency's published readings for the nearest station, and note the difference in altitude.

### 3. Shopping

| Item | Notes |
|---|---|
| Thermometers | At least two identical ones per hygrometer, plus spares. Alcohol (red/blue) thermometers or digital ones; **no mercury**. |
| Bottles and funnels | One gauge per team, so they can compare readings. |
| Jars and balloons | Several barometers, because some will leak. |
| Cups and sticks | Light paper cups spin best. |

### 4. Humidity table

Give students this table (approximate values for a wet-and-dry-bulb hygrometer; read the dry-bulb temperature and the difference between the two thermometers):

| Dry bulb | diff 1 °C | 2 °C | 3 °C | 4 °C | 5 °C | 6 °C | 7 °C | 8 °C |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15 °C | 90% | 80% | 71% | 61% | 53% | 44% | 36% | 27% |
| 20 °C | 91% | 83% | 74% | 66% | 59% | 51% | 44% | 37% |
| 25 °C | 92% | 84% | 76% | 69% | 62% | 55% | 48% | 42% |

These are sea-level values. At Addis's altitude, the true humidity is a few percent higher for the same difference; for this module, the table is accurate enough.

## Safety briefing (step 1; again at step 4)

- Station on open ground, away from power lines. No roofs.
- No readings in thunderstorms.
- You alone do the crushing-can demo.
- Car calibration: you hold the anemometer, another adult drives slowly on a quiet road, students stay seated inside and never lean out.

---

## Session plans

### Step 1: Can we predict it? (45 min)

Collect the traditional signs students bring from home; they make a good discussion in step 6 (some, like wind changes and cloud types, have a real basis). Start the forecast scoreboard on the board.

### Step 2: The rain gauge (75 min)

Common errors: measuring the funnel radius as the diameter; forgetting to convert cm to mm. **Check:** a 10 cm funnel (78.5 cm²): 100 mL → 1.27 cm = 12.7 mm.

### Step 3: Air has weight (90 min)

**Can demo:** about 1 cm of water in the can, heated until steam flows strongly for 30 seconds. Invert it quickly, mouth-down, into cold water. The steam condenses, the pressure inside drops, and the outside air crushes the can. Practise beforehand.

**Force on a hand:** about 150 cm² = 0.015 m², so F = 77,000 × 0.015 ≈ 1,150 N. It doesn't crush your hand because the pressure inside your body pushes outwards equally, and air presses on all sides.

**Jar barometers** are very sensitive to temperature: warm air inside expands and pushes the balloon up, looking like a pressure fall. Keep them indoors in a shaded spot that stays at a similar temperature, and read them at the same time each day. Expect day-to-day changes of only a few hPa. Many jar barometers will be noisy; this is a good discussion about reliability and why the phone reference matters.

### Step 4: Wind and wet air (120 min)

May need two sessions. The calibration drive can be done once by you with one team, and the results shared. Typical home-made cup anemometers turn at roughly 1–3 turns per second in a moderate breeze. The hygrometer must be fanned before reading, or the wet bulb reads too high.

### Step 5: A week of data (150 min)

The hardest part is consistency. Check the log daily for the first few days, and praise honest "missed" entries. A spreadsheet makes the graphs much easier if a computer is available.

### Step 6: The forecast (90 min)

Over a short period, students' rules rarely beat persistence by much. That is a **valid and important result**. Celebrate honest scoring; question any scoreboard that's suspiciously perfect.

### Step 7: The weather report (90 min)

Run the reports and sign-off conversations. If possible, keep one team responsible for the station after the module ends.

## Judging the sign-off items

- **instruments:** each works well enough to give readings that change sensibly.
- **calibration:** compares with a reference and explains that without it the numbers have no real units.
- **data-log:** five days at the agreed time, honest gaps.
- **rain-calc:** volume ÷ area, unit conversion correct.
- **graphs:** same time axis, units, a pattern described with evidence.
- **forecast-score:** forecasts written **before** the outcome; persistence compared; an honest verdict.
