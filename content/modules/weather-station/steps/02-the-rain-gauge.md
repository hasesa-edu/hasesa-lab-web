## Check yesterday's forecast (5 minutes)

Who was right: you, the app or "same as today"? Start a scoreboard in your notebook.

## Build (30 minutes)

1. Cut the top third off a 2 L bottle (the guide starts the cut). Turn the top upside down and push it into the bottom part to make a funnel. Tape the edges.
2. Put a few stones in the bottom so it doesn't blow over.
3. Measure the **diameter** of the funnel's opening carefully, in cm, across the widest point.

## Why measure depth, not volume?

A big funnel catches more water than a small one in the same rain. So "200 mL of rain" means nothing unless you know the funnel. Meteorologists report rain as a **depth**, which is the same whatever the funnel size.

## Theory

Re-read the **rain** section of *Air and weather*. Then read **Rearranging formulas**, so you can work backwards too: if you know the depth, what volume will you collect?

## Calculate (20 minutes)

1. Area of your funnel: $A = \pi r^2$ (in cm²).
2. If you collect $V$ mL (= cm³), the depth is $V \div A$ in cm. Multiply by 10 for mm.
3. **Calibration check:** pour exactly 100 mL of water into your funnel from the jug. Does all of it come out the bottom? Calculate what rainfall 100 mL represents in your gauge.
4. Make a quick-read table: for 10, 20, 50, 100, 200 mL, what's the rainfall in mm?

## Place it (20 minutes)

Choose a spot as a class: open ground, away from walls, trees and roofs (at least twice the height of any obstacle away), with the funnel about 30 cm above the ground. Fix it so it can't fall over.

## Record

A labelled drawing with the funnel diameter and area, your conversion table and the calibration check. The rule for readings: same time every day, pour into the jug, record mL and mm, empty, replace.
