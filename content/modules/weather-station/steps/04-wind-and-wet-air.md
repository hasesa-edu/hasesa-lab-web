Each team builds one of three instruments, then teaches another team how it works.

## Anemometer (wind speed)

1. Cross two sticks at their middles and tape them into a plus sign.
2. Tape a cup to the end of each arm, all facing the same way round. Colour one cup so you can count its turns.
3. Push a pin through the centre into the eraser of a pencil, with a bead between, so it spins freely.
4. Measure the radius $r$ from the centre to the middle of a cup.

**Reading:** count the turns of the coloured cup in 30 seconds. Work out the **frequency** (turns per second) and the cup speed, $2\pi r \times f$.

**Calibrate:** on a calm day, the guide holds the anemometer out of the window of a car driven by another adult at a steady, known speed on a quiet road (10, 20 and 30 km/h, read from the speedometer), while you count the turns from inside. Nobody leans out of a vehicle. (Convert: 10 km/h ≈ 2.8 m/s.) Plot turns per second against speed: that graph converts your counts into wind speed.

## Wind vane (wind direction)

1. Cut an arrowhead and a bigger tail from card. Slot them into the ends of a straw.
2. Push a pin through the straw at its balance point into a pencil eraser, so it swings freely.
3. Mark N, E, S and W on a board under it, lined up with a compass.

The arrow points to where the wind comes **from**.

## Hygrometer (humidity)

1. Fix two identical thermometers side by side on a board.
2. Wrap the bulb of one in a strip of cotton cloth, with the other end of the strip dipping into a small bottle of water so it stays wet.
3. Fan both gently for one minute before each reading. Read both.

Look up the relative humidity in the table your guide gives you, using the dry reading and the **difference**.

## Theory

Read the **wind** and **humidity** sections of *Air and weather*, and **Frequency and period** for turns per second.

## Teach (20 minutes)

Each team explains its instrument to another team: how to read it, and what it measures. Everyone should be able to take every reading.

## Record

A labelled drawing of your instrument, its readings today, and (for the anemometer) the calibration graph. Put all instruments on the station stand; thermometers go inside the white ventilated box, out of direct sun.
