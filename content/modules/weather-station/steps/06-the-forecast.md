## Make a forecasting rule (30 minutes)

Using the patterns you found, write a simple **rule** your team will use to forecast. For example:

> If the pressure has fallen by more than 2 hPa since yesterday **and** the humidity is above 70%, forecast rain in the next 24 hours. Otherwise, forecast no rain.

Your rule must use **your** measurements, not the phone app, and must be clear enough that someone else could follow it and get the same forecast.

## Forecast and keep score (over the next days)

Keep taking readings. Every day, before checking the sky, write three forecasts for the next 24 hours:

| Day | Your rule says | Persistence (same as today) | Phone app | What happened |
|---|---|---|---|---|

At the end, count how many each method got right. Do at least four days, more if you can.

## Theory

Re-read **From data to forecast** in *Air and weather*. Why is persistence a fair thing to compare against? Why isn't one lucky day enough to show your rule is good?

## Notice something puzzling

Your rule might get the forecast right for the wrong reason, or wrong for reasons your instruments can't see (a storm system 100 km away). What would you need to measure to forecast better? Where would you put other weather stations?

## Record (during the session: 60 minutes)

Write up your rule, the reasoning behind it from your data, and the scoreboard so far. Graph your instruments' latest readings and add the new days to the time graphs from step 5.
