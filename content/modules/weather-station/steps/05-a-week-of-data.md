## The rules (20 minutes, first day)

Data is only useful if everyone collects it the same way. As a class, agree and write on the log:

- **When:** the same time every day (for example 08:30), plus a second reading at 14:00 if possible.
- **Who:** a rota, two students per reading.
- **What:** in this order: rain (mL and mm), pressure (straw mark and hPa), dry and wet temperature, humidity, wind direction, wind speed (turns in 30 s, converted), cloud cover (eighths of the sky covered), and a short note ("drizzle overnight", "dust").
- **How:** the same method every time. If something goes wrong, write it down, don't guess.

Read **Fair tests and averages** again: which ideas about controlling variables apply to collecting weather data?

## Daily readings (about 10 minutes a day, for at least 7 days)

Follow the rota. Fill in the class log with units. If a reading is missed, write "missed", not a made-up number.

## Graph (60 minutes, at the end of the week)

Read **Graphs and relationships**, then draw, **one above the other with the same time axis**:

1. Pressure (hPa)
2. Rainfall (mm per day, as bars)
3. Dry temperature (°C)
4. Relative humidity (%)
5. Cloud cover (eighths)

Lining the graphs up by date lets you see whether changes happen together.

## Look for patterns (40 minutes)

Talk in your team and write down:

- Did rain days have anything in common? What was the pressure doing the day before? The humidity? The cloud cover?
- Is there a daily pattern (morning vs afternoon)?
- Which instrument was least reliable? How do you know?
- Compare your week with the season. Is this a typical bega, belg or kiremt week?

## Record

Your five graphs and at least two possible patterns, each with the evidence from the data. Be careful: one week is a very small sample. How confident can you be?
