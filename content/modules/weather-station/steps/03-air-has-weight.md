## Watch: the crushing can (10 minutes, from a safe distance)

The guide puts a little water in an empty drink can and heats it on the hot plate until steam pours out. Then, with tongs, the guide quickly turns it upside down into a bowl of cold water.

What happens? Write it down, then write your best explanation.

## Notice something puzzling

Nobody touched the can. Something crushed it from the outside. What? And why didn't that happen before it was heated?

## Theory: pressure

Read **Pressure**. You'll learn:

- what pressure is, $P = F/A$, and its units,
- why the air around you presses on everything,
- why air pressure in Addis is much lower than at the coast,
- how a trapped gas's pressure changes when it's squeezed or heated.

Now explain the can again. (Hint: what happened to the steam inside when it hit the cold water?)

## Do: how big is that force? (15 minutes)

Air pressure in Addis is about 77,000 Pa. Calculate the force of the air on:

- the palm of your hand (measure its area in cm², then convert to m²),
- the top of your desk.

Why doesn't the air crush your hand?

## Build: a jar barometer (40 minutes)

1. Stretch a piece of balloon tightly over the mouth of a dry glass jar. Seal the edge with a rubber band and tape: it must be airtight.
2. Glue the end of a drinking straw to the centre of the balloon, lying flat, so it sticks out past the edge like a pointer.
3. Stand a card scale beside the jar so the straw points at it. Mark the straw's position and today's date.

When the outside pressure rises, it pushes the balloon down and the straw tip goes **up**. When it falls, the tip goes **down**.

## Calibrate (15 minutes)

If anyone's phone has a barometer app, read the pressure in hPa and write it next to today's mark. Over the week, add a phone reading next to each mark so your card scale gets real numbers.

## Record

Your can explanation (with the word *pressure*), your force calculation, and a drawing of the barometer. Why must the barometer stay **out of the sun** and at the same temperature? (Think about what heating does to trapped air.)
