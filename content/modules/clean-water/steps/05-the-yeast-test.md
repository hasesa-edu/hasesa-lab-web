## The problem

We can't safely grow germs from river water in a classroom. So how can we test whether a filter stops microbes?

We use a **stand-in**: baker's yeast. Yeast cells are harmless single-celled fungi, about 3–10 µm across, bigger than most bacteria. If a filter lets yeast through, it certainly lets bacteria through.

And yeast is easy to detect: give it sugar in warm water, and it makes carbon dioxide gas.

## Do: the detector (15 minutes)

1. Make **yeast water**: half a teaspoon of dry yeast in 500 mL of warm (not hot) water. Stir. Wait 5 minutes.
2. Make a **sugar solution**: 4 teaspoons of sugar in 400 mL of warm water.
3. Set up two bottles, each with 100 mL of sugar solution:
   - **Positive control:** add 50 mL of yeast water.
   - **Negative control:** add 50 mL of plain warm water.
4. Stretch a balloon over the neck of each bottle. Leave them somewhere warm.

After 20–30 minutes, the positive control's balloon should start to inflate. The negative control's shouldn't.

## Do: test your filter (30 minutes)

Rinse your filter with clean water. Then pour 200 mL of yeast water through it. Collect what comes out.

Add 50 mL of the **filtered** yeast water to a third sugar bottle, with a balloon. Compare all three balloons after 30 minutes, then again at the end of the session.

Try the same with yeast water poured through **filter paper**, and through **cloth folded four times**.

## Notice something puzzling

The filtered water probably looks clear. Does its balloon inflate? What does that tell you about bacteria, which are even smaller than yeast?

## Theory

Re-read the **filter** section of *Treating water* and the **sizes** table in *Microbes*. Why does a sand filter make water look clean but not reliably make it safe? What would a slow sand filter that has run for weeks do differently?

## Record

A table: sample, balloon after 30 minutes, balloon at the end (none, a little, lots). Conclusion in two sentences: what does your filter remove, and what doesn't it remove? Then: **what extra barrier do we need?**
