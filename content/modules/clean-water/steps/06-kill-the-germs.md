## Three ways to disinfect (40 minutes to set up)

Make a fresh batch of yeast water. Then treat it three ways, and test each with the sugar-and-balloon detector from step 5.

**1. Heat (guide only).** The guide heats 200 mL of yeast water to a rolling boil for 3 minutes, then lets it cool to warm. Why 3 minutes and not 1 here in Addis? (Hint: what temperature does water boil at, 2,350 m up?)

**2. Chlorine (guide doses).** The guide adds a measured dose of chlorine to 200 mL of yeast water. Wait 30 minutes.

**3. Sunlight (SODIS).** Fill a clear, unscratched plastic bottle with yeast water, close it, and lay it flat in full sun, on a dark surface or a metal sheet. Put a second, identical bottle in the shade as a control. Leave them for as long as the session allows; the guide leaves them out for the full 6 hours and tests them next session.

Test each with sugar and a balloon, next to an **untreated** yeast-water control.

## Theory: light you can't see

SODIS works because sunlight contains **ultraviolet (UV)** light, which damages microbes' DNA. Look at the **ultraviolet** row in *The electromagnetic spectrum*, and the paragraph under the table. Why does SODIS need a clear bottle and not a coloured one? Why does it only work on clear water?

## Theory: how much chlorine?

Read **Solutions and concentration**. You'll learn:

- what concentration means, in g/L and mg/L,
- why, for water, mg/L is the same as parts per million,
- how to dilute a solution exactly with $C_1V_1 = C_2V_2$.

## Do: calculate a dose (15 minutes)

Your guide shows you the label on the chlorine product. Work out:

1. How many mg of chlorine are in each mL of the product?
2. How many mL are needed for a **20 L jerrycan** at a dose of 2 mg/L?
3. If one drop is about 0.05 mL, how many drops is that?

Check your answer against the dose printed on the bottle.

## Record

Results table for all three methods plus control. Your chlorine calculation, with units on every line. Which method would you recommend for a family with no fuel? For a family whose water is cloudy?
