## Plan (15 minutes)

Your filter goes in a 1.5–2 L bottle with the bottom cut off, standing upside down (cap end down) in a jar. Water goes in the top and drips out of the cap.

Decide, as a team:

- Which layers, in which order, from bottom to top? Choose from: cloth, gravel, coarse sand, fine sand, charcoal.
- How thick should each layer be?
- Why should the finest layer **not** be at the very bottom, next to the cap?

Sketch your design with layer thicknesses.

## Build (35 minutes)

1. The guide cuts the bottom off each bottle. Make 3–4 small holes in the cap with a hot nail (guide only), or plug the neck with a wad of cloth.
2. Wash the gravel, sand and charcoal in a bucket until the water runs almost clear. Unwashed sand makes muddy water muddier!
3. Fill the layers carefully, pressing each one gently flat.
4. Run 1 L of **clean** tap water through first, to rinse the filter. Throw that water away.

## Test (30 minutes)

Use settled water from step 3 **and** freshly stirred muddy water.

1. Pour 500 mL in. Start a timer.
2. Record how long it takes to collect 250 mL (the **flow rate** in mL per minute).
3. Measure the clarity depth of what comes out.

Compare with other teams' designs. Which design gives the clearest water? Which is fastest? Is there a trade-off?

## Improve (10 minutes)

Change **one** thing in your filter (a thicker fine-sand layer, or adding charcoal) and test again with the same water. Did it help?

## Record

Your filter diagram, flow rate and clarity depths before and after, for both kinds of water. One sentence: does clear water from your filter mean safe water? Keep your filter: you'll test it with microbes next time.
