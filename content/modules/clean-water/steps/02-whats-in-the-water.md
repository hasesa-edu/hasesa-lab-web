## Do: let it settle (15 minutes to set up)

Gloves on. Stir the muddy water well. Fill a tall clear bottle to the brim and stand it where nobody will bump it. Mark the level of the mud at 1 minute, 5 minutes, 30 minutes and at the end of the session.

What sinks first? What's still floating after an hour?

## Do: filter paper (20 minutes)

Fold a coffee filter or filter paper into a funnel. Pour 100 mL of stirred muddy water through it into a clear cup.

- What is left on the paper?
- Is the water underneath clear? Hold it up to the light next to a cup of tap water.

Now try a cotton cloth folded once, then folded four times. Which gives clearer water?

## Do: look closer (15 minutes)

With a magnifying glass, phone macro lens or microscope, look at:

- a drop of the muddy water,
- the mud left on the filter paper,
- a drop of the filtered water.

Draw what you see, with an estimate of size if you can.

## Notice something puzzling

The filtered water may look clear. But the *Microbes* table says bacteria are 0.5–5 µm across, and the holes in filter paper are around 10–20 µm. What does that mean for the water in your cup?

## Theory: mixtures and separation

Read **Mixtures and separation**. You'll learn the difference between a mixture and a solution, and which methods separate which kinds of mixture: settling (sedimentation), decanting, filtering and evaporating.

## Record

Make a table: *thing in the water*, *rough size*, *removed by settling?*, *removed by filter paper?* Include sand, silt, clay, leaves, worm eggs, protozoa, bacteria, viruses and dissolved salt. Use ✓, ✗ or "some".
