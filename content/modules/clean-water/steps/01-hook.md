## Look, don't drink

Gloves on. Your guide puts two jars on the table. Both came from the same river this morning.

- **Jar A** is brown and cloudy. Bits float in it.
- **Jar B** is crystal clear.

Your guide says: **neither is safe to drink.** Not yet.

Look closely at both. Hold them up to the light. Smell them (waft the air towards you with your hand; don't put your nose in). Write down everything you notice.

## Predict

On a page titled *The mystery*, write:

1. **How could Jar B be unsafe when it looks perfectly clean?** Give two possible reasons.
2. **What do you think is in Jar A?** Make a list, from the biggest things to the smallest.
3. **If you had only a cloth, some sand, a pot, the sun and a bottle, how would you make Jar A safe?** Draw your plan.

## Think about it

Around the world, many families collect drinking water from rivers, ponds and unprotected springs. Diarrhoea from unsafe water is one of the biggest causes of illness in young children. Making water safe is not a school puzzle; it's one of the most useful things a scientist can know.

## Questions

As a team, list every question you have. For example:

- What exactly makes water unsafe?
- Can a filter catch germs?
- Is boiling the only way? What if there's no fuel?
- How do water works in a city do it?
