Muddy water is easy to spot. Dangerous water often isn't. In eight hours you'll take river water apart, build a treatment chain from cheap local materials, and test it with a harmless microbe that behaves like a germ.

1. **Two jars** (45 min). Compare the cloudy and the clear jar, predict, and meet the germs you can't see.
2. **What's in the water?** (75 min). Let it settle, filter it through paper, and look closely. Sort what you find by size.
3. **Settle it** (60 min). Use alum and moringa seeds to make mud sink in an hour instead of a week, and measure clarity.
4. **Build the filter** (90 min). Layer gravel, sand and charcoal in a bottle. Measure how clear and how fast.
5. **The yeast test** (75 min). Does your filter stop microbes? Find out with yeast, safely.
6. **Kill the germs** (75 min). Boiling, sunlight and chlorine: test them on the yeast stand-in, and calculate a dose.
7. **The family guide** (60 min). Put the chain together and explain it to someone who needs it.

By the end you'll understand microbes, mixtures and separation, coagulation, disinfection and concentration, and why water that looks clean isn't always safe.
