# Clean Water: guide's notes

For the guide only. About 8 hours, best as five sessions of about 1.5 hours, for up to 16 students in teams of four. Every test uses cheap local materials. The one rule that never bends: **nobody drinks any water in this module.**

---

## Before the module

### 1. The muddy water

You need about 20 L across the module, and more for step 7. Two options:

- **River or pond water** is the most convincing, and step 1 depends on the story that both jars came from the same river. Collect it yourself in clean jerrycans, wearing gloves, from a spot you can reach safely (no steep banks, no deep or fast water). Keep it covered and use it within a few days.
- **Made-up muddy water** (garden soil stirred into tap water, about 2 heaped spoons per litre, then left 5 minutes for stones to sink). It's safer and more consistent. Use it for most of the tests.

For **Jar B** in step 1, let river water settle for two or three days, then pour the clear top part through a coffee filter. It looks clean but hasn't been disinfected.

### 2. Shopping

| Item | Notes |
|---|---|
| Gravel, coarse sand, fine (plaster) sand | 2 kg each. Wash in a bucket until the water runs nearly clear; this takes a while, so do it ahead. |
| Charcoal | Cooking charcoal, crushed in a cloth bag with a hammer, sieved to pea size and rinsed. |
| Alum | A small packet is plenty. If your source sells it in lumps, crush and dissolve to a 10 g/L stock: 1 mL of stock contains 0.01 g. |
| Moringa seeds | Use dry seeds; remove the shell and wings, and crush the kernels to a fine powder just before use. |
| Dry baker's yeast | 2–3 packets. Check the date: old yeast is slow. |
| Chlorine | A household water-treatment product (follow its label) or plain, unscented household bleach. **Note the strength** on the label for step 6. |
| Clear PET bottles | Clear, unscratched, no larger than 2 L, for SODIS. |
| Balloons | Two packets: the yeast tests use a lot. |

### 3. Practise the yeast test at home

Do it once before the module: yeast in warm sugar water should start to inflate a balloon within 20–40 minutes at room temperature, faster in a warm place (about 30–35 °C, like a sunny windowsill). If it's slow, use warmer water (hand-warm, never hot: above about 50 °C kills the yeast).

---

## Safety briefing (step 1, and again at steps 2 and 6)

- Nobody drinks any water today, or on any day in this module.
- Gloves for untreated water. Hands away from faces. Wash with soap at the end.
- You alone handle bleach/chlorine and boiling water.
- **Never mix bleach with vinegar, acids or other cleaners.** Keep the vinegar from other modules out of the room.
- Mop up spills immediately; wipe benches with soapy water.

## Session plans

### Step 1: Two jars (45 min)

Don't reveal how Jar B was made until the end of the session. Good reasons students give: germs, chemicals (dissolved, so invisible), worm eggs. Accept "chemicals" as a real possibility: fluoride and other dissolved substances are an issue in parts of the Rift Valley, and no home treatment in this module removes them. Mention it, but keep the focus on germs.

### Step 2: What's in the water? (75 min)

Settling: sand sinks in seconds, silt in minutes, clay can stay floating for days. Filter paper removes most visible mud but the water is often still slightly cloudy (clay). Cloth folded four times is noticeably better than once.

**Expected table:** sand, silt, leaves, worm eggs: removed by settling/paper ✓. Protozoa: some. Bacteria, viruses: ✗. Dissolved salt: ✗ by both.

### Step 3: Settle it (60 min)

**Clarity tube.** A 1.5 L bottle gives about 25–30 cm. Typical results: muddy water 3–8 cm. After 30 minutes: control 5–10 cm, alum 0.05 g: 20 cm or more, moringa: 15–25 cm, alum 0.2 g: often clear past the top, sometimes no better than 0.05 g. Too much coagulant can make results worse, which is a good discussion point.

If flocs don't form: stir faster in the first minute, then very slowly. Moringa works better on very muddy water than on slightly muddy water.

### Step 4: Build the filter (90 min)

Good order, bottom (cap end) to top: cloth plug, gravel 3 cm, coarse sand 4 cm, charcoal 3 cm, fine sand 8–10 cm, gravel 2 cm on top to spread the water. Fine sand directly on the cap washes out and blocks the holes, so it needs coarser layers below it.

Expect flow rates of 30–150 mL/min. Faster filters are usually less effective. Unwashed sand is the most common reason for brown water coming out.

### Step 5: The yeast test (75 min)

**Expected result:** water from a home-made sand filter, filter paper or cloth still makes the balloon inflate (perhaps a bit more slowly). Yeast gets through. This is the key insight: **clear ≠ safe**, and filters like these don't remove microbes reliably. A well-run slow sand filter or a ceramic filter would do much better, and you can mention them.

Students may object that "a little" yeast came through. Ask: if one bacterium can become 64 in two hours, does "a little" matter?

### Step 6: Kill the germs (75 min)

- **Boiling:** cool to warm before adding sugar. Expected: no gas.
- **Chlorine:** yeast water is full of organic matter that uses up chlorine, so a drinking-water dose may not stop the yeast. Run **two** tubes: a drinking-water dose (2 mg/L) and a much higher dose (about 3 drops of 5% bleach in 200 mL, roughly 40 mg/L). Expected: the high dose stops gas; the low dose may not. Discussion: this is exactly why water is settled and filtered **before** chlorinating.
- **SODIS:** it won't finish within the session. Leave the sun and shade bottles out for the full 6 hours (or two days if cloudy), keep them safe overnight, and test them at the start of step 7. Expected: the sun bottle produces less gas or none; the shade bottle produces lots. Results with yeast are less clear-cut than with bacteria; say so.

**Dose calculation, using 5% bleach (50 mg/mL):** 2 mg/L × 20 L = 40 mg; 40 ÷ 50 = 0.8 mL ≈ 16 drops. Products differ: always use the strength printed on yours.

### Step 7: The family guide (60 min)

Test the SODIS bottles first. While teams make their guides, run the sign-off conversations.

## Judging the sign-off items

- **clear-water:** measured in the clarity tube, at least 30 cm (or past the top of a shorter tube).
- **barriers:** must say that filters and settling remove particles but not reliably microbes, and that disinfection kills microbes but doesn't remove mud or dissolved chemicals.
- **yeast-result:** correctly concludes that filtered water can still contain microbes.
- **chlorine-dose:** units on every line; the answer matches the label within about 20%.
- **clear-not-safe:** uses size: microbes are much smaller than the particles that make water look cloudy.
- **family-guide:** correct order, a no-fuel option (SODIS, or chlorine if available), safe storage.
