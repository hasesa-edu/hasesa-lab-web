## The question

In step 3 you read that biogas is mostly methane and carbon dioxide. That's a claim from a book. Today you collect your own evidence.

## Theory: trustworthy tests (10 minutes)

Read the part of **Chemical tests** about controls. Today's test needs:

- a **negative control**: something you know has almost no carbon dioxide (room air),
- a **positive control**: something you know has plenty (your own breath),
- the **sample**: gas from your digester,

all tested the same way, with the same volume of limewater and the same volume of gas.

## Do: the limewater test (30 minutes, glasses on)

Limewater is a clear solution of calcium hydroxide, made by your guide from builders' lime (ኖራ, nora). Carbon dioxide reacts with it to make tiny white grains of calcium carbonate, so the liquid turns **milky**:

$$\text{CO}_2 + \text{Ca(OH)}_2 \rightarrow \text{CaCO}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{O}$$

1. Put 10 mL of limewater into each of three small clear cups or bottle caps. Label them *air*, *breath*, *biogas*.
2. **Air:** fill a syringe with 40 mL of room air. Push it slowly through a straw into the *air* cup. Watch for 1 minute.
3. **Breath:** blow gently through a clean straw into the *breath* cup for about 10 seconds. Blow, never suck.
4. **Biogas:** your guide shows you how to draw 40 mL of gas from your best digester into a syringe: from the top of a water collector through a short tube pushed up into it under water, or from the guide's balloon through a tube while the neck is pinched. Push it slowly through a straw into the *biogas* cup.
5. Hold all three cups against a dark background. Compare them. Repeat the biogas test with a sample from a second bottle.

| Cup | What went in | Milky? (none / slight / very) | Time to go milky |
|---|---|---|---|
| air | 40 mL room air | | |
| breath | about 10 s of breath | | |
| biogas | 40 mL from bottle ___ | | |

## Notice something puzzling

The biogas made the limewater milky, so it has carbon dioxide in it. But carbon dioxide puts fires **out**. So why does the guide's gas burn?

## Watch: the flame test (15 minutes, guide only)

Only if the guide's jerrycan has made plenty of gas, and only outdoors. Everyone stands at least 3 m back, wearing glasses. Nobody else holds the flame.

Your guide squeezes gas gently from the balloon through a long tube, and holds a long lighter to the far end of the tube, never near the balloon or the jerrycan. Watch the colour and size of the flame, and what happens as the guide squeezes harder or more gently.

If there isn't enough gas, or the gas won't light, your guide explains why and shows you the video for this step. Early gas is mostly carbon dioxide and won't burn; it only lights once the methane makers have got going. That is a result too.

## Theory: fuels and burning (10 minutes)

Read **Combustion and fuels**. The part of biogas that burns is **methane**:

$$\text{CH}_4 + 2\,\text{O}_2 \rightarrow \text{CO}_2 + 2\,\text{H}_2\text{O}$$

Pure methane releases about 36 MJ of energy per cubic metre. The carbon dioxide in biogas doesn't burn; it just comes along for the ride and dilutes the methane.

## Do it better: what can biogas cook? (25 minutes)

Work these out, with units on every line:

1. Biogas that is 60% methane: how many MJ per m³? How many kJ per litre?
2. Water boils at about 92 °C in Addis Ababa. How much energy does it take to heat 2 kg of water from 20 °C to boiling? Use $Q = mc\Delta T$ with $c = 4200$ J/(kg·°C).
3. A good biogas stove puts about 55% of the gas's energy into the pot. How many litres of biogas does it take to boil that pot?
4. Your team's best bottle made how many litres of gas in total? How many pots of tea is that? Be honest.
5. A biogas stove burns about 0.4 m³ of gas per hour. A household plant makes about 1.6 m³ a day. How many hours of cooking is that?

## Record

Your results table, a sentence saying what the limewater test proves and what it doesn't prove, what you saw at the flame test (or why it wasn't done), and your five calculations.
