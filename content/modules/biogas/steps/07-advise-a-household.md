## Finish the seed test (25 minutes)

1. Count the seeds that germinated in each cup (a root sticking out counts). Work out the germination percentage: $\dfrac{\text{germinated}}{20} \times 100$.
2. Measure the root length of every seedling in one cup of each liquid with a ruler, in mm, and work out the mean.
3. Compare each liquid with the water control. A useful single number is the **germination index**:

$$\text{GI} = \frac{\text{germination \% (liquid)} \times \text{mean root (liquid)}}{\text{germination \% (water)} \times \text{mean root (water)}} \times 100$$

A GI above 100 means the liquid did better than plain water. Well below 100 means it harmed the seeds.

4. Draw a bar chart of GI for each liquid.

## Back to the mystery (15 minutes)

Open the page titled *The gas from nowhere* from step 1. Next to each guess, write ✓ or ✗ and what you now know. How close was your guess of the gas from 1 kg of dung? Work out the real number from your standard bottle: total litres ÷ kg of dung.

Answer the first question properly, in your own words: **where did the gas come from?** Use these words: *anaerobic, microbes, archaea, methane, carbon dioxide, temperature*.

## Write your recommendation (40 minutes)

A family near Debre Birhan keeps four cows in a shed at night and collects about 30 kg of fresh dung a day. They are thinking of building a 6 m³ household plant. Write them one page of advice, using **your** data and the class's. Include:

1. **Feed:** what to put in, and what not to. Use the food test results.
2. **Water:** how much water to mix with 30 kg of dung, using the dilution results. Why not much more, or much less?
3. **Warmth:** Debre Birhan is cold at night. What do the temperature results tell them? Real household plants are built underground, under a dome of brick or concrete. Why might that help?
4. **Gas:** a household plant keeps each load of dung inside for about two months, so the microbes digest far more of it than in your bottles. Such plants make roughly 30–40 L of gas for every kg of fresh dung fed in. Using 35 L/kg, how much gas a day would 30 kg of dung a day give? How does that compare with your bottles' litres per kg? Using your step 5 results, how many hours of cooking, or how many pots of water boiled, is that?
5. **Slurry:** should they spread it straight on seedlings, or dilute it first? Use your seed-test results.
6. **Safety:** two things the family must never do.

Give every number its working and its unit.

## Explain it to the guide (25 minutes)

Your guide will ask each of you about your results. Be ready to:

- show your gas log and graph, and point to the lag, the steep part and the fastest rate,
- say which bottle in your test made the most gas, and whether you're sure, using the standard bottles' range,
- explain the three microbe teams and why the cold bottles were slow,
- say what the limewater test proved, and which gas burns,
- show your seed-test bar chart and say how a farmer should use bio-slurry,
- read out one line of your recommendation and the numbers behind it.

Hand in your recommendation. Your guide will sign off each item when you can show it.
