## Final readings (15 minutes, gloves on)

It's been about two weeks. Take your last readings and fill in your table. Then your guide helps you open each digester **outdoors**, slowly, pointing the cap away from faces.

Compare the slurry in each bottle with fresh dung slurry:

- **Smell:** fresh dung, sour, rotten eggs, or almost nothing?
- **Look:** colour, crust on top, bubbles, how runny it is.

Write a short description of each. Which smell matches the bottles that made the most gas?

## The question

Families with biogas plants spread the leftover **bio-slurry** on their fields. Is it really a fertiliser? Or could it harm young plants? Fresh dung can burn seedlings, and you've read that digesters can turn sour.

## Theory: what a seed needs (10 minutes)

Read the parts of **Photosynthesis and plant growth** about germination and measuring growth. A seed needs water, warmth and oxygen to germinate. Nutrients like nitrogen help the young plant once it's growing, but too many salts or too much ammonia in the water can stop a seed from germinating at all.

## Plan (10 minutes)

You'll use **garden cress** (ፌጦ, feto), which germinates in one or two days.

1. **Question:** Does bio-slurry help or harm cress seeds?
2. **I will change:** what the seeds are watered with. Use four liquids: plain water (the control), slurry diluted 1 part to 4 parts water, slurry diluted 1 : 1, and undiluted slurry. Use slurry from your standard bottle. If there is time, add a fifth: **fresh** dung slurry diluted 1 : 4, to compare digested with undigested.
3. **I will measure:** how many seeds germinate by day 2, and the root length of each seedling by day 5.
4. **I will keep the same:** ___ (at least four things).
5. **Repeats:** 20 seeds per cup, two cups per liquid.

## Do: set up the seed test (25 minutes, gloves on)

1. Pour a little slurry through a cloth or a tea strainer to remove lumps.
2. Make the dilutions with a syringe. For 1 : 4, that's 5 mL slurry and 20 mL water. Work out the others.
3. Line each cup or jar lid with a folded piece of tissue paper or a layer of cotton wool. Label it.
4. Wet the tissue with **10 mL** of the liquid. Count exactly 20 seeds onto it and spread them out.
5. Cover each cup loosely with a piece of plastic so it doesn't dry out. Keep them all in the same place, out of direct sun.

Then help your guide empty the digesters into the hole in the soil or the compost pit, rinse the bottles outdoors, and wash your hands.
