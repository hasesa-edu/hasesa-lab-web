## The rules first (10 minutes)

You'll be working with cow dung, which is full of microbes, and with a gas that burns. Your guide reads out the rules. Copy them into the front of your notebook:

1. Gloves on whenever you touch dung, scraps, slurry or a digester. Cover cuts with a plaster first.
2. Wash your hands with soap at the end, even after gloves.
3. No eating, drinking or touching your face at the digesters.
4. Digesters live outdoors. Never seal one without a balloon or a gas tube.
5. No flames near digesters, ever. Only the guide lights biogas.

## Watch (15 minutes)

A week ago your guide filled a 5 L jerrycan with fresh cow dung and water, stretched a flat balloon over the top, and left it in a sunny spot. Go outside and look at it now.

Look carefully, and write down:

- the size of the balloon (wrap a string around its widest part and measure it),
- the smell when the guide lets a tiny puff of gas out, far from any flame,
- what the slurry looks like through the side of the jerrycan: any bubbles, any crust on top?

If the gas is ready, your guide may also show a very small flame test, outdoors, with everyone 3 m back and wearing glasses. If not, the guide will describe what happens, and you'll see it in step 5.

## Predict (15 minutes)

On a page titled *The gas from nowhere*, write:

1. **Where did the gas come from?** The jerrycan had only dung and water in it. Write your best guess, then a second one.
2. **What is the gas?** Air? Steam? Something else? Why do you think that?
3. **Guess:** how much gas, in litres, would 1 kg of fresh cow dung make in two weeks? Each person writes a number. You'll find out in step 4.
4. **Guess:** what would make it faster? List three things you could change.

## Why it matters (10 minutes)

Many rural families cook on wood or on **kubet** (ኩበት), cakes of dried dung. Both make thick smoke in the kitchen, and burning the dung throws away the nutrients that could feed the soil. A family biogas plant takes the same dung, makes clean-burning gas for the stove, and gives back a **bio-slurry** that is still a good fertiliser.

Discuss with your team: who in the family benefits most? What would a family need before they could build one: how many cattle, how much water, how much money?

## Questions (10 minutes)

As a team, list every question you have. For example:

- Does it work with food scraps, or only dung?
- Why did it take a whole week?
- Would it be faster in the sun?
- How much water should you mix in?
- Is the slurry really a fertiliser, or just dirty water?

Circle the three you most want to answer. Next time you'll build your own digesters to test them. Tonight, read **Fermentation and respiration**, and look for the part about living things that release energy **without oxygen**.
