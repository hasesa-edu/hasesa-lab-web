## The class experiment (15 minutes)

Each team tests **one** question from your list, with three bottles. Your guide gives each team one of these:

| Test | Bottle 1 | Bottle 2 | Bottle 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Food** | fresh dung | food scraps | half dung, half scraps |
| **Temperature** | warm box in the sun | shade, outdoors | indoors (a room) |
| **Dilution** (dung : water, by mass) | 1 : 0.5 (thick) | 1 : 1 | 1 : 2 (thin) |

Look closely: one bottle is the same in every test. **Fresh dung, 1 : 1, in the warm box** is the class's **standard bottle**. Because every team makes one, the class gets several repeats of it for free. If your test is food, your other bottles are also 1 : 1 and in the warm box. If it's dilution, they're all dung in the warm box. Everything else stays the same.

Your guide also sets up one **control**: a bottle with only water, connected in exactly the same way.

## Plan (10 minutes)

Read **Fair tests and averages** if you haven't yet. Then write your plan in five lines:

1. **Question:** Does ___ affect how much gas a digester makes?
2. **I will change:** ___ (these three settings: ___)
3. **I will measure:** the volume of gas every day, with ___
4. **I will keep the same:** ___ (list at least five things: think about the bottle, the total amount of slurry, how fresh the dung is, how you seal it…)
5. **Repeats and control:** the standard bottles across the class, and the water-only bottle.

Why does the water-only bottle matter? Write one sentence. (Hint: what could change the reading even if no gas is made?)

Show your plan to your guide before you mix anything.

## Mix the slurry (20 minutes, gloves on)

Work outdoors, on a sheet of plastic or cardboard.

1. Weigh out your dung (or scraps) on the kitchen scale. For a 2 L bottle, use a total of **1.4 kg** of slurry. For 1 : 1, that is 700 g of dung and 700 g (700 mL) of water.
2. Food scraps: chop them small, then mash them with a stick or the back of a spoon. Why does chopping help? Look back at your guess list.
3. Stir until there are no lumps. Pour into the bottle through a wide funnel.
4. Fill each bottle to the **same height**, just below the shoulder. Less space at the top means less air inside.
5. Label each bottle with your team, the setting and today's date.

**Work it out.** For dung : water = 1 : 2, how many grams of dung and of water make 1.4 kg in total? For 1 : 0.5? Write the working.

## Build the gas collector (35 minutes)

Choose a **water collector** (more accurate) or a **balloon** (simpler). Your guide decides which, so the whole test uses the same method.

**Water collector**

1. Your guide makes a hole in each cap and pushes a tube through it. Seal around the tube with modelling clay or epoxy, then wrap tape over it. The seal must be airtight.
2. Take an empty 1.5 L bottle. Pour in water 100 mL at a time and draw a line at each 100 mL on the side. Number the lines, starting from the **bottom of the bottle**, which will be the top when it's turned over.
3. Fill this bottle completely with water. Hold your hand over the mouth, turn it upside down, and stand it mouth-down in a basin of water. No air bubble at the top.
4. Push the free end of the tube up into its mouth, under the water. Tape the bottle to a stick so it stands upright.
5. Screw the cap onto the digester. Gas from the digester will bubble into the collector and push the water down. You read the gas volume from the lines.

**Balloon**

1. Stretch a balloon a few times, then squeeze all the air out.
2. Stretch it over the mouth of the bottle and wrap tape tightly round the neck.
3. You'll measure its size every day with a string.

## Place and check (15 minutes)

Put your bottles where your test says: in the warm box (bottles wrapped in black plastic, standing in straw or cloth, clear plastic on top), in the shade, or indoors. Put a thermometer next to each group of bottles.

Before you leave, squeeze each digester very gently. Bubbles should come out of the tube under the water, not from around the cap. Any leak? Seal it now.

## Record

Copy this table into your notebook, one for each bottle. You'll fill it in every day for the next two weeks.

| Day | Date and time | Temperature (°C) | Reading (mL) | Gas today (mL) | Total gas (mL) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | | | 0 | 0 | 0 | set up |

Wash your hands with soap before you leave.
