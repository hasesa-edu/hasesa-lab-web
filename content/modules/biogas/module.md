In Ethiopia, the National Biogas Programme has helped tens of thousands of families build a digester in their yard. Every day they feed it cow dung and water. Every day it gives them gas to cook with, and a rich, dark slurry to spread on their fields. In ten hours over about three weeks, you build small digesters of your own, find out what makes them work best, and prove what the gas is made of.

1. **The gas from nowhere** (60 min). See the guide's digester, learn the hygiene rules, and predict where the gas comes from.
2. **Build your digesters** (105 min). Plan a fair test, mix slurry and build three bottle digesters with gas collectors.
3. **The hidden workers** (75 min). Meet the microbes that make methane, and use chemistry to see why your digesters are slow to start.
4. **The daily gas log** (90 min, spread over two weeks). Read your gas every day, then graph it and find the rates.
5. **What's in the gas?** (90 min). Test for carbon dioxide with limewater, watch the guide's flame test, and work out what biogas could cook.
6. **Bio-slurry** (75 min). Stop the digesters and test whether the slurry helps cress seeds grow.
7. **Advise a household** (105 min). Finish the seed test, put all your results together, and write a recommendation.

Gas takes days, so the steps are spread out: build early, read your digester for a few minutes every day, and come back for the next step when your guide says. By the end you'll understand anaerobic respiration, microbes, rates of reaction, fair tests, the limewater test and combustion, and you'll have your own data to show what makes a digester work.
