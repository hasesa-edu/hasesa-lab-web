## Do: Faraday's experiment (40 minutes)

1. Wind 100 turns of enamelled wire around a cardboard tube just wide enough for your magnets to pass through. Scrape the enamel off the last 1 cm of each end with sandpaper.
2. Connect the ends to a multimeter on its most sensitive **DC millivolt** setting (200 mV).
3. Push a strong magnet into the tube. Watch the meter. Hold it still. Pull it out.

Record what the meter shows each time. Then try:

- moving the magnet fast vs slowly,
- the other pole first,
- two magnets stacked together,
- a coil of 200 turns (wind another 100 on top, the same direction),
- moving the coil instead of the magnet.

For each, record the biggest reading.

## Notice something puzzling

When the magnet is sitting still inside the coil, the reading is zero, even though the magnet's field is right there. What exactly does the coil respond to?

## Theory

Read **Electromagnetism**, especially part 3, *A changing magnetic field makes a current*, then read **Electromagnetic induction**. You'll learn:

- that a voltage is induced only when the magnetic field through a coil changes,
- the three things that make the voltage bigger,
- why spinning magnets make alternating current,
- why generators fight back when you take power from them.

## Do: see AC (20 minutes)

Tape a magnet to the side of a pencil (or a cork on a nail) and spin it close to your coil. Watch the meter on the DC setting: it flickers between positive and negative. Switch to the **AC volts** setting: now it shows a steady number.

If anyone's phone has a free oscilloscope app and a headphone-socket adapter, the guide can show the AC wave shape on screen.

## Record

A results table for every test, with the biggest voltage. Three sentences: what makes the induced voltage bigger, why the reading changes sign when you pull the magnet out, and why a spinning magnet makes AC.
