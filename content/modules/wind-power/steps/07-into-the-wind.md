## Assemble (40 minutes)

1. Mount the generator and rotor on a short wooden arm.
2. Add a **tail vane** (a flat piece of card or plastic on the back of the arm) so the turbine turns to face the wind.
3. The guide fixes the arm on a pole with a bolt, so it can swing around freely.
4. Run the wires down the pole to your circuit box (in a plastic container, out of the rain).
5. Add a **brake**: a way to stop the blades safely, such as turning the tail so the turbine faces sideways to the wind.

## Test outdoors (40 minutes)

The guide puts the turbine up on a short pole in open ground, away from power lines and paths. **Stay out of the plane of the blades.**

Over at least 30 minutes, every 5 minutes record:

- wind speed (anemometer),
- output voltage after the rectifier,
- whether the LEDs are lit or the power bank is charging.

## Back to the mystery (10 minutes)

Open *The mystery* from step 1. Next to each guess, write ✓ or ✗ and what you now know. Then answer properly: **how does spinning a shaft in the wind make electricity?** Use the words *magnet, coil, changing field, induced voltage, AC, rectifier*.

## Explain it to the guide (30 minutes)

Your guide will ask each of you to:

- explain Faraday's experiment and what makes the induced voltage bigger,
- explain why the generator is harder to turn when it's making power,
- show your blade test and say why you chose your design,
- show your power and efficiency calculations,
- trace your circuit from generator to load and explain each part,
- say what would make your turbine better, and what it would take to power a home.
