## Why blades turn (15 minutes)

Hold a flat piece of card at an angle in the fan's wind (the guide controls the fan, glasses on). Which way is it pushed? Now hold it flat-on to the wind, then edge-on.

Read the **third law** in *Forces and motion*. A blade turns because it pushes air one way, so the air pushes it the other. Blades are angled so the push on them is mostly **around** the circle, not straight back.

## Make blades (45 minutes)

The guide cuts a 60 cm length of 110 mm PVC pipe into quarters lengthways, and then marks and cuts blade shapes from them. Each blade is about 30 cm long, wide at the root (about 7 cm) and narrow at the tip (about 2 cm). The pipe's curve gives the blade its shape.

Your team:

1. Sands every cut edge smooth (gloves and glasses).
2. Drills (guide) two holes at the root of each blade and bolts them onto the hub at equal spacing. Every blade must be at the **same angle** and the **same distance** from the centre.
3. **Balance test:** mount the rotor on a horizontal bolt so it spins freely. Let go. If one blade always ends up at the bottom, it's heavier: sand a little off it, and try again.

## Test: how many blades? (45 minutes)

Mount your rotor on a stepper motor (the guide shows how to connect it to the shaft). Connect one pair of the motor's wires to the multimeter on AC volts.

Put the turbine in front of the fan at a marked distance, fan at a fixed setting. Measure:

- the voltage with no load,
- the rotation speed: film in slow motion with one blade marked, and count turns per second.

Test with **2, 3, 4 and 6 blades** (teams share rotors). Keep the fan, distance and generator the same.

## Notice something puzzling

More blades catch more wind, so why don't big turbines have 10 blades? Which design spun fastest? Which started turning in the weakest wind?

## Theory

Read **Energy and power**, especially the last section, *Power from moving air*.

## Record

A results table (blades, rpm, voltage). Graph voltage against number of blades. Which design will you use, and why? Keep in mind that a fast-spinning rotor makes a bigger voltage, but a rotor that starts in weak wind works more of the time.
