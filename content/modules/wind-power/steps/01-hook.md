## Turn the handle

Your guide has a hand-crank torch (or a bicycle dynamo connected to a small bulb). Take turns:

- Turn the handle slowly. Then fast. What happens to the light?
- Turn it with nothing connected (the guide disconnects the bulb). Then with the bulb. Does it feel different?
- Turn it backwards. Does it still work?

Then watch a short video of the Adama wind farm. Each turbine's blades are longer than a bus.

## Look inside

If the guide has opened an old dynamo or a small motor, look at the parts. You should find **magnets**, **coils of copper wire** and something that spins. Draw them.

## Predict

On a page titled *The mystery*, write:

1. **How does spinning make electricity?** Your best guess, and a second one.
2. **Why was it harder to turn with the bulb connected?** Where was that extra effort going?
3. **Is the electricity from the crank the same kind as from a battery?** Why or why not?
4. **How much power do you think one big wind turbine makes?** Enough for one home? A hundred? Ten thousand?

## Questions

As a team, list every question you have. For example:

- Why are turbine blades so long and thin?
- Why don't they use more blades, like a fan?
- What happens when the wind stops?
- Could we make electricity at home with wind?

## The first clue

Read *Electromagnetic induction* tonight. Then, next time, you'll do Faraday's famous experiment yourself.
