## The design

A magnet spins on a shaft inside a box. Around the box is a coil of several hundred turns. Each time the magnet's poles sweep past the coil, the field through it changes, inducing a voltage.

## Build (90 minutes)

1. **The box.** Make a small cardboard box, about 4 × 4 × 3 cm, open at both ends, just big enough for the magnet to spin inside without touching the walls.
2. **The shaft.** Push a nail or steel rod through the middle of the box sides, so it spins freely. Fix 2–4 neodymium disc magnets to the shaft with tape or glue, so their poles face **outwards** across the box (a north face on one side, a south face on the other).
3. **The coil.** Wind 300–500 turns of thin enamelled wire around the outside of the box, across the middle, keeping the shaft free to turn. Count as you go. Tape the coil in place. Scrape and tin the ends (the guide solders if needed).
4. **The crank.** Fix a cork, bottle cap or small handle to one end of the shaft. Or wrap string around the shaft and pull it, like spinning a top.

## Test (40 minutes)

1. Connect the coil to the multimeter on **AC volts**. Spin as fast as you can. Record the reading.
2. Connect a red LED. Spin fast. Does it flash? (An LED only lights with current in one direction, so it flickers. Why?)
3. Connect two LEDs side by side, facing opposite ways. What happens now?
4. Spin with and without an LED connected. Can you feel the difference? Connect a short piece of wire across the coil (a short circuit). How does it feel now?

## Theory

Read **Charge, current and voltage**: what is the voltage pushing, and why does a bigger current need more effort?

Re-read **Nothing for free** in *Electromagnetic induction*.

## Improve (20 minutes)

Choose one improvement and test it: more turns, stronger magnets, a smaller gap between magnet and coil, or spinning faster. Record the voltage before and after.

## Record

A labelled drawing of your generator with the number of turns. Your voltage readings. Explain the flickering LED, the two back-to-back LEDs, and why the shaft got harder to turn when the LED was connected.
