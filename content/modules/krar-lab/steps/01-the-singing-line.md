## The string rules first (5 minutes)

Strings in this module are stretched tight. Tight nylon can snap and whip. Copy these rules into the front of your notebook:

1. Safety glasses on whenever a string is under tension.
2. A bucket under every hanging bottle, and feet out from under it.
3. Never more than 2 kg on the thinnest line, 3 kg on the others.
4. Tighten slowly, a quarter-turn at a time. Never put your face close to a string you are tightening.
5. Only the guide saws and drills.

## Watch and listen (10 minutes)

Your guide has a plank with one nylon fishing line on it. Listen carefully as the guide:

1. plucks the line when it is **loose**,
2. hangs a **1 L** bottle of water on the end and plucks again,
3. changes to a **2 L** bottle,
4. slides a pencil under the line to make the vibrating part **shorter**,
5. puts the whole plank on top of an empty **box** and plucks again.

For each one, write: higher or lower than before? Louder or quieter?

Then your guide plays a krar (or a recording of one). Count the strings. Are they all the same length? The same thickness?

## Predict (10 minutes)

On a page titled *The singing line*, write:

1. **What makes one note higher than another?** Give two guesses.
2. **What did the box do?** Did it add energy to the sound?
3. **Guess:** if you halve the length of a string, what happens to the note? And if you double the weight hanging on it?

## Questions (10 minutes)

As a team, list every question you have. For example:

- Why does a tighter string sound higher?
- Why do krars have thick and thin strings?
- How can a phone tell what note I am playing?
- Why do azmaris retune their krar between songs?

## The first clue (5 minutes)

A sound is a **vibration**. Look closely at a plucked string: it blurs, because it is moving back and forth too fast to see. The faster it moves, the higher the note. Read **Waves and vibrations** tonight. Next time you build your own one-string box and measure exactly how fast it vibrates.
