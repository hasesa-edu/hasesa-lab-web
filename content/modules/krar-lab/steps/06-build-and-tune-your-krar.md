## How a krar is made (5 minutes)

A traditional krar has a round wooden bowl covered with skin, two arms rising from it in a V, and a crossbar (yoke) at the top. The strings run from the bottom of the bowl, over a bridge on the skin, up to the yoke. Players tune by twisting each string's roll of cloth around the yoke.

Yours works the same way. Your box is the bowl, eucalyptus sticks are the arms and yoke, and eye bolts with wing nuts do the tuning.

## Build the frame (35 minutes)

Your guide has cut and drilled the sticks. Glasses on whenever a string is tight.

1. **Tail bar.** Screw the short (25 cm) bar across the bottom ends of the two arms, so the arms spread out in a V. Glue, then screw.
2. **Yoke.** Screw the long (35 cm) bar across the top ends of the arms. It has five holes drilled in it, about 5 cm apart.
3. **Box.** Sit the box between the arms, against the tail bar, lid facing up. Tie it to both arms with cord so it can't slide. The frame takes the pull of the strings, not the box. If your box is cardboard, glue a strip of wood inside the lid where the bridge will sit.
4. **Tuners.** Push an eye bolt through each hole in the yoke, from the box side. On the far side put a washer and a wing nut. Turn each wing nut only two or three turns on for now.

## String it (20 minutes)

Use the thickest line for the lowest note: 0.8, 0.7 (or 0.6), 0.5, 0.5 and 0.4 mm, if you have them.

1. Tie each line to the tail bar (or a screw in it).
2. Run it up over the box lid to the eye bolt and tie it through the eye. Pull it **hand-tight** before you tie: the wing nut only does the last part.
3. Put the notched bridge under all five strings, on the middle of the lid. Its notches keep the strings apart. The strings must press down on it; that is how their vibration gets into the box.

## The maths of a qenet (15 minutes)

A **qenet** (ቅኝት) is the set of notes a song uses. Two of the most common in Ethiopia are **tizita** (ትዝታ, "memory") and **bati** (named after the town of Bati in Wollo). Each has five notes. What matters is not the exact notes but the **ratios** between them. Multiply your lowest frequency by these:

| String | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tizita (major) ratio | 1 | 1.125 | 1.25 | 1.5 | 1.67 |
| Bati (major) ratio | 1 | 1.25 | 1.33 | 1.5 | 1.88 |

**Worked example.** Tizita starting from 262 Hz (the note C):

- string 2: $262 \times 1.125 = 295$ Hz
- string 3: $262 \times 1.25 = 328$ Hz
- string 4: $262 \times 1.5 = 393$ Hz
- string 5: $262 \times 1.67 = 438$ Hz

Choose your qenet and a starting note. Work out all five targets and write them in a table. Then work out the **period** of your highest and lowest notes in milliseconds.

## Tune (25 minutes)

Glasses on. One string at a time:

1. Pluck it and read the tuner.
2. Too low? Turn the wing nut a **quarter-turn** to tighten. Too high? Loosen it.
3. Stop when you are within 2% of your target. (For 262 Hz, that means between 257 and 267 Hz.)
4. Nylon stretches. After all five are done, go round and check them again, then again.

If a string won't reach its note without becoming very tight, swap it for a thinner line, or choose a lower starting note.

## Record (5 minutes)

Write your target table and the frequency each string actually reached. Work out the percentage error for each.
