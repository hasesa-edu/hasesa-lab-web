## The question (5 minutes)

On a krar, players tighten a string to raise its note. And the low strings are thicker. Today you find out exactly how much tension and thickness matter, with fair tests.

## Theory: fair tests and averages

Read **Fair tests and averages**. You'll learn:

- the independent, dependent and controlled variables,
- why you repeat each reading three times,
- how to find a mean and a range,
- why changing two things at once ruins a test.

## Plan (10 minutes)

Write a five-line plan for **test A: tension**:

1. **Question:** Does the weight hanging on the line affect its frequency?
2. **I will change:** the mass of the water bottle: 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0 and 3.0 kg (two bottles tied together for 3 kg).
3. **I will measure:** the frequency, with the tuner app.
4. **I will keep the same:** ___ (at least three things, including the length).
5. **Repeats:** three readings for each mass.

Show it to the guide before you start.

## Do: test A, tension (25 minutes)

Use the 0.5 mm line and a vibrating length of 50 cm. Glasses on, bucket under the bottle.

1. Weigh each bottle with its water (or count 1 kg per litre plus 40 g for the bottle).
2. Hang it **slowly**. Wait until it stops swinging.
3. Pluck and read three times.

The pull in the line, the **tension**, equals the weight of the bottle: $T = mg$, with $g = 9.8$ N/kg. A 1.5 kg bottle pulls with $1.5 \times 9.8 = 14.7$ N.

| Mass (kg) | Tension (N) | $f$ readings (Hz) | mean $f$ (Hz) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5 | 4.9 | | |
| 1.0 | | | |

## Notice something puzzling

Find the mean frequency at 0.5 kg and at 2.0 kg. You made the weight **four times** bigger. Did the frequency become four times bigger? Twice as big? What would you need to hang to double the frequency again?

## Do: test B, thickness (25 minutes)

Now change only the line. Keep the length at 50 cm and the weight at 1.5 kg.

1. Test the 0.3 mm, 0.5 mm and 0.8 mm lines, three readings each. Never hang more than 2 kg on the 0.3 mm line.
2. Measure the thickness on the reel's label (or ask the guide).
3. Work out $f \times d$ for each line (frequency times diameter in mm).

## Record (20 minutes)

1. Draw a graph of mean $f$ (y-axis) against tension $T$ (x-axis). Is it a straight line, or a curve that bends over?
2. Look at your $f \times d$ column. What does it tell you about thickness?
3. Write the three rules you have found, one line each: **length**, **tension**, **thickness**.
4. **Challenge:** square each mean frequency and plot $f^2$ against $T$. What shape do you get now?
