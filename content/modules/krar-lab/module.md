Every krar (ክራር) player in an azmari bet tunes by ear, turning the strings until the qenet (ቅኝት, the set of notes a song uses) sounds right. In this module you find the physics and the maths behind that ear: why a shorter, tighter or thinner string sings higher, and how a box turns a quiet buzz into a full note.

1. **The singing line** (40 min). Hear one fishing line turn into a note. Predict what changes the pitch.
2. **Build a one-string box** (75 min). Make a monochord with a movable bridge and a water-bottle weight, and take your first frequency reading with a phone.
3. **Shorter is higher** (85 min). Measure frequency at different lengths, learn about frequency and period, and graph frequency against 1/length.
4. **Tighter and thicker** (85 min). Two fair tests: hang more water, then change the line's thickness.
5. **The box that sings** (50 min). Measure what the sound box really does, and make one string play another with resonance.
6. **Build and tune your krar** (105 min). Five strings, a box and a frame. Tune to tizita or bati using frequency ratios.
7. **Krar concert** (45 min). Play, explain, and show your graphs.

By the end you'll understand vibrations, frequency and period, resonance, fair tests and straight-line graphs, and you'll take home an instrument you built and tuned yourself.
