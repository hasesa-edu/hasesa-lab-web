## The question (5 minutes)

A strong brick is no use if the first kiremt storm washes it away. Which mix stands up best to water?

## Do: the drip test (25 minutes to set up, then it runs by itself)

This test is based on one engineers use for earth bricks.

1. Prop a matchbox brick on a slope, so one end is raised: for every 2 cm along, it rises 1 cm.
2. Fill the pinhole bottle with exactly **100 mL** of water. Hold it upside down in the stand, with the pinhole **40 cm** above the brick.
3. Let all 100 mL drip onto the same spot. It takes a while, so set up every mix at once, side by side.
4. When it has finished, push a toothpick gently into the deepest part of the pit until it stops. Mark the toothpick at the brick's surface and measure that length in mm. That is the **pit depth**.

## Do: the dunk test (10 minutes, while the drips run)

Take a broken half-bar of each mix from the bending test. Lower them into a basin of water at the same moment and start a stopwatch. Watch for a few minutes. Which one goes cloudy first? Which slumps into mud? Which keeps its shape longest?

Dry mud turns back into mud when it gets wet: drying only removes water, so no new substance is made. Read the parts of **Properties of matter** on physical and chemical properties. Is softening in water a physical or a chemical property of mud? Does the stabilised mix (F) behave as if something more than drying has happened?

## Theory: graphs

Read **Graphs and relationships** to learn which variable goes on which axis, how to draw a line or curve of best fit, and how to read its shape.

## Record: your graphs (35 minutes)

Use the class means from step 5 and today.

1. **Sand.** Plot mean shrinkage (y-axis) against cups of sand per cup of soil (x-axis) for mixes A (0), B (1) and C (2). Draw a line or curve of best fit. What does adding sand do to shrinkage?
2. **Straw.** Plot mean breaking load (y-axis) against cups of straw (x-axis) for mixes B (0), D (1) and E (2). What does straw do? Is more always better?
3. **Water.** Draw a bar chart of mean pit depth for all six mixes.

Under each graph, write one sentence: *As ___ goes up, ___ goes ___.* Then check: is the difference between two mixes bigger than their ranges? If not, you can't be sure it's real.
