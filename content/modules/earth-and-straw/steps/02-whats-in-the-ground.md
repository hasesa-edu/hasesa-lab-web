## Look closely (10 minutes)

Tip a spoonful of your dry soil onto white paper. Use a magnifying glass.

- What colours can you see?
- Can you see separate grains? Are they all the same size?
- Is there anything that isn't rock: roots, bits of leaf, insects?

Write down three things you notice.

## Theory: soils and clay

Read **Soils and clay**. You'll learn:

- that soil is a mixture of sand, silt, clay, organic matter, water and air,
- the sizes of sand, silt and clay particles,
- how to estimate texture by feel and with the ribbon test,
- how a jar test sorts particles by size.

Read **Mixtures and separation** too, for how sieving and settling pull a mixture apart.

## Do: the feel, ball and ribbon tests (25 minutes)

Gloves on. Take a walnut-sized lump of your soil and add water a few drops at a time, kneading it until it feels like stiff dough.

1. **Feel.** Rub it between your thumb and finger. Gritty, silky or sticky? It may be more than one.
2. **Ball.** Roll it into a ball about 3 cm across. Does it hold together, or fall apart?
3. **Ribbon.** Squeeze the ball out between your thumb and finger, pushing upwards, to make a flat ribbon. Let it hang over your finger until it breaks. Measure the longest piece in centimetres.
4. **Shine.** Rub the wet soil with your thumbnail. Does it go shiny? Shiny means lots of clay.

Do the same with the guide's building soil and with a lump of plain sand, so you can compare.

## Do: sieve it (10 minutes)

Outdoors, put two big spoonfuls of dry soil in a kitchen sieve over a sheet of paper and shake gently. What stays in the sieve? What falls through? Rub what falls through between your fingers. Can a sieve separate silt from clay? Why not?

## Do: set up the jar test (25 minutes)

1. Fill a straight-sided clear jar about **one-third** with your soil. Break up the lumps and pick out stones and roots.
2. Add water until the jar is about **three-quarters** full, and one teaspoon of detergent.
3. Put the lid on tightly and shake hard for **three minutes**. Take turns.
4. Put the jar down where nobody will move it. Start a stopwatch.
5. After **1 minute**, mark the top of the layer at the bottom with a marker pen on the glass, or a strip of tape. That layer is **sand**.
6. After **30 minutes**, mark again. The new layer on top of the sand is mostly **silt**.

Leave the jar for at least a day. Your guide will keep it safe. The clay settles slowly on top.

## Record

Copy your ribbon table into your notebook. Draw your jar as it looks now and label the layers. Predict: what percentage of your soil is clay? You'll check next time.
