## The mud rules first (10 minutes)

Your guide reads out the rules for this module. Copy them into the front of your notebook:

1. Gloves on whenever you touch soil, ash or dung.
2. Wash your hands with soap before you eat, drink or touch your face, and at the end of every session.
3. Glasses on for anything that breaks or splashes.
4. Mix dry soil outdoors and keep it damp. Don't breathe the dust.
5. Only use soil the guide has approved.

## Look (15 minutes)

Your guide puts two small mud bars on the table. Both were made from the **same soil**, in the same mould, and dried in the same place for two weeks.

Look closely, and handle them gently with gloves on:

- Measure each bar's length with a ruler. They started exactly the same length. Are they still?
- Count the cracks. Where are they: on the top, the sides, the ends?
- Is either bar curled or bent?
- Hold each one by the ends and press **gently** in the middle. Does either snap?

Your guide then shows you a piece of an old chika wall, or a photo of one. Find the straw in it.

## Predict (15 minutes)

On a page titled *The mystery*, write:

1. **What do you think was mixed into the strong bar that wasn't in the cracked one?** Write your best guess, then a second one.
2. **Why do you think mud cracks when it dries?**
3. **If you were building a chika bet, what would you mix with the soil, and why?**

## Questions (10 minutes)

As a team, list every question you have. For example:

- Why do builders put straw in chika?
- Is all soil the same? Why is some red and some black?
- How could we test which mud is strongest, without building a whole house?
- Why do some chika walls wash away in the rain and others last fifty years?

## Your homework: bring the ground (10 minutes)

Before the next session, collect **one cupful of soil** from home or the school compound:

- dig 20–30 cm down, below the dark top layer,
- never from a rubbish heap, latrine area or drain,
- put it in a plastic bag and label it with your name and where it came from.
