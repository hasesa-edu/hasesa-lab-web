## Do: read your jar (20 minutes)

Look at your jar from the side, at eye level. Don't shake it. The water above may still be cloudy: that is clay still floating.

1. Mark the top of the settled soil. The new layer above your silt mark is **clay**.
2. Measure each layer with a ruler, in millimetres: sand, silt, clay, and the total.
3. Work out each percentage:

$$\text{percentage of sand} = \frac{\text{sand layer}}{\text{total}} \times 100$$

and the same for silt and clay. Check: the three should add up to 100%.

| Soil | Sand (mm) | Silt (mm) | Clay (mm) | Total (mm) | Sand % | Silt % | Clay % |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | | | | |

**Example.** Sand 30 mm, silt 18 mm, clay 12 mm. Total = 60 mm. Clay = 12 ÷ 60 × 100 = 20%.

Compare with your clay estimate from the ribbon test. Were you close?

## The class board (10 minutes)

Every student writes their soil's clay % on the board, with where it came from. Which soil had the most clay? The least? Were the red soils and black soils different?

## Notice something puzzling

Clay is the smallest particle of all, smaller than dust. How can the tiniest grains be the ones that hold a brick together? Sand grains are much bigger and harder.

## Do: feel the glue (15 minutes)

1. Take two smooth tiles or two flat pieces of plastic. Press them together dry. Pull them apart. Easy.
2. Now put a few drops of water between them and press. Try to **pull** them straight apart. Then try to **slide** them.
3. Make two small balls, one of wet sand and one of wet clay soil. Let them sit while you read. Which one can you squash and reshape? Which crumbles?

Water spreads into a thin film between flat surfaces and holds them together. Clay is made of millions of tiny flat plates, each with a water film.

## Theory: how clay glues

Go back to **Soils and clay** and read *How clay glues* and *Why mud cracks*. You'll learn:

- that clay particles are flat plates with a huge surface area,
- why wet clay can be shaped and dry clay is hard,
- why clay shrinks as it dries, and why that cracks a brick,
- why builders add sand and straw.

## Do it better: the sausage test (15 minutes)

Make three mixes from the guide's building soil, each in a small cup: soil only; 1 cup soil to 1 cup sand; 1 cup soil to 2 cups sand. Add water until each is like stiff dough.

Roll each into a sausage about 1.5 cm thick and 15 cm long. Lay it on a flat board. Measure its length exactly, and label it. Your guide will keep them somewhere shady until next time, so you can measure how much each one shrinks.

Write your percentages and the working. Then answer in two sentences: **what does clay do in mud, and what problem does it cause?** Next time you design the real test.
