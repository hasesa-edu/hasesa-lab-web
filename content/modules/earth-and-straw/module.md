Look around Addis Ababa: next to the glass towers are thousands of chika bet, houses with walls of mud and teff straw on a wooden frame. Earth is the oldest and cheapest building material on the planet, and by some estimates a third of the world's people still live in earth buildings. But bad mud cracks, crumbles and washes away in the kiremt rains. In ten hours you find out why, and you work out the recipe for good mud yourself.

1. **Two bars of mud** (60 min). See the mystery, learn the rules, and bring soil from home.
2. **What's in the ground?** (90 min). Feel, ball and ribbon tests, and start a jar test.
3. **Clay glue** (75 min). Read your jar, calculate percentages and find out how clay sticks.
4. **Make your bricks** (100 min). Plan a fair test of six mixes and mould bricks and bars. Then they dry for about two weeks.
5. **Shrink, crack, break** (105 min). Measure shrinkage and cracks, then break bars with a bucket of sand and crush bricks on a bathroom scale.
6. **Rain on the wall** (75 min). A drip test for water resistance, then your graphs.
7. **Build it, back it** (105 min). Recommend the best mix with your data and plaster a small chika panel.

Your bricks need time to dry, so there is a gap of about two weeks between step 4 and step 5. By the end you'll understand soil texture, how clay binds, mixtures, forces and pressure, and how to run a fair test, and you'll have the evidence to say which mud a builder should use.
