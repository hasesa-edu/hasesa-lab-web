## Do: build a speaker from a cup

You'll make a working speaker from wire, a magnet and a paper cup.

1. **Wind the coil.** Wrap enamelled copper wire 40–60 times around a marker pen or a battery. Leave 20 cm free at each end. Slide it off and tape it so it holds its shape.
2. **Clean the ends.** The copper is coated in thin, clear varnish called **enamel**, which doesn't conduct. Rub the last 2 cm of each end with sandpaper until it's shiny all the way round.
3. **Glue the coil** flat onto the bottom of a paper cup (outside).
4. **Prepare the cable.** Take an old earphone cable and cut off the earbuds. Inside you'll find thin wires: usually a bare one (ground) and one or two coloured ones (left/right). Scrape or burn off any coating at the ends (the guide will help).
5. **Connect** the ground wire and one coloured wire to the two coil ends with alligator clips.
6. Plug into a phone, set the volume to about half, and play music.
7. **Hold a neodymium magnet** right against the coil (or under the cup bottom). Listen.

## Notice

- With no magnet: is there any sound?
- With the magnet: what can you hear? Put your finger lightly on the cup bottom.
- Try two magnets stacked. Try more turns of wire.

**Puzzle:** the phone only sends electricity down the wire. Nothing touches the cup. So how does electricity turn into movement, and then into sound?

## Theory: just enough

Read *Charge, current and voltage* and *Simple circuits*: the phone is pushing a small current around a loop through your coil. Then read *Electromagnetism*. A current in a coil makes a magnetic field. A magnet pushes on a current with a force $F = BIL$. Music is a current that keeps reversing direction, so the coil is pushed in, out, in, out, and the cup pushes the air.

## Do it better

- Work out how much wire is in your coil: turns × π × diameter.
- Use $F = BIL$ to estimate the force, with $B \approx 0.2$ T, $I \approx 0.02$ A and your $L$.
- Change one thing at a time (turns, cup size, magnet strength) and record how loud it is on a 1–5 scale.

## Connect to the radio

The earpiece on the guide's radio is a tiny speaker too, but a special kind (a **crystal** or **piezo** earpiece) that works on a minute amount of energy. Normal earbuds need far more current than a crystal radio can give, which is why they stay silent on it.

And one more idea from *Electromagnetism*: a **changing** magnetic field can push electrons in a wire. Keep that in mind for the antenna.
