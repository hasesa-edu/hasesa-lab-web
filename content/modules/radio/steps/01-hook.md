## Listen

Your guide has a small wooden board with a coil of copper wire, a little glass part, a knob and a long wire running out of the window. Take turns holding the earpiece to your ear while the guide slowly turns the knob.

You should hear a voice or music. It's quiet, but it's there.

Now look carefully. **There is no battery. No plug. No solar panel.** Check underneath the board if you like.

## Predict

In your notebook, on a fresh page titled *The mystery*, write:

1. **Where do you think the energy for the sound comes from?** Write your best guess, even if it feels silly. Write a second guess too.
2. **What do you think would happen if…**
   - the long wire outside was unclipped?
   - the guide turned the knob all the way?
   - the little glass part was turned round?
   - you used normal earbuds instead of this earpiece?

Then test some of them with your guide. Put a ✓ or ✗ next to each prediction. Wrong predictions are useful: they show you exactly what you don't understand yet.

## Look at the parts

Draw the radio. Label every part you can see, even if you don't know its name yet ("the coil", "the knob thing", "the glass bead"). Draw arrows for where you think wires connect.

Your guide will tell you the real names: **antenna, ground, tuning coil, variable capacitor, diode, earpiece**. Add them to your drawing.

## Questions

As a group, list every question you have. For example:

- How does sound travel through the air with no wire?
- Why do you only hear one station at a time?
- Why does the radio need a wire to the ground?
- What is the glass part for?

Write your group's questions on a big sheet. Over the next six steps, you'll answer every one of them by building your own radio. On the last day, you'll come back to this page and see how your guesses compare.

## The first clue

Here is one clue to take home: a radio station sends out **waves**. Read *Waves and vibrations* tonight. Next time, you'll make waves yourself.
