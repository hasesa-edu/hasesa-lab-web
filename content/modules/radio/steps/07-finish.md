## Do: build it properly

Move your radio onto a wooden board so it's tidy and reliable.

1. Screw or glue the coil and capacitor to the board.
2. Use drawing pins or screws as terminals: **ANT**, **GND** and two for the **earpiece**. Label them with a marker.
3. Wire it up following your circuit diagram: antenna → top of coil; capacitor across the coil; diode from the top of the coil to the earpiece; earpiece back to the ground side.
4. Check every joint: shiny, scraped copper firmly pressed or twisted together. Tape down sharp wire ends.
5. Connect the antenna and ground, and tune in.

## Do: log the stations

Slowly sweep the capacitor from one end to the other at each tap of your coil. For every station, record:

| Time | Tap | Dial mark | What I heard (language, music, talk) | Loudness 1–5 | Calculated $f$ (kHz) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

If you can, listen again after dark: medium-wave signals travel further at night. Are there new stations?

## Explain the whole chain

On a new page, draw the full path, from the transmitter tower to your ear. For each part, write one or two sentences in your own words:

1. **Transmitter** sends an AM radio wave at $3.0 \times 10^8$ m/s.
2. **Antenna and ground**: the wave pushes electrons up and down your wire.
3. **Coil and capacitor**: resonance picks one station, at $f = \frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{LC}}$.
4. **Diode**: lets one half of each cycle through.
5. **Earpiece**: follows the average, the envelope, which is the sound.

Then answer the first-day question: **where does the energy come from?**

## Go back to the mystery

Open your page from the first session. Look at your predictions. Which ones were right? For each wrong one, write what you now know instead.

## Sign-off

Show your guide:

- your radio receiving a station clearly;
- your explanation of tuning, and what happens when $C$ or $L$ changes;
- why the diode is needed;
- your antenna wavelength calculation;
- your station log;
- your drawing of the whole chain.

Congratulations: you have built a machine that turns invisible waves into sound, using only the energy that crossed the city to reach you.
