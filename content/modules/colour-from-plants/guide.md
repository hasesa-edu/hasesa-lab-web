# Colour from Plants: guide's notes

These notes are for the guide only. The module is about 10 hours, best run as six sessions of 1.5–2 hours for up to 16 students in four teams. **Step 7 must be at least seven days after step 5**, because the sunlight test needs a week. Steps 2, 5, 6 and 7 need a stove (gas or charcoal), a sink or a water tap, and a place to hang wet cloth.

All heating is done by you, using the **jar-in-a-pot** method: dye or mordant goes in glass jars, and the jars stand in a large pot of hot water. Students never stand over an open boiling dye pot, every jar is the same temperature (which makes the fair tests fair), and spills stay small. Put jars in while the pot water is still warm, not boiling, so they don't crack.

### 2. Scour the cotton (a week ahead)

New abujedi carries starch sizing and grease, which block dye. Wash all the cloth in hot water with laundry soap, rinse well, and dry. Then cut:

- strips 6 cm × 18 cm: 3 per dye × 6 dyes = 18, plus 6 spare;
- squares 6 cm × 6 cm: 10 for step 6;
- 30 cm × 30 cm pieces: one per student plus 2 spare, for step 7;
- a few small strips for the step 1 demo and the step 3 cloth-indicator test.

### 3. Make the ash water (two days ahead)

Stir 1 cup of sieved wood ash into 1 L of water in a plastic bucket, with goggles and gloves. Leave it to settle overnight. Pour off the clear liquid through a cloth, without disturbing the sludge. Expect pH about 10–11 (it can reach 12 with very fresh hardwood ash; if you have pH paper, check it, and dilute with an equal volume of water if above 11). Label the bottle **ASH WATER: ALKALINE, EYES**. Make about 2 L in total.

### 4. Mordant the cloth (the day before step 1, and again before step 5)

The step 1 demo needs one alum-mordanted strip (A) and one water-soaked strip (B). Step 5 needs three bundles. Weigh each bundle dry.

- **Alum**: 15% WOF. For 30 g of cloth, dissolve 4.5 g alum in 600 mL hot water.
- **Ash**: the same cloth mass in 600 mL ash water.
- **None**: the same cloth mass in 600 mL plain water.

Wet the cloth first, put each bundle in its own jar, and heat all three jars in the same water pot at 80–90 °C for 45 minutes. Leave them to cool overnight in the liquid. Squeeze, rinse lightly, and dye damp or dry and label. Keep the alum bundle clearly labelled; also mordant the 10 squares for step 6 and enough 30 cm pieces for step 7 (most teams choose alum; mordant two-thirds with alum, a few with ash, keep a few plain).

Write the WOF calculation on the alum jar's label; step 5 asks students to check it.

### 5. Pre-dye the step 3 cloth strips (a week ahead)

Dye small strips of alum-mordanted cotton with turmeric and with red cabbage, so step 3's "dyed-cloth indicator" works.

## Safety briefing (read in step 1, repeat when the stove or ash water is out)

- Goggles whenever ash water, alum solution, vinegar or baking soda is on the table. Gloves near any dye bath or mordant.
- You light the stove, you move pots and hot jars, with tongs and a cloth. Students stay an arm's length from the stove.
- Ash water is the main chemical hazard. It is roughly as alkaline as strong soap solution and can damage eyes. Pour it into small labelled cups for teams; the stock bottle never leaves your table. Eye splash: rinse 15 minutes under running water, then medical help if it still hurts. Skin: rinse with water; it feels slippery.
- Alum solution is mildly acidic and an irritant; it is not dangerous in these amounts, but it is never tasted.
- Only alum and ash. Do not bring copper, chrome, tin or iron salts into the room, even if a market seller suggests them. Iron (rusty water) is used by some dyers to darken colours; it is left out of this module on purpose.
- Nothing is eaten or drunk. Karkade and tea in the dye room are dye, not drinks. Label the pots "dye only".
- Rubbing alcohol (step 4 extension): stove off and no flames in the room.

## Session plans

### Step 1: The gold and the pale cloth (45 min)

Bring a real netela, gabi or kemis with a tibeb border. Heat one jar of onion-skin water (20 g skins in 500 mL) before students arrive, so it is already coloured when you drop in more skins. Dye strips A (alum) and B (water) together in the same jar for 15 minutes. A comes out a strong gold to orange-brown; B a paler yellow-tan. After a minute of hard rubbing with soap, B fades noticeably and A much less. Don't tell them what A was soaked in until step 5.

Common predictions: "A was a different cloth", "A was washed with soap first", "A was left in longer". All good: ask how they could test each.

### Step 2: Pulling out the colour (90 min)

Make sure each team weighs and records. Typical extract colours: onion red-brown to orange; turmeric opaque bright yellow (it doesn't fully dissolve, so it is partly a suspension); karkade deep red; cabbage purple to blue-purple (depends on your tap water's pH); coffee dark brown; tea red-brown; eucalyptus yellow-orange to rust.

**Typical colour strength** (last coloured cup $n$, dilution $2^{n-1}$), against white paper in 50 mL cups:

| Extract | Last coloured cup | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Karkade | 7–8 | 64–128 |
| Coffee | 7–8 | 64–128 |
| Turmeric | 6–7 | 32–64 |
| Onion | 6–7 | 32–64 |
| Tea | 5–6 | 16–32 |
| Red cabbage | 4–5 | 8–16 |
| Eucalyptus | 4–6 | 8–32 |

Results vary a lot with the batch; what matters is that teams apply the method the same way.

**Answers.** Concentrations: onion 40 g/L, turmeric 20 g/L, karkade 40 g/L, cabbage 200 g/L, coffee 100 g/L, tea 40 g/L, eucalyptus 100 g/L. Turmeric absorbs violet-blue, so it looks yellow; karkade absorbs green (and some blue), so it looks red. A thin layer absorbs less, so it looks paler and sometimes a different hue; that's why a drop on paper looks different from a full cup. For more precision: use smaller dilution steps (for example 2 parts in 3), or compare against a reference cup.

Discard cabbage and karkade extracts after two days (they ferment). Make fresh dye baths for steps 5–7.

### Step 3: Colours that change (75 min)

**Expected colours:**

| Extract | Vinegar | Water | Baking soda | Ash water |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Red cabbage | red-pink | purple | blue / blue-green | green, sometimes yellow-green |
| Karkade | bright red | red | dull purple-grey to blue-grey | green-grey to olive |
| Turmeric | yellow | yellow | yellow-orange | orange-red to red-brown |
| Onion | pale yellow-orange | orange-brown | deeper orange-brown | dark red-brown |
| Tea | paler, yellower | red-brown | darker red-brown | very dark red-brown |
| Coffee | slightly lighter | brown | slightly darker | slightly darker |
| Eucalyptus | paler yellow | orange-brown | darker | dark red-brown |

Cabbage and karkade are both anthocyanin indicators. Turmeric changes only above about pH 8, so baking soda gives a modest shift and ash water a dramatic one. Lemon in tea is the same effect: the tea's colour compounds are pH-sensitive, so tea lightens in acid.

On the dyed strips, turmeric turns red-brown under ash water and baking soda and stays yellow under vinegar; cabbage-dyed cloth turns pink under vinegar and green under ash.

**Answers.** (2) $10^{10.5-8.3} = 10^{2.2} \approx 160$ times fewer H⁺. (3) Avoid turmeric, cabbage and karkade for cloth washed with alkaline soap: the colour shifts (and, as they'll find, also fades).

### Step 4: Hidden colours (75 min)

Coffee filters are thick and slow; allow 20 minutes. Paper towel is faster but spreads spots. Small, repeated spots are the key to clear results; students rush this.

**Typical results in salt water** (vary with paper, so use these as a guide only):

- Karkade: a long pink-purple streak, centre around Rf 0.6–0.8.
- Red cabbage: a blue-purple to greenish streak, Rf around 0.6–0.8 (the paper and salt water shift its colour, which is a nice talking point).
- Onion: a yellow band moving well (Rf about 0.7–0.9) and a brown or orange part staying lower (Rf 0.2–0.5): a mixture.
- Tea and coffee: brown streaks with a yellowish front; tea often shows a yellow component separating ahead of the brown.
- Turmeric: stays at or near the start line (Rf about 0–0.1). In alcohol it moves as a bright yellow spot (Rf about 0.7–0.9), because curcumin dissolves in alcohol but hardly in water.

**Answers.** Mixtures: onion, tea, usually coffee and eucalyptus; anthocyanin extracts often streak rather than separate cleanly, which students may read as one substance. Accept either if justified. (3) $0.65 \times 8.0 = 5.2$ cm.

### Step 5: Making it stick (105 min)

Students check the WOF: $0.15 \times 30 = 4.5$ g. Controlled variables to look for: same cloth, same strip size, same dye bath and volume, same temperature, same time, same rinse, same drying place, same mordanting time and temperature.

Split the six dyes across four teams (for example: team 1 onion and eucalyptus; team 2 turmeric; team 3 karkade and cabbage; team 4 coffee and tea). That's 18 jars: three pots of six.

Strips may not be dry before the session ends. Dry them in the sun for an afternoon (only a few hours: don't let the drying become an unequal light test), cut them, and put up the sun cards yourself if needed. Record the start date and weather each day of the sun week.

**Expected colours after dyeing:**

| Dye | Alum | Ash water | None |
|---|---|---|---|
| Onion | deep gold to orange-brown | browner, duller | pale yellow-tan |
| Turmeric | bright yellow | orange to rust (shifts back toward yellow on rinsing) | yellow, slightly paler |
| Karkade | mauve to purple-grey | grey-green to grey-brown | pale pinkish-grey |
| Red cabbage | lilac to blue-grey | green-grey to pale yellow | pale grey-lilac |
| Coffee | beige-tan | slightly darker tan | beige |
| Tea | tan, slightly golden | darker brown | tan |
| Eucalyptus | yellow-orange to rust | darker brown-orange | pale yellow-brown |

### Step 6: Deeper shade (90 min)

**Dilution table answers:** 40 g/L: 75 mL + 75 mL water; 20 g/L: 37.5 + 112.5; 10 g/L: 18.75 (accept 19) + 131; 5 g/L: 9.4 (accept 9) + 141.

**Expected darkness values** (onion on alum cotton, undyed abujedi about 220–230 brightness):

| Concentration (g/L) | 5 | 10 | 20 | 40 | 80 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Darkness $D$ | 20–30 | 35–45 | 55–70 | 80–95 | 95–115 |

The curve rises steeply at first and levels off: doubling from 5 to 10 g/L nearly doubles $D$; doubling from 40 to 80 adds only 15–25%. The fibre (and the aluminium on it) can hold only so much dye. A sensible "deep without waste" choice is 30–40 g/L. Students should draw a curve, not a straight line through the origin, and not dot-to-dot.

Wash test: by the fifth wash, karkade and cabbage water stays visibly coloured; tea and coffee barely tint the water.

### Step 7: Your recipe, your cloth (120 min)

**Expected fastness scores after 7 days of Addis sun and 5 washes** (rough, by eye):

| Dye + alum | Light | Wash |
|---|---|---|
| Onion | 3–4 | 4 |
| Tea, coffee, eucalyptus | 3–4 | 3–4 |
| Turmeric | 1–2 (fades to pale cream in a sunny week) | 2–3 |
| Karkade | 1–2 | 2 |
| Red cabbage | 1–2 | 2 |

Without mordant, wash scores drop by about one point for onion, eucalyptus and anthocyanins, but much less for tea and coffee (tannins hold on by themselves). Ash water mostly shifts the shade; its wash scores are usually close to "none", and turmeric on ash often shifts back to yellow. **Light fastness depends mainly on the dye, wash fastness depends on dye and mordant**: that's the conclusion you want teams to reach.

Typical % colour kept: onion + alum after washing 80–90%, after sun 65–80%; turmeric after sun 20–40%; karkade after washing 30–50%.

Most teams should end up choosing onion skins (or eucalyptus) with alum at 30–40 g/L. A team that chooses turmeric for its brightness is fine if they show they know it will fade and say so.

For the final dyeing, 30 cm × 30 cm of abujedi is about 12–15 g. Use a large jar or a small pot with about 1 L of bath, at 30–40 g/L onion (30–40 g of skins), heated 45 minutes. Keep the cloth moving or it comes out blotchy.

## Where students get stuck

- **Confusing colour with strength.** A dark extract isn't always a strong dye on cloth (coffee is very dark but gives a pale beige). Ask: "What's the colour on the cloth, not in the jar?"
- **The phone photo.** They photograph swatches one by one, so auto-exposure changes the brightness and the numbers are meaningless. Insist on one photo with the undyed reference in it, in shade.
- **Reading RGB in the wrong place.** Pick the centre of the swatch, not an edge or a fold. Better still, read three points and take the mean.
- **Rf measured to the top of the spot.** It's to the centre.
- **C₁V₁ = C₂V₂ with V₂ and water mixed up.** V₂ is the total; water added = V₂ − V₁.
- **Thinking ash water is a mordant like alum.** It changes pH and cleans the fibre; it doesn't bind dye the way Al³⁺ does. Their own data should show this.

## Questions to ask

- "If you add vinegar to the turmeric cloth after the ash, what happens? Is the dye gone, or changed?"
- "Why does the covered half of the sun card count as a control?"
- "Your graph levels off. Where is the extra dye going?"
- "Tea stains your cup forever, but karkade doesn't. What does that tell you about tannins?"
- "If you were a weaver selling netela, which colour would you never sell as 'colourfast'?"

## Judging the sign-off items

- **extracts:** a table with plant, mass, volume, time and colour for at least four extracts. The explanation must say the dye *absorbs* some colours and we see the rest; "the dye is yellow because it has yellow in it" is not enough.
- **ph-colours:** four liquids with the water cup used as the comparison. They name cabbage and karkade (and turmeric) as indicators and link the colour change to the molecule gaining or losing H⁺.
- **chromatography:** at least three strips with pencil start line and solvent front marked, Rf values with working (two decimals, between 0 and 1), and a correct judgement of which are mixtures.
- **mordant-fair-test:** the plan lists at least five controlled variables; the table has grey scores and % kept for their own dyes. Check one % kept calculation by hand.
- **shade-graph:** working for at least one dilution shown; correct axes with units; a smooth curve; a sentence that says it levels off and why.
- **final-cloth:** the cloth is reasonably even (small blotches are fine). The recipe states mordant, WOF, dye and concentration, and is backed by at least two numbers from their own data.
- **safety:** goggles and gloves without reminders whenever ash water or dye baths were out. If a student needed more than one reminder, talk it through before signing.
