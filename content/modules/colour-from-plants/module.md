For centuries, almost every colour on cloth came from plants, insects and minerals. In ten hours you'll pull colour out of things from the kitchen and the market, find out what makes it change and what makes it stay, and prove your best recipe with data.

1. **The gold and the pale cloth** (45 min). Watch a dye bath work, and predict why one cloth kept its colour.
2. **Pulling out the colour** (90 min). Extract six dyes with one fair recipe and measure how strong each is.
3. **Colours that change** (75 min). Test every dye with vinegar, water, baking soda and ash water.
4. **Hidden colours** (75 min). Run chromatograms on coffee filters and calculate Rf values.
5. **Making it stick** (105 min). A fair test of alum, ash water and no mordant, and the start of a week-long sunlight test.
6. **Deeper shade** (90 min). A dilution series, a graph of shade against concentration, and the wash test.
7. **Your recipe, your cloth** (120 min). Score the fastness tests, choose a recipe, dye your cloth and present the evidence.

Step 7 happens at least seven days after step 5, so the sunlight test has time to work.

By the end you'll understand why things have colour, extraction, pH indicators, chromatography, mordants, fastness and dilution, and you'll have a dye book and a cloth to show for it.
