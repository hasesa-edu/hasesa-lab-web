## Goggles on

Ash water is strongly alkaline. Goggles stay on for the whole step. The guide pours the ash water into small labelled cups for each team. Never carry the big bottle.

## Do: the four-liquid test (30 minutes)

You need four test liquids, in labelled cups:

| Liquid | About pH |
|---|---|
| White vinegar | 2.5–3 |
| Plain water (the control) | 7 |
| Baking soda, 1 teaspoon in 100 mL water | 8.3 |
| Ash water, made by the guide | 10–11 |

For each extract from step 2:

1. Put 4 spoons (or 20 mL) of the extract into each of four clear cups, side by side on white paper.
2. Add 2 spoons (10 mL) of vinegar to the first cup, water to the second, baking soda solution to the third, ash water to the fourth. Use a clean spoon or syringe for each liquid.
3. Stir. Compare each cup with the **water** cup, which shows the colour with no change of pH.
4. Take one phone photo of the four cups together.

Record in a table:

| Extract | Vinegar | Water (control) | Baking soda | Ash water |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Red cabbage | | | | |

## Notice something puzzling

Some extracts changed colour completely. Others hardly changed, or just got lighter or darker. The cabbage and the karkade, from very different plants, behave almost the same. Why?

And here's one from home: when you squeeze lemon into black tea, it goes paler. Is that the same thing?

## Theory (15 minutes)

Read **Acids, bases and pH**, especially **indicators**, and the section *Colour and pH* in **Dyes and mordants**. You'll learn:

- what acids and bases are, and what the pH scale means,
- that each step of 1 on the pH scale is a factor of 10,
- why some dye molecules change colour when they gain or lose H⁺,
- that karkade and red cabbage share the same family of colour molecules, the anthocyanins.

Then read *Making a test trustworthy* in **Chemical tests**: why you always need a control.

## Do it better: a dyed-cloth indicator (20 minutes)

Does a dye still change colour once it is on cloth?

1. Your guide gives each team small strips of cotton dyed last week with turmeric and with red cabbage.
2. With a dropper or syringe, put one drop of vinegar, one of water, one of baking soda solution and one of ash water on each strip, well apart.
3. Wait two minutes. What happens?

Now think like a weaver. Laundry soap is alkaline (about pH 9–10). What might happen to a turmeric-yellow border the first time it's washed with soap?

## Record

Answer in your notebook:

1. Which dyes are good pH indicators? Which are not?
2. Ash water is pH 10.5 and baking soda is pH 8.3. How many times fewer H⁺ ions does ash water have? (Hint: $10^{2.2}$.)
3. Which of these dyes would you **not** use for a cloth that will be washed with soap, and why?
