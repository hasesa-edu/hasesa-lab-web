## Back to the mystery

In step 1, cloth A kept its gold colour and cloth B washed almost white. The guide has now told you: A was soaked in **alum** the night before. B was soaked in plain water. Today you find out how much difference a mordant makes, for every dye, and whether wood-ash water works too.

## Theory: mordants (15 minutes)

Read **Dyes and mordants**, the sections *Dye, pigment and fibre* and *Mordants*. You'll learn:

- that cotton is cellulose, and why most plant dyes hold onto it weakly,
- how aluminium ions from alum link fibre and dye,
- why tannins in tea and coffee hold on by themselves,
- how to calculate mordant as a percentage of the weight of fibre (WOF).

**Calculate.** The guide mordanted 30 g of cotton at 15% WOF. How much alum was that? Check your answer with the guide's notes on the jar.

## Plan a fair test (15 minutes)

The guide has three bundles of cotton strips, each 6 cm × 18 cm, prepared yesterday in exactly the same way except for one thing:

- **Alum**: soaked in hot alum solution.
- **Ash**: soaked in hot ash water.
- **None**: soaked in hot plain water.

Write a fair-test plan in five lines (look at **Fair tests and averages** if you need to):

1. **Question:** Does the mordant change how well a plant dye stays on cotton?
2. **I will change:** ___
3. **I will measure:** the colour, and how much of it stays after sunlight and after washing.
4. **I will keep the same:** ___ (list at least five things).
5. **Control:** for each strip, one third that is never washed and never exposed.

Show the plan to the guide.

## Do: dye the strips (40 minutes)

The class shares the work: each team takes one or two dyes.

1. For your dye, label three jars: *alum*, *ash*, *none*. Put 150 mL of fresh dye bath (from the guide, made with the step 2 recipe) in each.
2. Put one strip of each kind in its jar. Push it under with a clean stick.
3. The guide stands all the jars in pots of hot water (80–90 °C, not boiling) for **30 minutes**. Gloves on if you stir.
4. The guide lifts the jars out. When cool enough, lift the strips out with tongs, squeeze, and rinse each under the tap for the **same** time: count to 20.
5. Hang them to dry on a line, labelled with a pencil on a paper tag.

While you wait, write down your predictions: which mordant will hold each dye best?

## Do it better: start the sunlight test (20 minutes)

When the strips are dry (the guide may dry them in the sun, then you finish next session), cut each strip into three 6 cm squares:

- **Control**: goes in a labelled envelope, in the dark.
- **Sun**: stapled to white card with **half** covered by folded black paper or foil.
- **Wash**: kept in a labelled envelope for the wash test in step 6.

The guide puts the sun cards in the sunniest window, or outdoors under a sheet of glass, for **seven days**.

## Record

Draw the results table you'll fill in over the next week. One row for every dye and mordant:

| Dye | Mordant | Colour after dyeing | Sun: grey score | Sun: % kept | Wash: grey score | Wash: % kept |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Fill in the first column of colours now, in words (for example "deep gold", "pale grey-pink"). Which mordant gave the deepest colour for each dye? Which mordant changed the **shade**?
