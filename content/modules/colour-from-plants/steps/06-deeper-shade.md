## The question

A weaver wants a pale gold thread and a deep gold one. Do you need twice as many onion skins for a colour twice as deep? Is there a point where adding more skins is wasted?

## Plan the dilution series (15 minutes)

The guide has made a strong onion-skin bath: **80 g of skins in 1 L**, so 80 g/L. You'll make five baths of **150 mL** each, at 80, 40, 20, 10 and 5 g/L.

Use $C_1 V_1 = C_2 V_2$ from **Solutions and concentration** to fill in this table before you measure anything:

| Bath | Concentration $C_2$ (g/L) | Volume of strong bath $V_1$ (mL) | Water to add (mL) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 80 | 150 | 0 |
| 2 | 40 | | |
| 3 | 20 | | |
| 4 | 10 | | |
| 5 | 5 | | |

Show your working for bath 4. The guide checks the table before you pour.

## Do: dye the series (30 minutes)

1. Measure each bath into a labelled jar with a syringe or measuring jug.
2. Put one 6 cm × 6 cm square of **alum-mordanted** cotton in each. Same size, same cloth, same mordant.
3. The guide heats the jars in hot water for 30 minutes, exactly as in step 5.
4. Rinse each square for a count of 20 and dry it on the line. Name the controlled variables in your notebook while you wait.

## Do: the wash test (25 minutes)

Take your **wash** squares from step 5. For each one:

1. Put it in a cup with 200 mL of warm tap water and a 1 cm × 1 cm piece of the class's laundry soap.
2. Rub it between your fingers for **one minute**, counting.
3. Rinse for a count of 20. Squeeze.
4. Repeat until it has had **five washes**, with fresh soapy water each time.

Hang them to dry next to their labels. Notice which washing water turns coloured. That colour is dye leaving the cloth.

## Theory: graphs (5 minutes)

Look back at **Graphs and relationships**: which variable goes on which axis, and how to draw a smooth curve of best fit rather than joining dots.

## Record (15 minutes)

When your shade squares are dry (or next session), photograph all five in one photo, with a square of **undyed** abujedi beside them, in shade. With a colour-picker app, read R, G and B at the centre of each square. Then, as in **Dyes and mordants**:

| Concentration (g/L) | R | G | B | Brightness | Darkness $D$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 (undyed) | | | | | 0 |
| 5 | | | | | |

Draw a graph of **darkness** (y-axis) against **concentration** (x-axis). Then answer:

1. Between 5 and 10 g/L, did doubling the dye double the darkness? Between 40 and 80 g/L?
2. Describe the shape of your graph in one sentence, and explain it using the idea that cotton has only so many places for dye to attach.
3. Which concentration would you choose for a deep shade without wasting skins? Read it from your graph.

Stick the five squares in a row in your notebook. That row is your own **depth scale** for onion gold.
