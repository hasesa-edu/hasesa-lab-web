## Score the fastness tests (30 minutes)

Collect the sun cards from the window and your dry wash squares. Lift the cover off each sun square.

**By eye.** Compare each tested square with its **control** (the covered half, or the square from the envelope). Give it a grey-scale score:

| Score | Change |
|---|---|
| 5 | no visible change |
| 4 | slight |
| 3 | clear |
| 2 | big |
| 1 | almost all gone |

Two people score each square separately, without talking. If you disagree by more than one, ask a third person.

**By numbers.** Photograph each tested square beside its control and a piece of undyed cloth, all in one photo, in shade. Read R, G and B for each, and work out

$$\text{colour kept (\%)} = \frac{D_{\text{after}}}{D_{\text{before}}} \times 100$$

Fill in the class results table from step 5. Mark the best result for each dye.

## Choose your recipe (15 minutes)

As a team, decide on a recipe for a cloth that will be worn and washed. Write it like a dyer:

- **Fibre:** cotton, mass ___ g
- **Mordant:** ___, at ___% WOF, so ___ g
- **Dye:** ___, at ___ g/L (from your shade graph), in ___ mL
- **Time and temperature:** ___

Under it, write **three pieces of evidence** from your own tables that support each choice, with numbers.

## Dye your cloth (45 minutes)

Each student gets a piece of cotton about 30 cm × 30 cm (a small netela-style scarf end, a napkin or a bag panel). The guide has mordanted pieces with alum and with ash water, and kept some plain, so you can use the mordant you chose.

1. Weigh your cloth and check your recipe's numbers still fit.
2. Wet the cloth first: dry cloth takes dye unevenly.
3. Optional pattern: fold, twist or tie parts of the cloth tightly with string. Tied parts stay pale.
4. The guide heats your dye in a pot or a big jar. Put the cloth in, push it under, and keep it moving gently with a stick (gloves on). Keep it there for the time in your recipe.
5. Lift out, rinse until the water runs nearly clear, untie and hang to dry.

## Back to the mystery (10 minutes)

Open *The mystery* from step 1. Next to each guess, write ✓ or ✗ and what you now know. How good was your ranking of the six dyes?

Answer properly: **why did cloth A keep its colour and cloth B lose it?** Use the words *alum, aluminium ion, fibre, dye molecule, insoluble, wash*.

## Present the evidence (20 minutes)

Lay out your dried cloth with your dye book. Your guide will ask each of you to:

- explain why a dye looks the colour it does,
- show one pH colour chart and say which dyes are indicators,
- show a chromatogram and its Rf values,
- explain your mordant fair test, and show your fastness scores and % colour kept,
- show your shade graph and explain its shape,
- justify your recipe using your own numbers, and say what you'd test next.

Take your cloth home. It's yours.
