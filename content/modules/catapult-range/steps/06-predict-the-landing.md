## Your graph is a calibration chart (10 minutes)

In step 3 you drew mean range against stretch, and you know which pull-back mark gives which stretch. Put them together: make a new graph of **mean range** (y-axis) against **pull-back setting** (x-axis), for the angle you will use on range day, with a smooth line of best fit.

Now you can read it backwards. Find a distance on the y-axis, go across to your line, then down to the x-axis. That is the setting to use.

This is called **calibrating** your catapult. Scales, thermometers and fuel pumps are calibrated the same way.

If you changed anything since step 3 (new rubber, a different tilt), fire three shots at three settings first and redraw the graph. Rubber gets weaker with use.

## Do: predict, then fire (25 minutes)

Your guide calls out five distances, one at a time, for example 2.5 m, 3.2 m, 4.0 m, 4.6 m and a distance **between** two of your settings, such as 3.7 m.

For each distance:

1. Use your graph to choose a setting. If it falls between two marks on your card, draw an extra mark halfway. Write the setting down **before** you fire.
2. Place a chalk cross on the ground at the target distance.
3. Fire three shots. Measure how far each one lands from the cross: + if it went too far, − if too short.

## Do it better: speed × time (10 minutes)

Use your step 5 results to check one prediction a different way. If you know the horizontal speed and flight time for a setting, $R = \text{speed} \times T$ should give its range. Do the two methods agree?

## Record (15 minutes)

Table: target distance, predicted setting, three misses (+ or −), mean miss.

Answer:

- Were your misses mostly too long, mostly too short, or mixed? Mostly one way means your graph needs moving; mixed means the catapult itself is not repeatable enough.
- What is the **spread** of your three shots at one setting? Your aim can never be better than that. How could you make the catapult more repeatable?
- Why is it risky to aim at a distance **further** than any you have tested? (Look up *extrapolation* in **Graphs and relationships**.)

Make one improvement before range day: a firmer base, a tighter cup, fresh rubber, or more marks on your scale. Then re-check two settings.
