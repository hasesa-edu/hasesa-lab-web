## Do: two coins (10 minutes)

Put two coins on the edge of a table. Hold a ruler flat on the table behind them and swing it so it flicks one coin forward and knocks the other one just off the edge. Listen. Do you hear one click or two?

Try it five times. What does it tell you about sideways motion and falling?

## Theory: projectile motion

Read **Projectile motion**. You'll learn:

- why a projectile's path is a curve,
- that the sideways motion and the up-and-down motion don't affect each other,
- that, ignoring air, the sideways speed stays the same the whole flight,
- how to work out the horizontal speed: $\text{horizontal speed} = \dfrac{\text{range}}{\text{flight time}}$,
- how to work out the upward speed at launch: $g \times \dfrac{T}{2}$.

## Do: time the flight (25 minutes)

A bean bag flight lasts less than a second. A stopwatch is too slow for that, because your reaction time is about 0.2 s at the start **and** at the end. A phone video is much better.

1. Set your catapult on a fixed setting (pull-back 40, flat ground).
2. One person films from the side, far enough back to see the launch **and** the landing in the picture. Hold the phone still, or rest it on a bag.
3. Fire five shots. Measure the range of each one.
4. Play each video back frame by frame (most phones let you drag slowly through the clip). Count the frames from the moment the bag leaves the cup to the moment it touches the ground.
5. Flight time = number of frames ÷ frames per second. Normal phone video is 30 frames per second; slow motion is often 120 or 240. Check your phone's settings.

**Example.** 27 frames at 30 frames per second: $T = 27 \div 30 = 0.90$ s.

No video? Use a stopwatch, but time 10 shots and take the mean. Then compare with a team that used video.

## Calculate (20 minutes)

For each shot, in a table:

| Shot | Range $R$ (m) | Frames | Flight time $T$ (s) | Horizontal speed $R \div T$ (m/s) | Upward speed $9.8 \times T/2$ (m/s) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Then find the mean of each speed.

**Check yourself:** the bag starts at about the same height as it lands, so the trip up and the trip down should take about the same time. Look at your video: is the top of the flight about halfway through?

## Extension: the real launch speed and angle (10 minutes)

The horizontal speed and the upward speed are two sides of a right-angled triangle.

- **Launch angle:** $\tan\theta = \dfrac{\text{upward speed}}{\text{horizontal speed}}$. Compare with the launch angle you measured in step 4.
- **Launch speed:** the long side, $\sqrt{(\text{horizontal})^2 + (\text{upward})^2}$.

## Record

Write out one full calculation, with units on every line. Then answer: at the top of its flight, is the bean bag moving? How fast, and in which direction? Check what you wrote on *The mystery* page.
