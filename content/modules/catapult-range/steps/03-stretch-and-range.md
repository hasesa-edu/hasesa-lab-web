## The question

In step 1, the guide's short pull-back went a short way and the long one went much further. But **how** does range depend on pull-back? Twice the pull-back, twice the range? More? Less? Let's find out.

## Theory: fair tests and averages

Read **Fair tests and averages**. You'll learn:

- the independent, dependent and controlled variables,
- why you need at least three repeats,
- how to work out a mean and a range,
- what to do with an outlier.

Then read the energy section of **Projectile motion**: how the stretched rubber stores energy and passes it to the arm and the bean bag.

## Plan (10 minutes)

Write a fair-test plan in five lines:

1. **Question:** How does the pull-back setting affect the range?
2. **I will change:** the pull-back setting. Choose at least five, for example 20, 30, 40, 50 and 60.
3. **I will measure:** the range, in metres, to where the bean bag first lands.
4. **I will keep the same:** ___ (list at least four things).
5. **Repeats:** three shots per setting.

Show your plan to the guide before you fire.

## Do: measure the stretch (10 minutes)

For each setting, measure the length of one rubber strip with a ruler while a partner holds the arm at that mark. The **stretch** (extension) is the length now minus the length with the arm at 0. Write it in your table. More pull-back, more stretch, more energy stored.

## Do: fire (30 minutes)

Lay the tape measure along the ground, starting at the front of your base. One person fires, one watches where the bag lands and calls out the distance, one records. Swap jobs every five shots.

Record **every** shot, even the strange ones. Note anything unusual: the base moved, the bag hit the stop bar, a gust of wind.

## Record (25 minutes)

1. Make a results table: pull-back setting, stretch (cm), range 1, range 2, range 3, mean range, spread (biggest minus smallest).
2. Read **Graphs and relationships**. Draw a graph of **mean range** (y-axis) against **stretch** (x-axis). Draw one line or smooth curve of best fit. Don't join the dots.
3. Answer:
   - Is your graph a straight line, a curve that bends up, or a curve that flattens off?
   - Does doubling the stretch double the range?
   - Where does the energy go that **doesn't** end up in the bean bag? (Listen to the bang when the arm hits the stop bar.)

Keep this graph safe. On range day, it is your aiming chart.
