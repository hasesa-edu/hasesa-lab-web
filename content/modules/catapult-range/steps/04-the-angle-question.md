## The question

In step 1, everyone guessed the best launch angle. Some of you said 90°, some 45°, some 30°. Who is right? You'll answer it with a fair test, not an argument.

## Find your launch angle (15 minutes)

The bean bag leaves the cup at right angles to the arm, at the moment the arm hits the stop bar. So:

1. Rest the arm against the stop bar. Measure the angle between the **arm** and the **ground** with a protractor. Call it $a$.
2. The launch angle on flat ground is about $90° - a$. An arm that stops at 60° from the ground launches at about 30°.

To check, film a shot from the side, a few metres away, with a metre stick in the picture. Pause the video just after the bag leaves the cup, hold a protractor against the screen and measure the angle of its path. Is it close to $90° - a$?

## Change the angle without changing anything else (10 minutes)

If you move the stop bar, you also change how far the rubber is stretched when the bag leaves. That's two changes at once: not fair.

Instead, **tilt the whole catapult**. Put blocks or folded cardboard wedges under the **front** of the base to aim higher, or under the **back** to aim lower. Measure the tilt with a protractor laid against the base.

$$\text{launch angle} \approx (90° - a) + \text{tilt}$$

Plan five angles from about 20° to 70°, for example 20°, 30°, 40°, 50°, 60° and 70°.

## Plan (10 minutes)

Write your five-line fair-test plan. Your controlled variables must include the **pull-back setting**, the **bean bag** and the **rubber strips**. Choose a pull-back setting in the middle of your scale, so even your best angle lands on the tape. Show the plan to the guide.

## Do: fire (25 minutes)

Three shots at each angle. Before each shot check: same pull-back mark, same bean bag, base not sliding, everyone behind the line, glasses on.

## Record (15 minutes)

1. Table: launch angle, range 1, 2, 3, mean, spread.
2. Graph: **mean range** (y-axis) against **launch angle** (x-axis), with a smooth curve.
3. Read the **launch angle** section of **Projectile motion**. Then answer:
   - Which angle threw furthest? Is it 45°, or a bit lower? Why might a light bean bag or paper ball do best below 45°?
   - Find two angles on your curve that gave about the same range. Do they add up to about 90°?
   - Why does a very low angle throw short? Why does a very high angle throw short? Use the words *time in the air* and *sideways speed*.

**Try it:** fire a paper ball and a ping-pong ball at your best angle. Do they go as far as the bean bag? What is slowing them?
