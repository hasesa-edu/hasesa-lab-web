## The challenge (25 minutes)

Your guide places a basin somewhere down the range. You don't know the distance until you see it, and it is different for every team.

1. **Measure.** Run the tape from the front of your base to the middle of the basin. Write the distance down.
2. **Predict.** Use your calibration graph to choose a pull-back setting. Write it on the class board **before** you fire, next to your team name.
3. **Fire.** Three shots. Record where each lands: in, touching the rim, or how far short or long.
4. **Adjust?** If your first shot misses, you may change the setting for shot two, but write down why.

The class board shows every team's distance, predicted setting and result.

## Back to the mystery (15 minutes)

Open the page titled *The mystery* from step 1. Next to each answer, write ✓ or ✗ and what you now know. Answer question 5 properly: **how did the guide know which mark to pull back to?**

Then explain in your own words what happens from the moment you pull back to the moment the bag lands. Use these words: *elastic store, kinetic, stretch, launch angle, gravity, time in the air, horizontal speed*.

## Explain it to the guide (20 minutes)

Your guide will ask each of you about your catapult and your results. Be ready to:

- show that your catapult is repeatable, with three shots close together,
- show your stretch-and-range table and graph, and say what pattern it shows,
- show your angle fair test and say which angle was best, and why very low and very high angles throw short,
- show one launch-speed calculation from your video,
- explain how you chose your setting for the target, and why you hit or missed,
- say one thing you would change to make your catapult more accurate, and how you would test it.

Take your catapult home. It's yours. Remember the rules still apply at home: soft things only, and never aim at anyone.
