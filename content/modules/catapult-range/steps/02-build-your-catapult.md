## The design (10 minutes)

Every team builds the same basic catapult, then improves it. Its parts are:

- a **base**: a rectangle of slats, about 40 × 20 cm, so it doesn't tip or slide,
- a **pivot** near the front of the base: a nail or bolt through the bottom end of the **arm**, about 35 cm long, so the arm can swing from lying flat (pointing backwards) up towards upright,
- two **uprights**, about 20 cm tall, fixed about 10 cm **behind** the pivot, with a **stop bar** across their tops. The arm hits the stop bar while it is still leaning back, at about 60° to the ground,
- a **cup**: a spoon or bottle cap taped firmly to the end of the arm,
- a **rubber strip**: rubber bands or inner-tube strips joining the arm to the top of the uprights. Pull the arm back and the rubber stretches. Let go and it pulls the arm forward until it hits the stop bar, and the bean bag flies on.

Sketch it in your notebook before you start, with lengths.

No wood? A strong cardboard box works as the frame: the arm pivots on a pencil pushed through both sides near one end, and a ruler taped across the top of the box, a little behind the pivot, is the stop bar.

## Build (45 minutes)

1. **Base.** Lay out two long slats and join them with two short crossbars. Your guide nails or screws the joints, or you lash them tightly with string and tape.
2. **Arm and pivot.** Your guide drills a hole near one end of the arm and pushes a nail or bolt through it and into two small blocks fixed near the front of the base. The arm must swing freely but not wobble sideways.
3. **Uprights and stop bar.** Fix the two uprights about 10 cm behind the pivot, one each side of the arm, then the stop bar across their tops. Swing the arm up: it should hit the stop bar while leaning back at about 60° to the ground. Make the frame rigid: a wobbly frame gives wobbly results.
4. **Cup.** Tape the spoon to the top of the arm, bowl facing back. Wrap it tightly so it doesn't move.
5. **Rubber.** Loop a rubber strip from the top of each upright down to the middle of the arm. Start with **one** strip each side. Tie or tape it so it can't slip.

## The pull-back scale (15 minutes)

A catapult is only useful if you can fire **exactly the same shot** again. For that you need a scale.

1. Tape a piece of cardboard upright beside the arm, so the arm sweeps past it.
2. With the arm resting against the stop bar, draw a line along the arm on the card and label it **0**.
3. Pull the arm back slowly. Every time it moves back by **10°** (use a protractor at the pivot), draw a line. Label them 10, 20, 30… up to where the arm is nearly flat.

Now every shot has a setting: "pull back to 40" means the arm lines up with the 40 mark.

## First shots (15 minutes)

Make **bean bags**: 20 g of dried beans tied in a scrap of cloth. Weigh them so every bag in your team is the same.

On the range, behind the firing line, with glasses on: fire three shots at the same setting (try 40). Measure each range with the tape, from the front of the base to **where the bean bag first touches the ground**.

Are the three shots close? If they are more than 50 cm apart, find out why. Does the base slide? Does the cup wobble? Does the rubber slip? Fix it.

## Record

Draw your catapult from the side, with measurements: arm length, pivot height, stop bar height, number of rubber strips, bean bag mass. Write down where the energy starts and where it goes when you fire, using the words *elastic*, *kinetic* and *stretch*.
