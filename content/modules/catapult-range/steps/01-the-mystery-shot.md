## The range rules first (10 minutes)

Before anything is fired, your guide reads out the range rules. Copy them into the front of your notebook:

1. Soft things only: paper balls, bean bags, ping-pong balls. Never stones or anything hard.
2. Never aim at a person or an animal, loaded or not.
3. Safety glasses on whenever any catapult is loaded or fired.
4. Everyone behind the firing line. Nobody crosses it until the guide calls **"range clear"**.
5. Fingers and faces out of the arm's swing.

People have thrown things for a very long time. Shepherds in the highlands still use a *wenchif* (ወንጭፍ, a sling) to throw stones and scare birds away from teff fields. Our catapults use the same physics, but we only throw soft things.

## Watch (10 minutes)

Your guide has a catapult on the floor and a basin somewhere down the range. The guide measures the distance to the basin, checks a graph, pulls the arm back to one mark and fires.

Then the guide fires three more times:

- pulled back only a little,
- pulled back as far as it goes,
- tilted up steeply on a block.

Look carefully at:

- the shape of the bean bag's path through the air,
- where it is going fastest and slowest,
- how long it stays in the air (count "one-and-two-and…"),
- whether it lands in the same spot every time.

## Predict (15 minutes)

On a page titled *The mystery*, write:

1. **What makes the bean bag go further?** List as many things as you can think of.
2. **Draw the path** of the bean bag from the side. Mark where it was fastest and slowest.
3. **At the very top of its flight, is the bean bag moving?** Yes or no, and why?
4. **Which launch angle throws furthest?** Each person writes a number in degrees.
5. **How did the guide know which mark to pull back to?**

## Questions (10 minutes)

As a team, list every question you have. For example:

- Why does a paper ball go less far than a bean bag?
- Does a heavier bean bag go further or less far?
- Why does it curve instead of going in a straight line?
- How could we measure how fast it was going?

## The first clue

Here is a clue to take home: **once the bean bag leaves the cup, nothing pushes it forward any more**. So why does it keep going? Read *Forces and motion* tonight, especially Newton's first law. Next time, you build your own catapult.
