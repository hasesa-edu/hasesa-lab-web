## The rules for this module

Copy these into the front of your notebook:

1. Food from our tests is **never** eaten or drunk, not even a taste.
2. Wash your hands with soap after touching test food, wet sand or wet cloths.
3. Carry clay pots with two hands. One person, one pot.
4. Hold glass thermometers by the top. If one breaks, step back and tell the guide.

## Feel (10 minutes)

Your guide filled two containers with the same water yesterday and left them side by side in the room:

- a clay **ensira** (water jar), or an unglazed clay pot,
- a plastic bottle.

Touch the outside of each. Feel the clay jar: is it dry or damp? Pour a little water from each into two cups and put a finger in each. Which feels cooler?

Now look at the **pot fridge** your guide set up this morning: a clay pot inside a bigger clay pot, with wet sand between and a wet cloth on top. Touch the outer pot.

## Watch (10 minutes)

Your guide reads the thermometer that has been inside the inner pot since the morning, then a second thermometer that has been hanging in the room, in the shade, nearby. Write down both readings.

Then the guide wraps a wet cotton cloth around one of two identical bottles of water and puts both by an open window. You will check them at the end of the session.

## Predict (15 minutes)

On a page titled *The mystery*, write:

1. **Why is the inside of the pot colder than the room?** Write your best guess, then a second one.
2. **Where does the water in the sand go?** It disappears over a day or two. Guess how.
3. **Would this pot work better in Addis Ababa, or in Semera in Afar (much hotter and drier)?** Why?
4. **Guess:** how many days longer will a tomato stay good inside the pot than on a table in the room? Write a number. You'll find out in step 6.

## Questions (5 minutes)

As a team, list every question you have. For example:

- Does the pot have to be made of clay? Would a plastic bucket work?
- Why does the sand have to be wet?
- Does it work in the rainy season?
- Does colder food really go bad more slowly? Why?

## The first clue (5 minutes)

Go back to the two bottles by the window. Feel them. Which is colder?

Here's a clue to take home: **heat always flows from hotter to colder**. Read *Heat transfer* tonight. Next time you'll find out what the water is doing to make things cold.
