## Plan the log (10 minutes)

Your zeer has been standing since the last session, and the sand is wet. Today you'll find out how well it works across a whole day, from the cool morning to the warm afternoon.

Make a rota so someone from your team reads the thermometers **every 30 minutes** from the start of the school day to the end (in break times and between lessons is fine; your guide will agree the times with your teachers). At each reading, record:

| Time | Outside (°C) | Inside (°C) | Difference (°C) | Wet bulb (°C) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 08:00 | | | | |
| 08:30 | | | | |
| … | | | | |

Read the **inside** thermometer without lifting the lid for long: lift one edge, read, and cover it again. For the wet bulb, fan your wet-and-dry thermometers for a minute next to the zeer.

## Do: log all day

Follow your rota. Don't skip a reading; if one is missed, leave the box empty rather than guessing. Note anything that might matter: sun reaching the pot, a breeze, rain, someone moving it.

## Graph (30 minutes)

Read the section on line graphs in **Graphs and relationships**.

1. Draw **one** graph with time on the x-axis and temperature on the y-axis. Plot the outside readings and the inside readings as two lines, in two colours, with a key. Add the wet-bulb readings as a third line if you have room.
2. Mark the time when the outside was hottest. Was the inside hottest at the same time?
3. Work out the **biggest difference** between outside and inside, and the **mean difference** over the day.

## Do it better: how good is your zeer? (20 minutes)

The wet-bulb temperature is the coldest that evaporation could possibly make anything in that air. No real zeer gets all the way there, because heat leaks in. So a fair way to score your zeer is:

$$\text{score} = \frac{\text{outside} - \text{inside}}{\text{outside} - \text{wet bulb}}$$

Work it out at the hottest time of the day. A score of 1 would be perfect; 0 means no cooling at all.

Now use the table *Ethiopia: same pot, different places* in **Evaporation and humidity**. Suppose your zeer gets the same score everywhere. Predict the inside temperature on a dry afternoon in:

- Semera, Afar (outside 38 °C, wet bulb about 24 °C),
- Dire Dawa (outside 33 °C, wet bulb about 21 °C),
- Addis Ababa in kiremt (outside 17 °C, wet bulb about 15 °C).

Where does the zeer cool the most? Where is that cooling most needed?

## Record

Your table, your graph, the biggest and mean differences, your score with working, and your three predictions. Finish with two sentences: *"Our zeer works best when… It works badly when…"*
