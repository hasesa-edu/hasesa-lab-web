## Show it working (10 minutes)

Your guide reads the inside and outside thermometers of every team's zeer, in front of the class. Each team says the difference and its zeer's score from step 5. Which zeer is coldest? What is different about it: its pots, its sand, its place?

## Back to the mystery (10 minutes)

Open the page titled *The mystery* from step 1. Next to each guess, write ✓ or ✗ and what you now know. How close was your guess about the tomato?

Answer the first question properly, in your own words: **why is the inside of the pot colder than the room?** Use these words: *evaporation, fastest particles, energy, heat flows, wet sand, clay, humidity*.

## The recommendation (15 minutes)

An organisation wants to give clay pot fridges to families who have no electricity. They can only afford to start in one area. Write a one-page recommendation for them:

1. **Where and when.** Which area and season would you choose first: Addis Ababa, Dire Dawa, or the Afar lowlands around Semera? Which season would be worst?
2. **Your evidence.** Use at least three numbers from your own work: your zeer's temperature difference, its score, your keeping-test results, and your predictions from step 5.
3. **How to use it.** Three rules for a family: where to stand it, how often to wet the sand, and what to keep in it.
4. **A warning.** What a zeer can't do. (Can it make spoiled food safe? Does it kill microbes? Is it as cold as an electric fridge?)

## Explain it to the guide (10 minutes)

Your guide will ask each of you about your zeer and your results. Be ready to:

- explain why evaporation cools, and where the heat in the pot goes,
- show your fair test and say which factor made the biggest difference,
- show your day-long graph and point to the biggest difference,
- show your keeping-test results and explain them with microbes and temperature,
- say what you'd change to make your zeer colder, and what fair test would check it.

Take your zeer home if you like. Empty the sand first; it's much lighter dry.
