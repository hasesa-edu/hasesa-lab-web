## Check your pots (10 minutes)

You need two clay pots: a big one (outside) and a smaller one (inside) that leaves a gap of 3–5 cm all round and sits with its rim a little below the big pot's rim.

1. **The outer pot must be unglazed.** Glaze is the shiny, glassy coat on some pots. Water can't get through it, so nothing evaporates. Test: drip a little water on the outside. On good unglazed clay it soaks in and leaves a dark patch.
2. Look for cracks. Tap the pot gently with a knuckle: a clear ring is good, a dull clunk may mean a crack.
3. If the outer pot has a drainage hole, plug it with a cork or a lump of wet clay.

Soak both pots in water for 10 minutes (or since the morning, if your guide did it). Dry clay soaks up water from the sand before it starts to sweat.

## Build (35 minutes)

Do this **where the pot will stand**: once it's full of wet sand it is heavy.

1. Rinse your sand in a bucket and pour off the muddy water.
2. Put about 3 cm of wet sand in the bottom of the big pot.
3. Stand the small pot on it, in the middle. Check the gap is even all round.
4. Fill the gap with sand, pressing it down gently with a stick, up to about 2 cm below the rim of the small pot.
5. Pour water slowly into the sand until it is soaked but no water is standing on top. **Measure** how much water you pour in with the jug and record it.
6. Soak the cotton cloth, wring it out, and lay it over the top as a lid, so it touches the wet sand all round.
7. Weigh the whole zeer if your scale can take it, or record the water you added: this helps you work out later how much water it uses.

## Place it (15 minutes)

Use your results from step 3. As a team, decide where your zeer will stand for the next week, and write down **three reasons**. Think about:

- sun or shade,
- moving air or still air,
- a dry place or a damp one (near the kitchen? the washing area?),
- off the ground, on a stand or bricks, so air reaches the bottom too.

Then put a thermometer inside, on the bottom of the inner pot, and another hanging in the shade next to the zeer, at the same height, not touching anything. These two will be your **inside** and **outside** thermometers.

## Theory: putting it together

Re-read *Heat transfer* and *Evaporation and humidity* and label a drawing of your zeer with arrows showing:

- where water moves (sand → clay → outside surface → air),
- where evaporation happens,
- which way heat flows (from the food, through the inner pot, into the cold wet sand),
- where heat leaks in from the warm room, and what slows that down.

## Record

Your labelled drawing, the sizes of your pots, the water you added, where the zeer stands and your three reasons. Keep the sand wet: check it every day and add water when the top feels only damp.
