## The question

Faster evaporation means faster cooling. If you know what speeds evaporation up, you know how to make your pot fridge colder, and where to put it. Each team tests **one** factor, then shares results with the class:

- **Wind:** does moving air speed up evaporation?
- **Surface area:** does spreading the water out speed it up?
- **Humidity:** does damp air slow it down?

## Theory: fair tests

Read **Fair tests and averages**. You'll learn:

- the independent, dependent and controlled variables,
- why you repeat each setting at least three times,
- how to work out a mean and a range.

## Plan (15 minutes)

You'll measure evaporation by **mass**: weigh a wet cloth, wait 30 minutes, weigh it again. The mass lost is the water that evaporated.

Write a fair-test plan in five lines:

1. **Question:** Does ___ affect how much water evaporates from a wet cloth in 30 minutes?
2. **I will change:**
   - *Wind team:* three cloths fanned with card (take turns, steady fanning) and three left still.
   - *Surface team:* three cloths spread flat on plates and three folded into a tight square on plates.
   - *Humidity team:* three cloths each sealed inside a clear plastic bag with air in it, and three hanging in the open beside them.
3. **I will measure:** the mass lost in 30 minutes, in grams.
4. **I will keep the same:** ___ (list at least four things: think about cloth size, starting water, place, time…).
5. **Repeats:** three cloths for each setting.

**Tip:** to give every cloth the same water, weigh it dry, soak it, squeeze it, and add or squeeze out water until it has gained the same mass as the others (for example 40 g).

Show your plan to the guide before you start.

## Do (35 minutes)

Weigh each cloth on its plate. (Humidity team: weigh each cloth on a plate, then slide cloth and plate into the bag and seal it. At the end, take them out and weigh them straight away. Why can't you weigh the sealed bag instead? Think about where the evaporated water goes.) Start them all at the same moment. Weigh again after exactly 30 minutes.

While you wait, measure the **humidity** of the room with your wet-and-dry thermometers from step 2. Fan them for a minute, read both, and look up the relative humidity in the table in *Evaporation and humidity*. Record it, and record the room temperature: these are controlled variables you can't control, so write them down.

## Record (25 minutes)

1. Make a results table:

| Setting | Cloth 1 (g lost) | Cloth 2 | Cloth 3 | Mean | Range |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| e.g. fanned | | | | | |
| e.g. still | | | | | |

2. How many times faster was the faster setting? (Divide the bigger mean by the smaller.)
3. Is the difference between the means bigger than the ranges? If not, can you be sure?
4. Share with the other two teams. Copy their results into your notebook.

Then answer, using all three tests: **Where should a pot fridge stand: in the sun or the shade, in a breeze or a closed corner, in a dry room or a steamy kitchen? Should the outer pot be big or small?** Give a reason for each, from the data where you can, and from *Heat transfer* for the sun (you didn't test that one).
