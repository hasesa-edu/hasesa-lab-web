In hot, dry places, food spoils in a day or two and many homes have no fridge. In eight hours you'll build a fridge that needs no electricity, find out exactly how it pulls heat out of food, and test whether it really keeps tomatoes, greens and milk fresh for longer.

1. **The pot that sweats** (45 min). Feel the cooling of a clay ensira and a wet cloth, and make predictions.
2. **Evaporation takes heat** (75 min). Wet and dry thermometers, wet bottles and the physics of escaping particles.
3. **What speeds it up?** (90 min). Fair tests of wind, surface area and humidity, measured with a scale and thermometers.
4. **Build the zeer** (75 min). Two pots, wet sand, a wet lid, and the right place to put it.
5. **A day in the pot** (75 min). Log inside and outside temperatures over a day and graph them.
6. **The keeping test** (75 min, spread over a week). Tomatoes, gomen and milk: pot against room, and what microbes have to do with it.
7. **Pot report** (45 min). Show your results and recommend where in Ethiopia a zeer is most worth having.

By the end you'll understand evaporation and latent heat, humidity, how heat moves, how temperature controls the growth of microbes, and how to prove a claim with a fair test and a graph.
