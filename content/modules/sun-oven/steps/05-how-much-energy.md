## The question

Your oven gets hot. But how much energy is it really collecting, and how much of the sunlight is it wasting?

## Do: a heating run (40 minutes)

1. Weigh exactly 0.5 kg of water into the pot. Measure its starting temperature.
2. Put it in the oven, aimed at the sun, at the start of the session.
3. Record the temperature every 5 minutes for 40 minutes. Re-aim after 20 minutes.
4. Measure the **area** of sunlight your oven catches: the window's area plus the reflector's area, as seen from the sun. (Width × height, in metres.)

## Theory: energy and power

Read **Energy and power**. You'll learn:

- how to calculate the energy needed to heat water, $Q = mc\Delta T$,
- what a watt is, and $P = E/t$,
- that clear-sky sunlight delivers about 1000 W per square metre,
- how to calculate efficiency.

## Calculate (25 minutes)

1. Draw your temperature–time graph and a best-fit line. Choose a straight part of it.
2. From two points on that part of the line, find $\Delta T$ and the time $t$ in seconds.
3. Energy into the water: $Q = 0.5 \times 4200 \times \Delta T$.
4. Useful power: $P_{\text{out}} = Q \div t$.
5. Sunlight power in: $P_{\text{in}} = 1000 \times A$.
6. Efficiency: $P_{\text{out}} \div P_{\text{in}} \times 100\%$.

## Notice something puzzling

The line on your graph probably gets **less steep** as the water gets hotter. The sunlight hasn't changed. Why does the oven heat the water more slowly when it's already hot? (Hint: think about the cooling race in step 3.)

## Record

All six calculations with units on every line. Compare your efficiency with the other teams. Which oven is most efficient, and what's different about it?
