## Choose one change (10 minutes)

Pick **one** thing that you think will make your oven hotter or faster. For example:

- adding side reflectors (four flaps instead of one),
- a double-layer window instead of a single one,
- more insulation, or a different insulation material,
- painting the outside of the pot black if it isn't already,
- changing the reflector angle.

Read **Fair tests and averages** and write a five-line fair-test plan. What will you keep the same? (Think: amount of water, starting temperature, time of day, the pot, where the oven sits.)

## The problem with the sun

The sun gets stronger towards noon and weaker after. If you test the old design at 10:00 and the new one at 12:00, the new one will look better even if your change did nothing.

How can you make this a fair test? Two possible ways:

- **Twin ovens:** borrow another team's oven of the same design, make your change to one of them only, and run them **side by side at the same time**.
- **Back-to-back runs:** test old, new, old, new, each for 15 minutes, starting from the same water temperature, and compare the heating rates.

## Do (40 minutes)

Run your test. Record temperatures every 5 minutes for both versions.

## Record (10 minutes)

A results table and a graph with both lines. Calculate both heating rates. Did your change help? Is the difference bigger than the difference you'd expect from chance (for example, between two identical ovens)? Keep the change if it helped.
