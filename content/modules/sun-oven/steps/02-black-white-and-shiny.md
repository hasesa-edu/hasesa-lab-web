## Do: the can race (35 minutes)

Each team has three identical drink cans:

- one painted or wrapped in **matt black** paper,
- one in **white** paper,
- one wrapped in **shiny foil**.

1. Put exactly 200 mL of water from the same jug into each can.
2. Measure the starting temperature of each. They should be the same.
3. Stand them side by side in full sun, all facing the same way.
4. Measure the temperature of each every 5 minutes for 30 minutes. Stir gently before each reading.

Keep a fourth can in the **shade** as a control.

## Notice something puzzling

All three cans are in the same sunlight. Why don't they warm up equally? And why does the black one get hotter even though black and white paper are the same thickness?

## Theory: heat transfer

Read **Heat transfer**. You'll learn:

- conduction, and why metals feel cold and still air insulates,
- convection, and why warm air rises,
- radiation, and why black surfaces absorb it and shiny ones reflect it,
- how a solar oven uses all three.

## Theory: graphs

Read **Graphs and relationships**, especially the parts about the line of best fit and the **gradient**.

## Record (25 minutes)

1. Draw all four cans' results on one graph: temperature (y-axis) against time (x-axis), with a key.
2. Draw a best-fit line for each.
3. Calculate the gradient (heating rate in °C per minute) of the black and the foil can.
4. Write one sentence for each can explaining its result, using *absorb* or *reflect*.

Which colour should the inside of your oven be? Which colour should the reflector be?
