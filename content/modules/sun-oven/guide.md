# Sun Oven: guide's notes

For the guide only. About 8 hours, best as five sessions of 1.5–2 hours, all in the **late morning** (about 10:00–13:00), when the sun is highest. Up to 16 students in teams of four. In Addis, the clearest months are roughly October to February; the long rains (June–September) make the outdoor steps unreliable, so plan the module for the dry season, and keep a cloudy-day plan (theory reading, building, the indoor cooling race) ready.

---

## Before the module

### 1. Build your demo oven (a week ahead)

Build one oven exactly as in step 4 and test it. It should take 0.5 kg of water above 80 °C in about 1.5–2 hours of clear sun around midday. Note your times and temperatures: these are your reference.

**For step 1:** start the demo oven at least two hours before the session with an egg in about 2 cm of water in the black pot. An egg is hard-boiled after about 15–20 minutes above 80 °C. Test the timing on a practice day.

### 2. Shopping

| Item | Notes |
|---|---|
| Cardboard boxes | Two per team, plus spares. The inner box about 40 × 30 cm; the outer box 5–8 cm bigger all round. Same-shape boxes make building easier. |
| Foil | One roll per team. |
| Window | Clear oven bags are cheap and heat-proof. Ordinary thin plastic works but may sag. If you use glass, tape all edges with thick tape before students touch it. |
| Pots | Small dark metal pots with lids, or large tins painted matt black outside (let the paint dry and air for a day; don't paint the inside). |
| Thermometers | Probe thermometers that read above 100 °C. Glass thermometers break easily in the field. |
| Cans | 4 per team: black, white, foil, and one for the shade. |

---

## Safety briefing (step 1, again at steps 4 and 7)

- Gloves or cloths for the pot and lid. Open lids away from faces.
- **Never look at the sun or its reflection.** Aim by the shadows.
- You handle the craft knife and any glass.
- Short spells in the sun, hats, water, shade between readings. Watch for students who look unwell.
- Food: only eat what is cooked hot all through, straight away.

## Session plans

### Step 1: The egg in the box (45 min)

Do the "open pot in the sun" test as the step says, and read the thermometer at the end. Expect perhaps 35–45 °C: warm, nowhere near cooking. That contrast is the point.

### Step 2: Black, white and shiny (60 min)

**Expected results** after 30 minutes from about 20 °C: black 35–45 °C, white 25–30 °C, foil 22–27 °C, shade about 20–22 °C. Bare aluminium cans warm faster than foil-wrapped ones because the printed paint absorbs. Make sure every can is fully covered.

### Step 3: Trap the heat (75 min)

Use the kettle to fill all cups at once (or as fast as possible), then let students take the first reading together. **Expected:** A loses the most (perhaps 20–25 °C in 24 minutes); B (lid only) and C (paper only) are in between, often with the lid doing at least as well as the paper; E loses the least (perhaps 8–12 °C). The lid result surprises students: most heat leaves an open cup through the top, by convection and evaporation.

**Greenhouse:** the bagged can is usually a few degrees warmer after 15 minutes.

### Step 4: Build the oven (120 min)

Can be split across two sessions. Common mistakes:

- Insulation packed so tight there's no air in it.
- Gaps around the window: warm air leaks out. Tape all edges.
- Wrinkled foil. Glue it flat.
- The reflector at the wrong angle. It should bounce light onto the window, which usually means tilting it about 100–120° from the window, depending on the sun's height.

### Step 5: How much energy? (75 min)

**Example result:** 0.5 kg of water rises from 22 °C to 46 °C in 20 minutes (1200 s). Q = 0.5 × 4200 × 24 = 50,400 J. P_out = 42 W. With an opening of 0.20 m², P_in ≈ 200 W, so efficiency ≈ 21%. Values of 15–40% are reasonable.

Students should notice the curve flattening. The hotter the pot, the faster heat leaks out (the temperature difference with the outside is bigger), so less of the incoming power goes into raising the temperature. At the top temperature, all the incoming power leaks out again.

### Step 6: Make it better (60 min)

Side-by-side twin ovens is the cleaner method; help teams pair up. Side reflectors and double windows usually help most. Encourage teams to report a "no difference" result honestly.

### Step 7: Cook! (60 min)

Start the cooking as early as possible on the day. Rice needs about 2 hours; small potatoes 2–3 hours; eggs about 1–1.5 hours from cold. You check every food with gloves before anyone eats it.

## Judging the sign-off items

- **hot:** use your demo oven on the same day as a reference. If even yours can't reach 80 °C because of haze, accept 70 °C and note it.
- **three-ways:** reflector and black pot (radiation), window and sealed lid (convection), insulation and box-in-box (conduction).
- **temperature-graph:** labelled axes with units, best-fit line, gradient with units (°C/min).
- **efficiency:** all steps with units; a plausible result (5–50%).
- **improvement:** one variable changed, a fair comparison (same time or alternating), an honest conclusion.
- **cooked:** safely, with gloves.
