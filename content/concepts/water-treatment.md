No single step makes dirty water safe. Water engineers use several **barriers**, one after the other. Each one removes or kills some of what the last one missed.

## Barrier 1: settle (and coagulate)

Leave muddy water to stand, and heavy sand and silt sink to the bottom. Fine clay particles may stay floating for days, because they all carry the same **negative electric charge** and push each other apart.

A **coagulant** neutralises that charge, so tiny particles stick together into bigger clumps (**flocs**) that sink in an hour or two:

- **Alum** (aluminium sulfate), used by many water works.
- **Moringa seeds.** Crushed *Moringa* seed kernels contain positively charged proteins that do the same job. *Moringa stenopetala* (shiferaw or haleko) grows in southern Ethiopia.

Settling and coagulation remove much of the mud, and with it many germs, but not all.

## Barrier 2: filter

Pour the settled water through layers of gravel and sand. Particles bigger than the gaps get stuck. Over weeks, a slow sand filter grows a slimy biological layer on top that traps and eats many germs. A cloth folded several times removes larger particles and some germs stuck to them.

A quick home-made filter makes water **clearer**. It does **not** reliably remove bacteria and viruses, which are smaller than the gaps between the sand grains.

## Barrier 3: disinfect

Disinfection kills germs that are still in the water.

| Method | How | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Boiling** | A rolling boil for 1 minute, or **3 minutes above 2,000 m** | Very reliable. Uses fuel; doesn't stop the water being re-contaminated later. |
| **Chlorine** | Add the dose on the bottle, stir, wait 30 minutes | Leaves a little chlorine in the water that keeps protecting it. Less effective in cloudy water. |
| **SODIS** (solar disinfection) | Fill clear plastic (PET) bottles of 2 L or less with **clear** water, lay them in full sun for 6 hours (two days if cloudy) | UV-A light damages the germs' DNA; warming above 50 °C helps. Free, but slow, and only for clear water. |

## Barrier 4: store safely

Treated water can be contaminated again by a dirty cup or hand. Store it in a clean container with a lid and a tap or narrow neck, so nobody dips into it.

## Concentration and dosing chlorine

Chlorine doses are measured in **milligrams per litre** (mg/L). A typical target is about 2 mg/L, leaving a small amount after 30 minutes.

$$\text{mass needed} = \text{dose (mg/L)} \times \text{volume of water (L)}$$

$$\text{volume of product} = \frac{\text{mass needed (mg)}}{\text{concentration of product (mg/mL)}}$$

**Example.** Household bleach at 5% contains about 50 mg of chlorine per mL. For a 20 L jerrycan at 2 mg/L: mass needed = 40 mg, volume = 40 ÷ 50 = 0.8 mL (about 16 drops). Always follow the instructions on the product: strengths differ.
