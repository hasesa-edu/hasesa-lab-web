A seed is a baby plant in a lunchbox: a tiny **embryo** packed with a food store, wrapped in a tough **seed coat**. It can wait for months or years until the conditions are right.

## Inside a seed

Soak a bean or chickpea overnight, peel off the coat and gently split it open:

- **Seed coat**: the protective skin.
- **Cotyledons**: the two fat halves. They are the food store, full of starch and protein.
- **Embryo**: tucked between them, with a tiny **root** (radicle) and **shoot** (plumule).

Grasses like teff, wheat and maize have one cotyledon instead of two, and a separate food store called the endosperm, which is what we grind into flour.

## Germination

A seed **germinates** when it gets:

1. **Water**: it swells and splits the coat, and its enzymes start working.
2. **Warmth**: enzymes work faster when warm (but not too hot).
3. **Oxygen**: the embryo respires, using its stored food for energy.

It does **not** need light or soil to start. The root grows down first, then the shoot grows up towards the light.

## Photosynthesis: making food from light

Once the first green leaves open, the plant makes its own food. In the **chloroplasts**, green **chlorophyll** captures light energy and uses it to turn carbon dioxide and water into sugar:

$$\text{carbon dioxide} + \text{water} \xrightarrow{\text{light, chlorophyll}} \text{glucose} + \text{oxygen}$$

$$6\,\text{CO}_2 + 6\,\text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 + 6\,\text{O}_2$$

The plant uses the glucose for energy, builds it into cellulose for new cells, or stores it as **starch**. You can test a leaf for starch with iodine: blue-black means it has been photosynthesising.

## What limits growth

A plant grows only as fast as its scarcest need allows. The main **limiting factors** are:

- **light** (photosynthesis stops in the dark),
- **water** (it wilts and closes its leaf pores),
- **carbon dioxide** (rarely limiting outdoors),
- **temperature** (too cold or too hot slows enzymes),
- **minerals** from the soil, such as nitrogen for green leaves.

## Measuring growth

- **Height**: from the soil surface to the tip of the shoot, in cm, with a ruler held straight.
- **Germination percentage**: $\dfrac{\text{seeds that germinated}}{\text{seeds planted}} \times 100$.
- **Growth rate**: change in height ÷ change in time, in cm per day.
- **Mass**: plants can be dried and weighed at the end (dry mass is more reliable than fresh mass, which changes with water).

Plotting height against time usually gives an **S-shaped curve**: slow at first, then fast, then levelling off.
